# Where operations live in Gojo

Design review and first EncodingC implementation · 25 November 2026 · Master baseline `9b805964`

## Position

An operation lives in the **connected structure that makes its roles and law readable**. A connection can simply carry a reference or a conserved packet. Put the operation where its inputs, dependencies, and authority to change state can be inspected. A Boolean relation and a gated passage already fit three roles in one row. A separate local structure becomes useful when the operation needs its own state, settlement or reusable boundary.

Keep **relation, process, and fold** available as distinct reads of the operation:

| Read | What must be connected | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Relation** | Bound or open roles, their basis and scope, and the conditions that admit a tuple | A product relation admits $(3,4,12)$. With a result of 12 and two open inputs, it can retain alternative factor pairs. |
| **Process** | A before-state, the routed transition or construction, affected receivers, and the schedule when it matters | A rate of three units per tick can move stock from a supply into a receiver. Each eligible firing accounts for what moves. |
| **Fold** | The selected output basis and what it preserves, including any required residue or provenance | Read the count 12, retain the row and column factors, or crystallize a new carrier with a stable tick promise. |

Pinning means making the participants, their roles, and any shared site or calibration explicit. It can precede all three reads. A result needs a readable output role. That role can stay open, be read live, be materialized independently, or be bound to a receiver. An admitted tuple supplies evidence for a candidate action. An explicit route and receiving structure determine what changes. This fits the existing separation between relation and transition rows in EncodingC, including the use of a transition table as a relation for asking what a later state would admit. [EncodingC laws and wiring](../../Gojo.EncodingC/README.md#nouns) · [Transition as relation](../../Gojo.EncodingC/Laws/TransitionLaw.cs#L91) · [Canonical prompt, §§4, 6–8](GojoSystemPrompt.md)

I would settle this operational grammar using small exact quantities alongside qualitative laws. The new tension system already runs bounded count and comparison laws, so a first operation can expose a computed result, demand, and accountable response without waiting for a general numeral system. Order, occupancy and landmarks carry useful laws before measurement. A claim about length scale, distinct pair count, packet rate or capacity adds a numeric or calibrated witness. Comparing these witnesses tells us what a future rung needs to preserve.

All three operations notes favor exposing output roles, separating read from enactment, and letting native compilation serve the structural contract. The first implemented EncodingC slice follows the bounded comparison below. Its three circuits use a shared arithmetic example with independently bound roles. The relation, the pair construction, and the delivery each expose their own result and evidence. ## What changes when we say add, multiply or Boolean?

### Addition has a receiving basis or a join

Before numbers, two traversals can be joined at a shared or explicitly corresponding site. Two arrivals can also occupy one receiving scope together, retaining their sources. These are already discernible acts. A scalar sum is a further compatible read. For example, appending a two-tick traversal to a three-tick traversal constructs an intermediate site and a route. Depositing two and three units into a common stock raises the accounted amount to five if both are admitted. Two observers can start at the same site and fold their reports to five while leaving the stock available.

A pointwise sum needs a correspondence of positions and compatible contributions. The quantity role matters alongside the unit. Mixing two samples of water combines their energy and amount under a heat-capacity model, then yields a temperature read. Combining two compatible temperature changes adds changes in the same basis. Different dimensions or different tick scales can keep their separate participation until a common calibrated read exists. Adding positions is generally origin-dependent. Translating a position by a displacement, composing two displacements, adding two lengths, and joining two paths all give different promises about the output. For cancellation, retain source identity and orientation. Equal-looking but separately observed samples provide weaker evidence than the same sample used in two opposite roles.

### Multiplication has more than one physical construction

| Construction | What lives in the structure | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Repeat a contribution** | One contribution or reusable pattern, an ordered set of occasions or placements, a repeat controller, and any cumulative read. Distinct material copies require supply and identity. | Four placements of three apples give twelve apples if all twelve are supplied. Four reads of one stock of three are four observations of three apples. |
| **Form a product** | Two factor roles, their independent or deliberately repeated participation, and a resulting product basis. An occupied product can pair admitted positions from those factors. A later measure can fold the pairs without storing every cell when the exposed factorization suffices. | A rectangle made from `3 m` and `4 m` has `12 m²`. An unbound edge leaves a partially assembled area with one known factor. Equal-length edges retain their separate identities. |
| **Apply a gain or ratio** | A controller or calibrated ratio, the target's exposed displacement or contribution, an origin or pivot when relevant, and the transformation's output correspondence. The target's dimension is preserved for a dimensionless gain. | `(3 m / 1 m) × 4 m = 12 m`. Changing the reference to `2 m` changes the output to `6 m`. |

These constructions can agree on $\mathbb{N}$ when the only question is a count. They can disagree about units, identity, material effects, and which value acts as a controller. A product between predicates on the **same** partition selects common admissibility per cell. Pairing predicates from **independent** axes instead selects a set of tuples. In the latter case counting all independent pairs can yield the product of counts. On a shared axis the overlap can be anything from zero to the smaller count. Shared position or sample identity must be carried into the relation, especially when a law reads one role twice.

I would take the rectangle intuition literally when we have two axes and an area question, and the stretch intuition literally when the selected structure exposes a displacement and a gain. They share mechanics such as ordered roles, alignment, projection, and counting. The differences deserve structural evidence, even when the final code can evaluate both by the same integer multiplication.

For a pair of two-sided factors, keeping the four pairwise contributions is useful independently of any numerical display. A current kernel conformance example retains the source pair for each term and reads through those sources when it folds the result. An expanded term's face can record involvement of an opposed operand. Selecting a final scalar sign or complex coefficient requires retaining the role of both operands and the chosen fold. [Expanded product conformance](../../Tests/Gojo.Tests/Core/OperationConformanceTests.cs#L105)

### Boolean is a read over a scope, and a gate is an action

Take a scope from 0 to 12, with occupied spans from 1 to 7 and 5 to 10. Partition at their endpoints. The interiors from 1 to 5 and 7 to 10 have exactly one source. The interval from 5 to 7 has both. The rest of the supplied scope is uncovered. Read the boundary sites separately according to the declared endpoint inclusion. Geometric overlap persists if both spans run in the same direction. The current span reader's **opposed** overlap measures cancellation opportunity and can be zero in that very case. Conversely zero net participation can retain two opposing contributions. [Span participation reads](../Core/Relation.Reads.cs) · [Span partitioning](../Reads/RelationReads.cs)

At the one-bit rung, AND reads co-presence and is the carry of the one-bit sum. OR is the clamped sum and XOR the wrapped sum. This interpretation uses the selected one-bit basis. On aligned unary count planes, AND gives the minimum and OR the maximum. XOR selects the span between the counts, which a set reader can use even when it is an invalid unary count. For three or more contributors, select all, at least two, exactly one, or parity according to the question. [EncodingC Boolean and count law tests](../../Gojo.EncodingC/Tests/LawTests.cs#L183) · [Claude’s comparison of representations](https://github.com/It3rate/Gojo/blob/e0f06b05/ops_claude/GojoOperations-2026-11.md#L417)

For an exhaustive, closed membership question we can call the uncovered part a complement. An open boundary, an unbound contribution, or a blocked lane leaves a different witness. EncodingC's presence polarity also belongs to a particular plane. A zero bit can say a tuple is inadmissible, a site is unoccupied, or a sign is the recessive member, depending on what that plane shows. A present ODV sample below detection, an unselected site, a known empty region and a contradictory support each carry different evidence.

A Boolean relation may answer which cases are admissible. Driving flow through a selected case requires a wired transition, a receiver, and a rule for accepted and blocked units. A shared gate can be read many times, while one conserved unit can go to one receiving route. This is where the low-level connection can physically realize logic through its topology. ## A larger structure as the target

Take a plate with a rectangular outline, a hole, mounting datum, and an interface to a surrounding assembly. Five requests give different outcomes.

| Request | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Publish the plate through a scaled frame | Dependent reads use a different calibrated basis. The same plate and its source state remain available. A single shared frame edit can be cheap when every relevant read already factors through that frame. |
| Repeat the plate three times | Three instances with explicit placements and correspondences. Each instance has its own identity. The same design can be shared by reference. |
| Stretch it threefold along one exposed axis | Displacements along that axis are transformed. Area scales by 3 in a calibrated plane. A circular hole generally reads as an ellipse if its geometry participates. A demanded circular passage can stay a connected mismatch. |
| Dilate the plate uniformly about the datum | Selected points follow $p'=o+3(p-o)$. In-plane lengths scale by 3 and areas by 9. Thickness and mass have their own participation. With constant thickness and density, mass scales by 9, or by 27 if the thickness scales too. |
| Form a product with a separate thickness extent | The plate's occupied area and the thickness can supply a volume basis. A hole's exclusion needs correspondence into that product when it is meant to be a through-hole. |

The target is a **focus and an exposed promise**, for example “the metric outline and hole about this datum.” The focus may discover joined endpoints or dependencies on use. Keep genuine shared sites shared in the output, keep independent equal-coordinate sites distinguishable, and carry parts outside the focus with their context. If a proposed plate movement conflicts with a fixed mounting site, the application first exposes the mismatch. It enters material tension only where the target has a wired enactment or a physical response that receives the change. The boundary must expose which choices are admissible. The application can include the linked site, preserve the interface if the transform permits it, or retain an open result with the mismatch. Similarity by a positive gain can preserve enough structure for a proxy to answer coarse questions. An exact reified patch may need more evidence. Which result is allowed to commit is a separate question. Uniform negative gain in a plane is a half-turn plus stretch, while reversing just one axis reflects. Zero gain collapses the selected geometric extent and leaves the inverse open.

In a workspace, a candidate sees the source by reference and holds changes where it needs them. Scope can start at the head exposed by the ports and refine to local uses. The candidate can be compared or dropped. Writeback needs the application's checked correspondence and appropriate settlement, including a stale-source check and a coherent group where changes depend on each other. A workspace gap is evidence for a supposition. Provenance for a candidate hole, attachment or receiving surface points to where it was introduced. The observations of the original plate stay independently readable. EncodingC implements narrow workspace commit and fork semantics. A workspace’s `Gap` is live bit disagreement. Read a concrete discrepancy in both directions with two `Counts.Shortfall` laws when its magnitude matters. A fork with isolated ports can take from snapshots and cannot commit to the live stores. The general transform/geometry and kernel bridge remain open. ## Does A × B change B?

Give the relation concrete input and output roles. Whether $B$ is changed follows from what the output is bound to and how its execution is timed. With gain 2 and initial value 5, the table compares how each use treats the would-be product 10.

| Use | Connected evidence after the first result |
| --- | --- |
| Read $A$ and $B$ | The result 10 can be read through its output. The provided inputs remain available for further reads. |
| Publish or crystallize | Publish a live output held against a separately exposed baseline, or capture an independent output with a stable tick promise. A live read tracks later input. A captured value has its own state and provenance. |
| Apply once to $B$ | Bind a receiver to $B$ and sample $B_0=5$ at the application's before boundary. Request 10 and let the explicit value or structural port settle or decline the change. Record requested and accepted change, including a partial or refused application. |
| Feed back to $B$ | Connect each committed after-state to the next before-state. With an exact unconstrained gain of 2, snapshots are 5, 10, 20, 40, and so on. The repeat controller, stop condition and time boundary matter. |
| Ask $B=2B$ at one instant | This is a constraint on one shared sample. In exact scalar arithmetic its only solution is 0. With $B$ known to be 5 it is incompatible. Neither read authorizes erasing the state of $B$. |

The difference between a one-shot application and recurrence is easy to lose if each settlement pass rereads the receiver as its own operand. Settling one event and advancing time are distinct acts. Aliasing an output to its input needs a captured before-state, an independent baseline or intentionally wired feedback. Use the outcome after the receiving boundary as evidence of what happened. A request for 10 with capacity 8 leaves a claim about the shortfall after acceptance. A separate transfer witness establishes how much physical stock is waiting to enter.

The numeric kernel has separate live-output and follow paths. A claim can report the mismatch between a read and a requested value. A follow relation can use it as input to an explicit adjustment with before/after evidence. EncodingC gives a related example using a transition table as a relation to narrow a two-step goal, then a distinct enactment to set a gate for a conserving move. A backwards query is useful for “what input could account for this?” The supported answers may be several, none or one. With the exact scalar question $A B=0$, a known zero factor leaves the other factor open. A solved role provides evidence for a possible action, and the receiver still governs writes. ## What the new tension system changes

Material tension now has a bounded executable form in EncodingC. A unary value can have two separate unary counts beside it. `T+` holds units admitted as pressure that have yet to be absorbed. `T−` holds booked draws or obligations that have yet to be paid. External arrivals and demands enter those counts. If the intake field itself is full, the new unit or draw is reported blocked at entry. Wired moves absorb, draw, or explicitly cancel. A full value can leave pressure waiting and an empty one can leave debt unpaid. Cancellation needs a relation that makes the two faces accountable to one another. The room view used to pay a draw is writable capacity minus value on the same count. Reorientation retains the site and changes its orientation of reading. [Material tension implementation](../../Gojo.EncodingC/Flow/Tension.cs) · [Room view](../../Gojo.EncodingC/Substrate/Field.cs#L40-L52) · [Material tension tests](../../Gojo.EncodingC/Tests/TensionTests.cs#L57-L139)

| Evidence at an operation boundary | What can act on it |
| --- | --- |
| **A calculated shortfall or excess** | `Counts.Shortfall` reads concrete have and want, and swapping them reads the other face. The law leaves its inputs alone. A goal, demand or order route can use the result to decide whether to act. |
| **An unabsorbed unit or unpaid order** | The local material accounting keeps pressure at the site that accepted it, or an outstanding obligation at the site where it was booked. `Flows.Fulfill` can take a packet, deliver it, and close one packet order together. The optional receipt tally is another bound role with its own capacity and accounting. |
| **Open or empty support** | `Counts.Tally` can publish the count of remaining possibilities for small supports. More than one possible answer matters when a selected question requires one; an empty demanded support exposes incompatibility. `Settling.ValueGap` reads whether two open values may agree or differ. A quantitative distance between those open values is still open work. |
| **Competing eligible writes** | A same-step contest holds competing writes and exposes which rows competed. An explicit choice can narrow to one route, a justified gate can reopen when its reasons return, and a work-following turn can give two routes their opportunities. |
| **A discrepancy in a candidate workspace** | The candidate can read and act on its discrepancy within the supposition. A checked commit, or a separately wired action, is needed for effects in the source. |

The counting laws make comparisons usable by other laws. For example, a live shortfall of three can remain published across several steps. A route that books an order needs its own event, acknowledgement or already-on-order read so that the same three are accounted for once. A blocked attempt leaves units with their owner until a transfer actually accepts them. Published loss at a coarse arithmetic port remains read evidence until something uses it. These distinctions apply even when all three quantities have the same magnitude. ## A common comparison using 3 × 4

The same numeral can test three claims. Each claim exposes a different operation surface, and the observations below are enough to tell them apart.

### A bounded relation answers questions in any role

Use exact nonnegative integer roles $a,b\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}$ and $c\in\{0,\ldots,16\}$, with the result admitted where $c=ab$. A dense EncodingC relation takes $5×5×17=425$ logical table bits, 54 bytes of packed table payload before role shapes and bindings. It admits 25 tuples. This fits a small exact reference relation. It supplies a domain and admits joint alternatives without requiring an operand or output to be overwritten. [EncodingC table layout](../../Gojo.EncodingC/Laws/Law.cs) · [Role narrowing and shared references](../../Gojo.EncodingC/Laws/RelationLaw.cs#L39-L82)

| Given or bound | Expected local evidence |
| --- | --- |
| $a=3,\ b=4$ | $c=12$. |
| $a=3,\ c=12$ | $b=4$. |
| Only $c=12$ | Ordered alternatives $(3,4)$ and $(4,3)$. Both individual supports read $\{3,4\}$. The joint relation still excludes $(3,3)$ and $(4,4)$. |
| $a=0,\ c=0$ | All five values of $b$ remain possible. |
| $a=2,\ c=5$ | Empty support for $b$. The requested output lies inside the declared result domain. |
| Bind both input roles to the same sample | Only diagonal pairs remain. Their product support is $\{0,1,4,9,16\}$, so an additional request for 12 contradicts that binding. |

Reading one role backward is useful even when the answer is a set. The joint relation matters because marginals cannot generally preserve correlation. A network of local reads can also stay quiet with choices open. For example, the three constraints $x≠y$, $y≠z$, $z≠x$ on Boolean roles each admit both values locally, while no assignment satisfies all three. A unique answer or a global contradiction needs enough connected evidence to establish it.

### A pair construction accounts for how the result grew

Take two scopes with distinct occurrences. Pair each admitted occurrence from the first scope with each admitted occurrence from the second. The product can be exposed through those factors at a resolution where no individual cell is needed. If a later question needs cell identity, a pair needs a correspondence to both constituent occurrences.

For monotone arrivals, let $a,b$ be the counts **before** a step and $\Delta a,\Delta b$ its new arrivals. Its new pairs are

$$
\Delta(ab)=b\Delta a+a\Delta b+\Delta a\Delta b.
$$

The three terms pair new $A$ with old $B$, old $A$ with new $B$, and new arrivals with each other. These three histories all end with 3 arrivals of $A$ and 4 of $B$.

| Schedule | New $A$ with old $B$ | Old $A$ with new $B$ | Same-step pairs | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Finish $A$, then admit $B$ | 0 | 12 | 0 | 12 |
| Three simultaneous paired arrivals, then one $B$ | 3 | 6 | 3 | 12 |
| Serialize each paired arrival with $A$ before $B$, then admit the last $B$ | 3 | 9 | 0 | 12 |

The corner count is real at this resolution. Dropping it needs an explicitly chosen approximation or a fold that returns only the final total. The final scalar 12 cannot recover which schedule ran. Record the split if a downstream question needs it, or reconstruct it from an adequate retained starting state, event sequence and schedule. A crystallized count may deliberately forget the path and keep its selected tick promise. The same count could arise by reading an already-formed product with no arrival history offered. ## Historical constructions worth borrowing

### Old Babylonian calculation

Jens Høyrup's translations and reconstruction distinguish several actions that a modern rendering can flatten into multiplication. A table asks for a stated number of steps of an abstract number. Raising can give the measured quantity for a concrete proportion, such as scaling a standard-thickness base to a chosen height. Making two sides hold constructs a rectangle and may be followed by a separate read of its measure. Physical repetition or doubling can instead assemble a rectangle from two copies of a right triangle. Høyrup also distinguishes accumulating measuring numbers from physically joining same-kind magnitudes. That vocabulary is evidence for distinct procedures in the texts he studies. The Gojo inference is that one numeric backend can serve several constructions while their factor roles, units and state effects remain readable. [Høyrup, *Algebra in Cuneiform*, chapter 1](https://www.mprl-series.mpg.de/media/textbooks/2/2/textbooks2ch2.pdf)

The multiplication tablet catalogued as P493017 lists eight steps of a written `7:30` as a written `1`. As a modern absolute normalization, read `0:07:30` as $7/60+30/3600=1/8$ and get $1$. Read the same factor digits as $7(60)+30=450$ and the corresponding output rung is $3600$. The table supplies a relative digit pattern. The application supplies a scale. Christine Proust's analysis of Nippur school tablets likewise distinguishes metrological surfaces from the positional computations used with them. Her study of large-number multiplication reconstructs partial-product procedures from stereotyped errors. That procedure is a plausible historical reconstruction and a useful analogy for carrying across Gojo rungs. [CDLI tablet P493017](https://cdli.earth/artifacts/493017) · [Proust on Nippur](https://www.numdam.org/articles/10.24033/rhm.144/) · [Proust on large multiplications](https://www.numdam.org/item/RHM_2000__6_2_293_0/)

Reciprocals give a second lesson. In sexagesimal notation $1/4=0;15$ is finite. Multiplying 30 by it gives $7;30$, i.e. $7.5$. The reciprocal $1/7$ has no terminating sexagesimal expansion, yet $21/7=3$ settles exactly. Whether a particular quotient is exact, whether a reciprocal fits the chosen radix, and whether a table contains a row are separate questions. A missing row can motivate another resolution or procedure while retaining the original query. [CDLI reciprocal tablet P252955](https://cdli.earth/artifacts/252955) · [Høyrup, chapter 1](https://www.mprl-series.mpg.de/textbooks/2/2/index.html)

In BM 13901 problem 1, Høyrup reads the procedure as constructing a unit-width strip from the side, rearranging it around the square and filling the corner with a half-unit square. In modern dimensional bookkeeping, with side $x$ and reference width $u$, $x^2+ux=3u^2/4$ becomes $(x+u/2)^2=u^2$. The positive side comes out $u/2$. The moment worth carrying into Gojo is the attached unit-width strip. It turns a side measurement into area participation that can be joined to a square. The chosen positive geometric side supplies a domain. Both details would disappear in a bare symbolic adder. [Høyrup's translation and reconstruction, chapter 3](https://www.mprl-series.mpg.de/media/textbooks/2/4/textbooks2ch4.pdf)

A table of squares also suggests the identity $ab=((a+b)^2-(a-b)^2)/4$. It is a valid modern factorization to compare with doubling and digit partial products. Surviving Babylonian square tables make such a use plausible and leave their role in general multiplication uncertain. [McFarland, *Quarter-Squares Revisited*, pp. 1–2](https://ccpr.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/Quarter-Squares-Revisited_-Earlier-Tables_-Division-of-Labor-in-Table-Construction_-and-Later-Implementations-in-Analog-Computers.pdf#page=2)

Euclid's *Elements* VII defines numerical multiplication in ordered repeat roles and proves equality when they are exchanged in VII.16. ## The landmark oscillator

The physical picture starts with signed displacement $x$ from a landmark and velocity $v$. In the ideal undamped oscillator the acceleration is $a=-\omega^2x$. Speed increases on approach. Momentum carries the body through the landmark, where acceleration is zero. Once the body crosses, acceleration changes sign and slows it until velocity reaches zero at the turn. A side-only restoring force can also oscillate and gives a different waveform. In the ideal model $x$ and $v/\omega$ are same-unit quadratures, with sign and phase chosen by the orientation. [OpenStax, simple harmonic motion](https://openstax.org/books/university-physics-volume-1/pages/15-1-simple-harmonic-motion)

There are two constructions worth keeping distinct:

- **Read a phase contour.** Connect a seam, orientation, radius, angular calibration and two projection directions. Equal-time samples of a uniform angular advance give sinusoidal projections. Order of crossing and turning can be observed before equal angles or elapsed time are measurable. For an exact test of the metric part, let $u$ be the other same-unit projection. The rational map $(x',u')=((3x+4u)/5,(-4x+3u)/5)$ preserves $x^2+u^2$. It takes $(5,0)$ to $(3,-4)$ and then to $(-7/5,-24/5)$. A whole-tick receiver needs refinement or a chosen approximation with visible loss at the second step. This is a contour step even before it is calibrated to time. [Gojo contour rotation fixture](GojoContourRotationFixture.md)
- **Run a restoring process.** Connect an actual landmark, an observed displacement, a restoring gain, a time increment and the before/after state. One discrete model first updates velocity and then advances position. With $u=v\,\Delta t$ and $k=\omega^2(\Delta t)^2$, this kick-drift step is $u'=u-kx$, $x'=x+u'$. The position update uses the new $u'$. A single transition can realize that same dependency from its before-state. Two independently evaluated old-state rules would instead take the position step with $u$. That is a different update. An isolated physical implementation would also need a source or reaction account and compatible momentum units. [Gojo conditional and temporal mechanics](GojoConditionalTemporalMechanics.md) · [HAKMEM, items 149–152](https://www.plover.com/misc/hbaker-archive/hakmem/hacks.html#item149)

The unrounded kick-drift map preserves $u^2+kx^2-kxu$, which is positive definite for $0<k<4$. This gives bounded motion in that range. It returns exactly after six steps for $k=1$ from $(1,0)$, along $(0,-1)$, $(-1,-1)$, $(-1,0)$, $(0,1)$, $(1,1)$, then back. An exact period needs its own evidence at other gains. Quantization, clipping, and changes in sampling can change the orbit. A qualitative sequence of crossing and turning can establish an event cycle. Equal phase increments and sinusoidal measurement have additional requirements. [Claude’s sampled oscillator and schedule](https://github.com/It3rate/Gojo/blob/e0f06b05/ops_claude/GojoOperations-2026-11.md#L579) · [Neon’s kick-drift proposal](https://github.com/It3rate/Gojo/blob/1e842ae0/Gojo/docs/GojoOperations-2026-11.md#L480)

The Gojo numeric contour fixture applies discrete turns using supplied radial geometry. The continuous phase read and integrated spring dynamics remain open. These are useful later tests of whether stacked laws carry the right time, orientation and calibration evidence.
