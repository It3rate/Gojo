# Bourgoin atlas: sliding contacts and open courses

This experiment revises B24, B26, B29 and B31–B33 against the source diagrams.
The [gallery](../../experiments/QualitativeFrieze/results/bourgoin-patterns/gallery.html)
and [whole atlas](../../experiments/QualitativeFrieze/results/bourgoin-patterns/overview.html)
show the current native constructions. Each revised card retains its previous
SVG and has an adjustable source overlay. The earlier
[vocabulary study](GojoBourgoinVocabularyStudy.md) describes checkpoint
`9869814`; its case descriptions and counts are historical.

## Sliding is a contact question

Translation was already expressible. What the earlier B24/B29 recipes lacked
was a side with an inspectable partial contact, rather than just a whole side
or corner. The corrected construction keeps these stages separate:

```text
actual whole-side polygon prototype
  -> ordered side subdivision, retaining the original endpoint displacement
  -> actual transformed images and relative disposition words
  -> exposed contact-end ports
  -> current seam questions and accepted/blocked endpoint agreements
```

Each side retains its whole word, contact subword, exposed subword, ordered
actual replacement, reduction and zero displacement-difference witness.
This accounts for the side's formal displacement. It is not material transfer,
an assertion of friction, or a filled-face calculation. Repeated coincident
strokes retain separate participation histories.

The authoring studies `CreateTriangleSlides` and `CreateHexagonSlides` produce
families that share the **actual same coarse prototype, frame and reach**.
The selected finite multiplicities become relation/placeholder words before
execution; there are no measured numeric carriers in the saved packets.

| Case | Earlier reading | Revised construction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B24 | Triangle tips around equally sized gap sides | Two opposed triangle images with partially shared sides. Three reaches per whole side split into one contact reach and two exposed reaches; shorter six-edge circuits emerge between triangles. |
| B29 | Hexagons with additional triangular hooks | One slid hexagon prototype. Four reaches per side split into three contact reaches and one exposed reach. Three exposed remainders form each small triangular circuit; no triangle motif is supplied. |

B24's selected periods are `(5,-1)` and `(1,4)` in the shared native frame.
B29's are `(8,-5)` and `(5,3)`. These pairs are inspection shorthand for
repeated directional participation, not a new coordinate/value store.
Tip-only/aligned controls and opposite signed hexagon slides preserve the
coarse contour while changing contact and handedness. A missing seam law
does not merge coincident endpoint positions; a wrong neighbour move blocks
the advertised connection.

There is a finite-window limit: B24's complete short six-edge route needs
three column slots. A 2×2 publication is valid but cannot enclose that route;
the witness is tested in a 3×2 publication. A closed native route does not,
by itself, prove that it bounds an empty or occupied face.

## Refining the motif without inventing the gaps

**B26** starts with a six-chord coarse hexagon through the flat-tip midpoint
landmarks. Each chord has an endpoint-preserving four-chord replacement.
Together they make six flat tips and six inward corners, with eighteen
turning corners and six additional collinear midpoint sites. The original
hexagonal disposition now leaves small six-sided gaps between three stars.
Those gaps are not separately placed motifs. Two stem prototypes each have
six actual substitution images, giving twelve centre rays. The construction
read admits the prototype refinement and its bridge to the executed image.
Twelve rays are a participation count, not a proof of twelvefold rotation or
equal angular spacing.
Petal heads, thick coloured bands and filled faces remain outside this sketch.

**B31** uses four actual quarter-related images of one slender, open,
three-chord arm. Each terminal hand travels outward beyond its wrist. The
staggered disposition places the junctions around a separate round marker.
The marker is a native twelve-chord closed contour, with flat facets and some
collinear subdivisions. It is a deliberately coarse circle approximation,
not an SVG circle hiding a diamond and not an analytic-circle law. The
source's short weave breaks and exact printed thickness remain untraced.
B31 has local arm-junction agreements but no cross-cell seam promise in this
selected construction. Its empty frontier is therefore intentional. The atlas
replay test now derives frontier expectations from declared neighbour moves,
rather than assuming every visually repeated pattern must connect its units.

## A line family can contain free ends, loops and long passages

**B32** has four open paths from two actual prototypes: two opposed course
backbones and two hooked attachments. The inner ends remain one source-grid
step apart, carried through the explicit diagonal word map.
Exposed intermediate roles admit the valley T contacts and cross-course
attachments. Fourteen executable chords replace the earlier single nested
spiral. Coincident boundary fragments still have separate histories.

**B33** has an open angular course and a two-chord attachment as its two
shared prototypes. The attachment meets two published course sites, creating
a small diamond circuit. Repeating the course joins its end to the next start.
Two oppositely handed course images occupy successive staggered rows in each
macrocell: four open paths and fourteen chords altogether. The second period
repeats this pair downward, leaving larger angled channels between the strips.
None of the authored paths is declared closed. Thus local closed routes and
long open passages can coexist without encoding the pattern as isolated
closed L-shaped ribbons.
B33's 3×3 publication has six continuous course components and eighteen graph
cycles, despite declaring zero closed paths. Those cycles require admitted
shared sites; they are not diamond or face opcodes.

The source overlay caught a disposition error after the basic course was
already correct. Widely spaced identical rows missed the dense field;
compressing the same-handed rows produced an unwanted ladder. Alternating
handedness and a half-drop aligned the actual line construction with the
source's lower field. Motif identity, row placement and row transform are
separate structural choices.

Channel continuity has a different evidential status from endpoint identity.
Native tests check the actual paths, ports and admitted joins. A sampled
chosen-basis complement can additionally check visible passages; that read
does not establish a native filled-face or general region algebra. The
source overlays remain adjustable visual comparisons, not pixel-based
certificates or generation instructions.
In particular, B31's initial source registration reflects the horizontal
display direction. It compares the chosen native frame with the engraving's
frame; alignment alone is not evidence of identical chirality or metric
geometry. B26's registration rotates the triangular screen interpretation.

**B34** remains a faceted loop-lattice approximation. Its explicit nonjoining
interior crossings do not encode over/under knot order, ribbon thickness or
the full source knot topology. This correction does not claim those missing
laws have been supplied.

## What this adds to the reusable vocabulary

- Partial-side correspondence: refine a whole span into ordered public
  subspans, then ask which are accepted contacts in the current disposition.
- Boundary substitution: retain coarse landmark promises while refining
  the path between them; new interstices can emerge without gap motifs.
- Open course with attachments: a compact recurring line family can produce
  local loops while preserving separate ends and longer passages.

These use existing native words, relations, substitutions, ports and seam
witnesses. No ornament-specific kernel primitive was added. The source still
guides authored choices; this is not automatic recognition, training, live
sliding dynamics or adaptive neighbour renegotiation. It supplies explicit
counterfactual examples for those later experiments.

## Reproduction and verification

```powershell
dotnet run --project experiments/QualitativeFrieze -c Release -- --bourgoin-patterns
dotnet test Gojo.slnx -c Release
```

Sources are the reviewed crops in the atlas and Jules Bourgoin's
[*Théorie de l'ornement*](https://archive.org/details/thoriedelorneme01bourgoog):
fig.193 and plates13/17/19/20. Construction prose is authored; the adjacent
structure and execution panels are reads of the imported native graph.
The previous six SVGs and their catalogue/diagnostics are retained in
`results/bourgoin-patterns/vocabulary-pass/`.

The regenerated thirty-six fields contain **404 sampled occurrences, 8,579
strokes and 6,989 accepted seams**. All complete with zero blocked or unresolved
interior connections, zero rejected construction reads and zero open evidence
scopes. Declared finite-frontier obligations remain separate. Every saved
packet contains only relation/placeholder nodes and zero numeric participants.
The thirty unaffected ink geometries match checkpoint `9869814`.

`dotnet test Gojo.slnx -c Release` passed **4,081 tests across twelve
assemblies**, with no failures or skips. The qualitative assembly has 1,651
cases. Twenty-eight new cases cover actual prototype reuse, native archive
replay, retained refinements and subspan accounting, emergent circuits,
opposite slides, missing/wrong seam laws, separated central ends and the finite
white-passage diagnostic. The latter uses quarter-reach sample links with
enough endpoint clearance to retain at least .2 reach along each entire link;
this remains a measured interpretation of native output, not a native face law.

Browser checks passed for thirty-six cards and computed structure panels,
sixteen adjustable source overlays, all atlas links, source image decoding,
occurrence colours, ports, construction landmarks and line weight. No browser
or SVG parse errors, broken local links, or desktop/mobile overflow were found.
The final atlas PNG and the six revised source overlays were visually reviewed.
