# Historical ornament rules for qualitative pattern experiments

Research checkpoint, 2026-10-17. Historical ornament manuals supply useful
construction grammars, diagnostic exercises and style preferences for the
pre-numeric pattern layer. Their strongest contribution is the relationship
between an underlying scaffold, selected ornament, intervening field shapes,
repeat boundaries and subordinate detail. Much of that relationship can be
expressed before lengths, angles or areas are calibrated.

The historical findings below come from selected relevant chapters and plates.
The proposed Gojo experiments are not new implemented behavior. Current code
and focused tests remain the authority for the capability table.

The [visual skeleton atlas](../../experiments/QualitativeFrieze/results/skeleton-atlas/index.html)
now illustrates ten construction families and twelve named goal patterns.
Its diagrams and target contracts are authored references, not additional
implemented Gojo laws. The glossary progresses from simple concepts to
structural elaborations; the goal cards provide expectations for future runs.

## Sources and scope

| Source | Edition and relevant material |
|---|---|
| Lewis Foreman Day, [*Ornamental Design*](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl) | 1888 collection of *The Anatomy of Pattern*, *The Planning of Ornament* and *The Application of Ornament*. Their page numbers restart; citations identify the constituent work. |
| Day, [second linked scan](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl_0) | 1890 collection, including revised/second editions. These two links represent one source family, not independent corroboration. |
| International Correspondence Schools, [*A Textbook on Ornamental Design*, volume 1](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala) | Scranton, International Textbook Co., c1901; lessons carry copyright dates from 1893 to 1901. Pagination restarts by section. The seam exercise is illustrated with foliage, but its repeat mechanics apply independently of freehand style. |
| David Ramsay Hay, [*Original Geometrical Diaper Designs*](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd) | 1844. Here a diaper is an all-over ornamental field. The essay's explanatory plates and the finished design plates have separate numbering. |
| Owen Jones, [*The Grammar of Ornament*](https://library.si.edu/digital-library/book/grammarornament00jone) | 1856; an additional source for construction, hierarchy and enrichment. |

Use the manuals as proposed construction and style contracts. Their claims
about universal beauty, musical proportions or the superiority of particular
traditions do not establish mathematical or perceptual laws.

## Rules worth carrying forward

The historical observations and our proposed computational interpretations are
separate columns. A construction requirement can become a hard obligation when
the experiment explicitly adopts it; an aesthetic preference remains a chosen
profile.

| Historical observation | Proposed Gojo interpretation |
|---|---|
| A repeat must continue across adjoining copies. Day discusses end/start and side/side matching; the textbook defines continuous repetition. [Day, *Anatomy*, pp.34–36](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n51/mode/1up); [textbook, §2 pp.72–73](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala/page/n135/mode/1up). | Require declared periodic seam correspondences. This means compatible continuation under the chosen contract, not that every motif must touch every neighbour. Keep unresolved and blocked seams visible. |
| Change the tile cut to reveal awkward joins and empty pockets. Day moves former corners to the centre; the textbook exchanges halves, repairs, then rechecks other arrangements. [Day, *Anatomy*, p.43](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n60/mode/1up); [textbook, §2 pp.80–83, Fig.43](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala/page/n144/mode/1up). | Observe the same periodic field through different fundamental regions. A subsequent repair is a distinct mutation and must renew every affected seam agreement. |
| Intervening figures contribute alongside the projecting forms. Hay explicitly judges the shapes generated between neighbouring forms. [Hay, p.31](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n66/mode/1up). | Publish occupied and complementary regions under one scope. Inspect the gap's contour and connections as well as the motif. Equal importance does not imply equal area. |
| Greek frets can give motif and ground corresponding outlines; several dark/light treatments can reuse one skeleton. [Textbook, §2 pp.16–17, Figs.8–9](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala/page/n79/mode/1up). | Keep scaffold, selected boundary, fill assignment and role-exchange map distinct. Test counterchange as an actual correspondence rather than an ink-percentage target. |
| Establish main forms and divisions before enriching them. Jones states this sequence; the textbook keeps subdivisions subordinate at distant viewing. [Jones, propositions 7–10, p.4](https://archive.org/details/grammarornament00Jone/page/n13/mode/1up); [textbook, §4 p.84](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala/page/n315/mode/1up). | Give enrichment an explicit correspondence to a retained coarse skeleton. Ask separately whether the main structure survives a coarser read and whether the finer detail adds useful variety. |
| One field can have several construction histories. Day illustrates six constructions of a star diaper; Hay selects several designs from a common scaffold. [Day, *Anatomy*, plate 16](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n102/mode/1up); [Hay, plate XXII](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n86/mode/1up) and [p.39](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n88/mode/1up). | Preserve both output correspondence and causal provenance. A recognized image need not identify one unique machine. Compare alternative generators without merging their identities or histories. |
| Lines and masses both need consideration; visual balance is not identical to exact symmetry. [Day, *Planning*, pp.30–31](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n184/mode/1up) and [pp.45–46](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n199/mode/1up). | Keep path incidence, occupied field, complement and symmetry as separate observations. Add perceptual balance only with an explicit observer and basis. |
| A half-drop changes the directions that repetition emphasizes; a small drop can produce conspicuous diagonal stepping. [Day, *Anatomy*, pp.34–36](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n51/mode/1up). | Couple row placement and motif phase through a shared period. Observe emergent alignments; do not assume every diagonal or stripe is a defect. |
| Varied sizes can retain a shared construction rule. Hay couples rectangle dimensions and prefers a common angle for projections along a line. [Hay, p.30](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n51/mode/1up) and [p.31](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n66/mode/1up). | Represent shared shape or turn promises explicitly. Mere wider/higher ordering does not prove equal aspect ratio or angle. Keep that stronger claim unresolved without correspondence or a supplier. |
| Two independent outlines can interweave into a more elaborate fret. White interruptions can communicate over/under order. [Textbook, §2 pp.17–18, Fig.10](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala/page/n81/mode/1up) and [p.72](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala/page/n135/mode/1up). | Retain thread identity through crossings. A screen intersection is not automatically a material junction; over/under is an additional local relation. |

The books also provide a useful disagreement. Jones advocates particular
proportions in proposition 9; Day's *Planning*, p.46, argues against expecting
fixed proportional rules to produce distinguished design. Test these as
different authored preferences rather than building either into the kernel.

## A small catalogue of reusable skeletons

These names index examples. Their meaning must come from the connected
construction, not a runtime switch on a style name. Shared reach and equality
promises need not give a numerical size, but they must be present if the claim
depends on them.

| Skeleton | Connected construction to encode | Extra premises or useful variants |
|---|---|---|
| Periodic border | Ordered sites, recurring landmarks, a forward repeat passage and optional return paths. [Day, *Anatomy*, plate 4](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n78/mode/1up). | Step, fret, zigzag, interrupted band; detect return over the same occupied edge separately from a legitimate return on another level. |
| Selected cross-lattice | Two independent repeat directions, shared sites, selected edges and optional region fills. [Day, plate 4](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n78/mode/1up). | Checks, keys, basket-like selections; crossing and junction contracts can differ on the same projected lattice. |
| Staggered or half-drop field | Row adjacency plus a phase/placement correspondence; two half-shifts correspond to the shared full period. [Day, plates 25–26](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n120/mode/1up). | Aligned, alternating and staggered views; retain the extra period promise rather than inventing a numeric midpoint. |
| Counterchanged stepped fret | One bounded field partition, an outline map and an occupied/complement role exchange. [Textbook, Fig.8](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala/page/n79/mode/1up). | Same scaffold with different fills, contrasting colours or scoped motif/field inversion. |
| Two-thread interlace | Two ordered paths, independent identities, crossing sites and optional over/under relations. [Textbook, Fig.10](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala/page/n81/mode/1up). | Hold geometry constant while changing crossings into junctions, or reject an inconsistent over/under circuit. |
| Nested square network | Boundary cycles, containment, corner-sharing and repeated turn correspondences. [Hay, plate X](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n54/mode/1up). | A four-sided cycle alone does not prove a square. Equal sides and the required turn law are additional promises. |
| Triangular, lozenge and star field | Alternating cycles, shared edges, compound cells and selected subgraphs. [Day, plate 6](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl/page/n82/mode/1up); [Hay, plate XII](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n58/mode/1up). | Topological triangles are available before equilateral metric interpretation. Keep angle and length equalities explicit. |
| Mixed-cell scaffold | Different boundary cycles share edges in a repeated adjacency network. [Hay, plate XIII](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n60/mode/1up). | Local polygons do not prove global closure or an indefinitely repeatable Euclidean tiling. Test both repeat directions. |
| Several ornaments on one scaffold | Retained construction graph with alternative selected paths and filled regions. [Hay, plate XXII](https://archive.org/details/originalgeometri00hayd/page/n86/mode/1up). | Particularly useful for learning: preserve the scaffold while varying edge selection, fill and local enrichment. |

## Classification and judgment

Keep four kinds of result separate:

1. **Construction obligations:** required seam compatibility, valid scope,
   retained identity, legal crossings, declared closure, non-retracing and
   conserved participation. Each verdict needs an actual witness.
2. **Descriptive structure:** repeat generators, motif orbit, connected
   components, junction vocabulary, thread order, motif/field correspondence,
   hierarchy and emergent alignment. These classify without ranking.
3. **Selected preferences:** regular gap rhythm, recognizable principal form,
   restrained accidental striping, a chosen amount of variety, or preferred
   symmetry. A profile can reject or leave these unknown without changing their
   structural meaning.
4. **Measured or perceptual reads:** area fraction, Euclidean clearance,
   stroke thickness, exact angular distortion, visual weight and human ratings.
   Attach the calibration, sampling basis or observer. A topological gap centre
   is not automatically a Euclidean distance maximum.

A native assessment can ask whether every declared seam has a current
agreement, a field correspondence preserves adjacency, or an enrichment
preserves a chosen coarse graph. It cannot derive uniform physical clearance
from adjacency alone. Finite occupancy shells give useful resolution-relative
evidence, not arbitrary continuous measurements.

Prefer a vector of witnessed outcomes, or selection requiring all adopted hard
obligations, before an optional weighted preference total. Otherwise several
pleasant symmetries can compensate numerically for a broken seam. Missing
evidence must remain unknown rather than scoring as a favorable zero. See the
existing [aesthetic profile contract](GojoPatternAestheticRules.md).

## Proposed experiment sequence

### Rebase and repair one repeating field

Start with a small rectilinear field and explicit opposite-boundary
correspondences. Supply unmeasured subdivision landmarks and, where a half-cut
is claimed, its shared half-period promise. The geometric fact that a landmark
is halfway cannot be inferred merely from its being between two endpoints.

Expose original, horizontally rebased, vertically rebased and jointly rebased
views of the same structure. A rebase changes observation and presentation;
it does not redraw the motif, move material or duplicate packets. Preserve
participant correspondence and cut edges across views. Reassemble the two
independent shifts in either order and check the declared correspondence on
the shared field, including the corner where the two seams meet.

Begin with cuts through declared landmarks. Do not invent a fractional stroke
position to split a contour. Deriving the field partition from new drawing,
publishing a repaired partition and accepting arbitrary partial-contour edits
are explicit extensions beyond the current paired-variant adaptive fixture.

Publish a gap candidate through a field surface. A local rule may propose a
straight-line decoration or motif replacement. The target accepts or blocks
that proposal under its own grammar and capacity. After acceptance, obtain
fresh agreements for every affected seam before queued participation passes.
Keep old agreements as history and blocked proposals as visible evidence.

Useful controls: an unchanged field looks different under a new cut but has
the same incidence; a repair improves one view while breaking another seam;
an old seam witness is offered after a motif revision; and a valid isolated
motif is mistaken for a globally compatible repeat.
Also test an apparently blank pocket whose observation is incomplete or whose
scope excludes a crossing stroke: absence of occupancy evidence is not proof
of emptiness.

### Compare scaffold selections and their complements

Use a small shared lattice to generate a stepped fret, a counterchanged
version and a different fill treatment. Keep construction, selected strokes,
occupied regions and complementary regions available as separate surfaces.
Try candidate correspondences against both motif and field.

Compare equal-occupancy fields with different connectivity; a valid topological
role-exchange whose physical areas differ under a supplied interpretation; and
an all-filled field that games
an empty-pocket objective. The native question is correspondence under the
declared scope, not a universal requirement for half the area to be dark.

### Enrich a principal skeleton without erasing it

Give a border or nested field a coarse scaffold and a small number of legal
enrichment slots. Test a proposed coarse-observation law against actual
construction correspondences. An extra branch can preserve the coarse read;
closing an important gap can destroy it. A policy intentionally replacing the
skeleton should expose that replacement rather than calling it refinement.

This is where long-range alignment and dominance observers would help. Mere
counts of turns, motifs or filled cells cannot establish which structure a
viewer will see first.

### Retain several machines for the same pattern

Reconstruct a small star or lattice selection by two routes inspired by Day's
plate 16. Require output correspondence, then compare construction history,
shared parts, seam obligations and execution costs separately. Recognition
should retain both supported explanations until a further question or witness
distinguishes them.

## Learning and evaluation

These constructions provide a bounded vocabulary of proposals: change repeat
phase through a legal correspondence, select another scaffold edge, exchange
field roles, insert an independent thread, enrich a declared slot, or repair
an exposed seam. A genetic search can mutate those operations. A transformer
or human can propose them intentionally. A market can allocate inspection,
repair and refinement effort. All use the same acceptance surfaces and
witnesses; proposal source does not determine legality.

First compare these proposal sources on the same small tasks and operation
budget. Record accepted, blocked and unresolved changes; retained coarse
structure; demonstrated symmetry; motif/field correspondences; and execution
cost. Record measured gap and crowding reads separately when supplied. Visual
preference can later be evaluated independently, including disagreement among
viewers. Selecting examples because we already like them is useful design
exploration, but does not validate the resulting preference rules.

The immediate priority is the cut-and-shift exercise, followed by alternative
scaffold selections. Together they test whether one readable construction can
support multiple views, local repairs and intentional variation while retaining
the uncertainty and provenance needed for learning. See the existing
[adaptive training discussion](GojoAdaptiveWallpaperTraining.md) for the current
proposal/selection boundary.
