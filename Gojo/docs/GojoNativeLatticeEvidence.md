# Native lattice evidence through connected query ports

Experimental layer above the kernel, 21 October 2026. This connects the
[structural-potential reader](GojoStructuralPotential.md) to executed native
ornament programs. The source offers passages, endpoint words and explicit
interpolation contracts; the reader calculates the premises it needs instead
of receiving an authored list of Yes/No answers.

[Interactive source and question gallery](../../experiments/QualitativeFrieze/results/native-lattice-evidence/gallery.html)
and [all-source overview](../../experiments/QualitativeFrieze/results/native-lattice-evidence/overview.html).

## What is connected

```text
executed ornament program / field
  -> published stroke graph, endpoint words and passage contracts
  -> local collinear-support view with source correspondence
  -> selected pair of supports in each direction
  -> actual candidate's exposed query port
  -> requested native premise calculation
  -> admitted evidence / blocked reply / remaining open question
  -> existing construction laws
  -> source answer and optional observational boundary
```

The same six construction questions remain available: supporting-line boundary,
rectangle and square potential, and their finite-support versions. The new
supplier discovers support selections from the source graph. It derives
separation, direction independence, relative spacing and finite encounter order
from the offered native words. It reads whole-passage straightness from an
explicit contract; endpoint displacement alone does not establish it.

The source, candidate, predicate, revision and response remain connected native
participants. A reply is admitted only for the actual request, port, revision
and predicate. A reply for another source or stale request becomes blocked
evidence. Existing evidence is consulted before the service; repeated leaves
share a read within one query. A decisive refusal can stop finer queries.
Request capacity and traversal capacity have separate visible cuts.

This is non-draining observation. There is no material tension transfer,
settlement or conservation claim in the service callback. Its calculated
evidence and request witnesses explain the result without a new kernel
primitive or a recipe-name classifier.

## Public structure and retained construction

Publication uses fresh site, stroke and uncalibrated reach identities. It
preserves actual incidence, direction, repeated reach participation and the
open/closed state of offered bindings. Correspondences back to the executed
source remain on a separate private audit surface. The public graph does not
carry a stroke's construction cause, or an earlier closed position that was
intentionally withheld from the endpoint-word port.

This separation matters: merely omitting the top-level provenance wrapper did
not remove ancestral execution structure reachable through the original
stroke's cause. The reader already ignored that ancestry, but exporting it
would have weakened the sparse-communication experiment and enlarged its
archive. Publication now makes that boundary structural.

Public-port transport serializes the exposed native structure, discards its
authoring helper, rebinds its frame through one shared correspondence, and
reconstructs a new supplier. Site sharing and reach aliases survive that
transport. Labels and recipe IDs do not select its answers.

## Support reduction and geometric scope

Subsegments with the same axis displacement can form a longer support candidate
through actual shared sites. At a branch the reducer follows a unique same-axis
continuation when available; it stops at a bend or ambiguous continuation. The
later premise read checks the interpolation of every constituent. Each source
passage and its orientation remain visible. Duplicate links may share a support
read, but each constituent's interpolation and endpoint consistency must still
be checked.
The original mechanical graph remains unchanged.

The current reader handles two straight directional families in an explicitly
selected common planar interpretation. Its geometric model relates the four
directed frame actions to a closed marked contour with a common radial reach.
A separate equal-turn promise permits the geometric quarter-turn read.
Independent axes or the renderer's horizontal and vertical lines alone do not
prove perpendicularity. Cross-axis gap comparison explicitly maps each gap to
one ordered axis, preserving its reach identities and retaining the
substitution witness. Treating a shared reach as the same unmeasured extent in
both directions belongs to this selected interpretation; it is not calibration
discovered from an arbitrary graph. Unrelated reach atoms remain unresolved
rather than unequal.

Finite coverage compares the formal encounter word with each support's offered
endpoint words. It retains before/after order and accepts an encounter at an
endpoint. A possible intersection of extended supports does not establish that
both finite strokes reach it. Neither this encounter nor a derived corner
joins the original mechanical paths.

A successful finite read can emit the same four-edge observational boundary
used in the preceding experiment. An independent motif reader checks its
circuit. A circuit is not an elementary face: it may contain smaller boundaries,
crossing strokes or other ornament. No empty interior, fill ownership or
material connectivity follows from this result.

## Recorded cases

The run includes thirteen sources, four of them sampled from existing Bourgoin
programs: B01 running brick, B07 alternating-width bands, B17 rectangle mesh
with diagonals, and B18 square mesh with diagonals. B07 is also reread under an
explicit additional turn model. The other sources isolate clipping, coincident
supports, withheld endpoint information, unspecified interpolation and a
notched route.

| Source / question | Recorded result |
| --- | --- |
| Three supports in each direction, without equal-turn promise | Nine finite boundaries; rectangle questions remain open |
| Same source with explicit turn model | Nine finite rectangles, five satisfying the shared-reach square question |
| One family's intervals doubled | Nine finite rectangles; two square selections remain |
| One horizontal support clipped | Five finite boundaries supported, four refuted; extended-line potential remains available |
| One endpoint word withheld | Three finite boundaries supported, six open |
| Whole-passage interpolation withheld | All nine finite boundaries remain open |
| Actual B07 graph | Thirty-two stroke subsegments reduce to eight support passages; finite rectangle questions remain open without the extra turn promise |
| Same B07 graph with explicit turn model | All thirty-two searched selections support finite rectangles; nine support squares |

Nine support-pair boundaries do not mean nine elementary cells. The count
includes larger boundaries spanning several smaller cells. Counts are finite
diagnostics of the selected source window and candidate search, not numerical
premises consumed by the geometry rules.

The saved run evaluates 209 selected candidates, 1,254 construction questions
and 1,254 independently transported public-port comparisons. All transported
answers and service-request counts agree. The gallery includes 213 successful
boundary views alongside their actual source supports.

All 104 focused tests pass, including the preceding construction-rule tests.
Browser validation covers 292 states, 435 SVGs and 305 local references with
no errors. The full solution passes all 4,860 tests. These are mechanism and
transport checks on the supplied sources,
not recognition accuracy on an independent dataset.

The gallery renders the published graph and words. Its passage and site counts
refer to that publication, whose identities are fresh; they are not an export
of the source execution's site identities or its construction history.

Four sources exceed the default capacity of thirty-two candidates. Their
unsearched support selections remain visible. In particular, all first
thirty-two selections of the notched-route example are refused; this does not
claim the remaining sixty-eight selections are impossible. The current
lexicographic selection order is a useful limitation to expose before adding
candidate ranking.

## Counterexamples and unresolved work

Focused checks cover wrong-source and stale replies, service/traversal cuts,
native port transport, malformed passages, open binding inventories, unknown
reach order, clipped coverage, coincident supports, bends and duplicate links
with inconsistent source data. An unresolved signed gap cannot be treated as
a known magnitude difference: two uncalibrated gap expressions may still have
equal magnitude in an admissible interpretation.

Completion cannot assume away an explicit open supplier assessment, a blocked
reply or a computational cut. Revising an assessed account needs a further
explicit refinement context. The previous candidate and its qualifications
remain available.

This establishes a connection to actual native source structure, not general
ornament recognition. Diagonal and mixed-direction passages are retained as
unsupported by this two-family reader. They are not negative evidence that
the ornament lacks other shapes. Candidate capacity bounds constructed frames;
the current enumerator still records every omitted support combination, so it
is not a scalable global search budget. Arbitrary affine families, elementary
face discovery, cross-candidate reuse, candidate ranking and learned proposals
remain further work. There are no learned probabilities or bitmap inputs.

The supplier also does not reconstruct a withheld endpoint binding backwards
from another endpoint and an offered displacement word. Open means unresolved
by the current exposed read and derivation rules, rather than a proof that no
further native calculation could answer the question.

The follow-up [native interior discovery study](GojoNativeInteriorDiscovery.md)
finds local boundary walks and shape-feature evidence from these exposed native
passages, with separate obstruction reads and conditional gap closures.

## Reproduce

```powershell
dotnet run --project experiments/QualitativeFrieze -c Release -- --native-lattice-evidence
node experiments/QualitativeFrieze/tools/check-native-lattice-evidence.cjs
dotnet test Tests/QualitativeExecution.Tests -c Release --filter "FullyQualifiedName~NativeLine|FullyQualifiedName~StructuralEvidenceService"
dotnet test Gojo.slnx
```

Use `--candidates 256` for a larger selection window, and `--output DIRECTORY`
to keep a separate run. Saved outputs include `summary.json`, the interactive
gallery, per-selection diagrams, browser-check results and the native
`sources.gojo.json.gz` archive. Browser inspection needs Playwright available
to the checker; the C# experiment itself runs locally without an API key.
