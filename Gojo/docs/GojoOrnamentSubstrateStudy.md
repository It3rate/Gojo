# Continuous strands, curved substrates and periodic cells

## Historical sources and proposed construction families

The sources below suggest constructions for the next native ornament study.
They do not establish how any particular historical object was originally
designed, and the new examples should be labelled adaptations rather than
facsimiles. A visible repeated result can support several construction reads.

Lewis Foreman Day's *Pattern Design* first appeared in 1903. The consulted
[Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/patterndesignboo00dayl)
contains a later impression; its title matter lists 1903, 1915 and 1923. Its
[full text](https://archive.org/stream/patterndesignboo00dayl/patterndesignboo00dayl_djvu.txt)
was consulted directly, particularly chapters V, VIII and X. The earlier
[*Anatomy of Pattern*](https://archive.org/details/ornamentaldesign00dayl)
provides related plates with different numbering. Use the book title as well
as the figure number when comparing them.

Owen Jones's [*Grammar of Ornament*, 1856](https://archive.org/details/grammarornament00Jone)
was consulted through the retained original scan/OCR, with the
[original French edition's propositions](https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Grammaire_de_l_ornement/Principes_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9raux)
available as a primary transcription. The historical assertions about cultural
superiority or a single origin of ornament are not assumptions of this study.

The [International Correspondence Schools textbook, volume 1](https://archive.org/details/textbookonorname01inteiala)
has section-specific pagination. Its references below are to §2, *Freehand
Drawing*, rather than the scan's overall page count.

| **H07 — Dropped and reversed continuous repeats** | Day, *Pattern Design*, pp.60–72, figs.81–91; *Anatomy*, pp.34–38 and plates25–26: phase, matching edges and alternate repeat plans. Mirroring alone does not guarantee that neighbouring tangent directions agree. |

[Source](https://archive.org/details/grammarornament00Jone). | Retain one large curved frame, a second interlaced network and fine offshoots with separate exposed selections. Let colour intentions change which chain dominates without rebuilding the underlying relations. A visual upper plane does not automatically establish material transfer or a depth order at every crossing. |

The strongest direct precedent for the user's opposed-boundary idea is H06.
For the new engine, its mechanical interpretation should be stronger than an
area balance: matching additions and removals need the **same ordered seam
profile under the actual repeat correspondence**. Two equal-size but differently
shaped edits can preserve area and still leave a gap or an overlap. This is a
proposed structural requirement of the experiment, not a geometric theorem
claimed for the whole historical discussion.

## Structural questions suggested by the sources

These questions are proposed experiment targets. They should not be reported
as implemented capabilities until their native readers and tests exist.

1. Can a repeated section's exit port feed the next section's entrance port,
   with unresolved tangent or side-boundary correspondence kept visible?
2. Can a curved scaffold be read as continuous strands and also as a sequence
   of bounded compartments, without discarding the first read?
3. Can one seam recipe participate in two opposed cell boundaries, with an
   explicit transform and traversal correspondence rather than duplicated
   coordinates?
4. Can layer and strand identity survive colour changes, width changes,
   local occlusion, a changed crop and a different query focus?
5. When a wide band folds or collides, is that a blocked construction, a permitted
   crossing, a joined region or unresolved support under the selected question?
6. If the frame itself bends, are motifs attached to its local relation surface,
   or are they merely placed by an unrelated global coordinate recipe?

The literature gives practical examples of the first five concerns. Mapping a
whole repeated design onto a general curved substrate is our extension of those
examples. It should be evaluated through explicit correspondence and retained
loss, rather than described as a historical algorithm recovered from a plate.

## Implemented experiment

The [gallery](../../experiments/QualitativeFrieze/results/ornament-substrate/gallery.html)
and [whole sheet](../../experiments/QualitativeFrieze/results/ornament-substrate/overview.html)
retain twelve finite designs. Earlier composition and band studies remain
unchanged. Each new design has four readings: repeat substrate, one prominent
strand with its repeat events, paired boundaries/cell cycles, and finished colour.
The substrate can also be overlaid on the finished image.

| ID | Construction | What the example tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S001 | Unbroken plain weave | Five horizontal and five vertical full-length bands; 25 independent crossing events, alternating order. No cell-sized segmentation of these strands. |
| S002 | Two-over two-under twill | The same continuous-strand mechanism with a four-phase crossing cycle. The resulting diagonal order differs while the substrate remains an ordinary grid. |
| S003 | Diagonal braid | An oblique pair of repeat generators, long diagonal strands and explicit crossing order. |
| S004 | Three-family triangular net | Joined substrate sites, selected triangular edge cycles, and shared edges rather than overlapping closed triangle motifs. |
| S005 | Continuous waves | Reusable cubic profile sections form one shared-site thread per course; the recurrence landmarks remain available through the curved deviation. |
| S006 | Opposed waves and ogee lenses | Two opposite continuous chains share repeated sites; lens-shaped spaces emerge between them. Separate leaf units occupy those spaces. |
| S007 | Scalloped half-drop courses | A dropped row generator and one scalloped chain per course. The curved profile crosses its repeat points without acquiring terminal caps. |
| S008 | Sinuous foliate cables | Continuous cubic stems, repeated endpoint encounters and separate placed leaf compounds. Leaf placement is not yet a general live tangential attachment law. |
| S009 | S-shaped counterchange cells | A shared curved edge lattice, closed cell cycles and translated opposite profiles. |
| S010 | Paired tab-and-recess compartments | Three-part straight/curved boundary profiles; every excursion is reused as the adjacent cell's recess. |
| S011 | Bowed triangular counterchange | Curving the shared edges changes the visible triangular compartments while preserving the underlying repeat landmarks. |
| S012 | Interlocking oblique feather cells | The tab/recess construction on an oblique substrate with separate alternating interior leaves. |

These are construction examples, not a learned aesthetic ranking. In S004 and
S011 the cell cycles are deliberately selected from the known edge network;
this run does not discover the faces of an arbitrary unknown drawing.

## Native encoding

The lowest added capability is a repeat occurrence surface independent of any
particular motif or strand. In the selected common-reach basis:

```text
lattice = ((frame -> (origin -> (U -> V))) ->
           ((Closed(U phases) -> Closed(V phases)) -> (U links -> V links)))
          -> Closed(actual occurrence nodes)

node = (actual site -> actual position word) ->
       ((U phase -> V phase) -> (horizontal receipt ->
        (vertical receipt -> route agreement)))

visit = ((lattice -> actual node) -> actual envelope) ->
        (start difference -> end difference)

cross = (lattice -> actual node) ->
        ((first envelope -> second envelope) -> crossing witness)
```

Each interior occurrence retains two real additive routes from predecessor
occurrences and a checked zero difference. Neither a matching coordinate nor a
shared phase creates a shared physical site. A weave event selects an existing
long envelope and does not cut it. A curved endpoint visit only establishes the
provided endpoint incidence; it does not manufacture an interior intersection.

The source of each generated spine is now a reusable native local profile:

```text
profile = frame -> (Closed(local control words) ->
                   (Closed(side A offset words) -> Closed(side B offset words)))

profile application =
  (actual profile -> (anchor selection -> actual anchor word)) ->
  (Closed(actual additive receipts) -> actual generated curve)
```

At a repeat landmark the anchor selection references the actual lattice/node
surface and consumes its position word. Every curve control consumes the exact
word returned by its additive receipt. Repeated profiles reuse actual native
control/offset scopes. The finite authoring cache only locates these profiles;
its array keys are not the definition of the resulting pattern. Non-landmark
anchors, such as an internal tab start, retain their supplied common-reach word.

Curve sections in a continuous thread share actual spine and side endpoint
sites. The seam read separately checks positions, tangent direction, same-side
agreement and exchanged-side agreement. The scallop valleys intentionally have
corners; continuous does not mean smooth everywhere.

Generation and verification are distinct. The boundary check consumes each
actual source control under a native substitution and displacement, then
compares the returned word with the corresponding target control:

```text
pair request =
  ((frame -> (source curve -> target curve)) -> (transform -> translation)) ->
  (target curve -> selected target curve)

boundary pair = pair request ->
  Closed((source control -> target control) ->
         (substitution receipt -> (displacement receipt -> difference witness)))
```

A reversed cell edge retains an actual reversed curve plus correspondence to
the original. Translated opposite profiles can agree without sharing endpoint
identity. `Welded` in this narrow reader concerns matching actual endpoint
aliases at the same positions; it is not a union operation or a quotient-space
gluing theorem. Neighbouring cell cycles instead reuse the same edge sections
and their explicitly reversed reads.

The design root connects the source unit, lattice, per-thread event selections,
boundary checks, actual cell cycles and profile applications. Role and colour
selections stay separate. Native archives retain this graph and its aliases;
they are review/transport packets, not a new generic standalone curved-program
executor.

## What is and is not pre numeric here

The construction uses explicit finite counts and repeated common-reach moves in
a selected independent basis. These give executable unmeasured positions and
relative profiles, not a physical centimetre calibration. Exact native sums,
opposed moves and polynomial interpolation produce the curve controls. Floating
point coordinates, RGB values, visibility masks and SVG commands appear at the
presentation boundary.

The substrate remains a repeat frame while the paths between its landmarks can
bend. This is not yet an arbitrary curved coordinate field that automatically
warps every attached sibling. A future profile edit can be checked against
retained correspondence, but this run does not implement autonomous live
retargeting of all dependent motifs.

The pale coloured cell interiors are a display reading of selected closed
contours. Their closure is checked through actual consecutive site identity
and position. This alone establishes neither simple area nor absence of a
bisecting edge. No material is transferred by generation or observation;
geometric excursions and recesses are not a carrier-value conservation claim.

## Results, validation and remaining gaps

The finite corpus has **99 certified straight crossing events, 499 endpoint
visits, 205 compatible full boundary correspondences, 100 closed cell cycles,
and 427 curved source/decorative spans**. All twelve substrates and templates
read completely; all supplied boundary pairs agree. No opposite pair gains
endpoint welding merely from matching after translation. These are local
construction checks, not independent replications or beauty probabilities.

Across the twelve separate native worlds, **31 local profiles feed 493 source
span applications**, before the independently composed decorative units. For
example, S005 uses one profile for 30 curved spans; S010 uses six profiles for
93 straight/curved spans. The retained archives deliberately expand the
application and verification receipts: about **37.3 MB compressed** for this
run. Reusable authoring structure is therefore established, while a compact
standalone executable transport for this combined surface remains future work.

Browser validation checked all 60 SVGs, 12 native archives, 69 browser states
and 88 local references without errors. Its generic archive check verifies
quiet carriers and integer addresses; semantic pre-numeric conformance comes
from the exact native readers and tests, not a blanket ban on timeline ordinals.
The final `dotnet test Gojo.slnx` run passed **5,263 tests across twelve test
assemblies**, including all 25 new substrate checks. The captured generation
took about 100 seconds while the full solution tests ran concurrently; it is
not an isolated throughput benchmark.

Seventeen primitive tests cover missing and dependent bases, forged or missing
route receipts, foreign equal-coordinate landmarks, endpoint visits versus
crossing, complete curved profile comparison, explicit reversal, transport
aliases, and quiet unmeasured holders. Eight integration tests cover the twelve
designs, unsplit weave strands, shared side continuity, source-profile consumption,
cell closure and boundary reuse. A changed interior bulge fails even when both
curve endpoints still match. Curved interior crossing remains open.

Remaining gaps are a general curved-crossing supplier, exact constant-distance
offsets, global simple-region and non-overlap checks, arbitrary curved-substrate
transport, and live local attachment/renegotiation of leaves and other siblings.
The bowed and tabbed cells make the local correspondence concrete; they do not
settle those wider questions. Recognition, learned defaults and aesthetic
training have not been run in this experiment.
