# First assessment: live ports work; selection remains crude

The fixed protocol demonstrates actual data-bearing attachment points,
prospective service promises, bounded local negotiation and conserved resource
exchange. It does not demonstrate an efficient market or autonomous evolution.
The negative results are useful: several shortcomings are now observable in
connected behavior rather than hidden in a single fitness score.

The protocol and stimuli were committed as `caf8cae` before the first four
trials. No policy or price parameter was tuned afterward. Raw observer replays,
selected native witnesses, final worlds and measured counters are preserved in
[results](results/first-run.md). This assessment aggregates those existing
native reads; its tables never feed the learner.

## The attachment really carries data

The most important contract check changes an exposed port's live coordinate,
settles its already attached receiver, and observes the receiver change. The
earlier issued prediction remains unchanged. The first draft instead copied a
coordinate into a separate delivery site; review caught and repaired that
wiring before the fixed trials. The final publication directly consumes the
actual exposed site through its executable native calibration.

The richer interpreter's `d` offer is the same difference recipe used inside
its `2d-a` extrapolator. The direction offer is a synthesized read with its own
basis and explicit proxy to source units. Neither requires a named concept or
terminal-answer lookup to carry its meaning. Only admitted paid computations
refresh current published data; an unpaid diagnostic forecast is a separate
pure read. Missing current publication is displayed as absent, not as a stale
number or a known zero.

In the main trial, the fine consumer purchased the intermediate port 38 times
and the extrapolated port nine times. The direction consumer purchased a mix
of all four offers. Usage counts include paid promises even when their outcome
is missing or still pending; they are not success counts. Different consumers
can therefore use different points of the same
calculation and assess them through different requested bases.

## Results including unserved demand

The generated summary separately reports errors on paid, evaluated services.
That is useful for inspecting service quality but omits occasions with no
purchase. The table below includes **all evaluated demanded issues**, using
the explicitly supplied zero/no-service prediction when no route was purchased.
It preserves the two consumer bases rather than adding incompatible errors.

| Trial | Direction service coverage | Direction error: no service / delivered | Fine service coverage | Change error: no service / delivered |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Changing input | 84 / 107 | 91 / 16 | 84 / 107 | 379 / 222 |
| Fine demand withdrawn | 88 / 107 | 91 / 15 | 44 / 60 | 158 / 75 |
| Scarce funding | 48 / 107 | 91 / 51 | 77 / 107 | 379 / 211 |
| Different initial encounters | 84 / 107 | 91 / 18 | 85 / 107 | 379 / 190 |

Direction errors are direction-basis ticks, not counts of wrong answers. Change
errors are source-difference ticks. Coverage excludes unresolved observations
and final outstanding promises. The coarse-to-fine one-tick step is a supplied
forecasting hypothesis, not inferred source magnitude. Improvement over a zero
baseline does not establish improvement over a well-chosen persistent trend
predictor or an optimized allocation of these same supplied offers.

The main run issued 172 paid services: 168 were evaluated, two met an unknown
immediate observation, and two remained pending at the end. On the evaluated
paid set there were 145 positive, 15 zero and eight negative relief outcomes.
Native accounting preserved all 4,096 resource ticks on every frame in every
trial. Final balances include unspent reservoir, wallets, reserves and the
explicit spent sink; interpreting a successful read never minted currency.

An independent read-only reload of all four final native worlds confirmed the
same balances, resolved conservation and direct shared live-port references.
The focused experiment checks passed all 16 groups; shared-library verification
passed all 1,752 tests in `Gojo.slnx`.

## What the market did and did not select

Initial asks `[3,5,5,7]` became `[3,4,2,3]` in the main run. Paid contacts
produced five counteroffers, and 33 contacts met occupied provider capacity.
The richer provider's two ports cannot each claim an independent service slot
or resource wallet.

The inefficient duplicate did not disappear. All three provider reserves
reached their 32-tick cap, with surplus returned to the reservoir. This is not
evidence of diversity being optimally valued: there is no retention rent or
population growth, and the supplied consumer subsidies continue supporting
experimentation. The fixed population provides the occupancy bound.

The cheap direction provider retained its ask of three even though its operating
floor is one. Buyers could sometimes obtain richer information for less after
other asks fell. The current policy only reduces asks on particular rejected
contacts or harmful outcomes; it does not compare prices continuously or raise
them when a useful provider is oversubscribed.

The forced local challenge after four services can replace a profitable
incumbent with another service that is merely better than doing nothing.
Continuation is not yet based on marginal improvement over the incumbent.
Initial encounter order consequently matters: the same input produced fine
error 222 versus 190 with different starting cursors. This is a limitation of
the supplied local search, not proof of a stable optimum or an ordering bug.

## The clearest failure: paying for unnecessary exploration

In the main trial's 16 flat-input arrivals, consumers paid for 32 contacts and
16 services. Consumer outlay was 80 resource ticks; 64 ticks reached the spent
sink through contact and operating costs. Evaluated services issued during this
phase had total calibrated gross benefit -4. The loss came from an extrapolated
forecast of +5 issued at the first flat observation, followed by no change.
The other 15 services supplied no benefit over the zero baseline, including
the last flat-period promise whose next outcome was the returning rise.

The controls also resumed wasteful exploration once normal demand and funding
returned for the flat phase.
Across the entire scarce-funding run, paid service issues fell from 172 to 128,
but contacts rose from 112 to 145. The system spent more of its smaller available
intake looking for another offer. Fixed exploratory willingness and cyclic
search give it no learned reason to pause that search.

This separates resource accounting from attention. Accounting works; effective
pressure to stop unnecessary work is not established. Tightening a budget alone
can redirect spending into repeated failed contacts rather than useful service.

## Structure and implementation cost

Source equations, offered recipes and physical publication identities persist.
Focused checks exclude earlier world envelopes, earlier frames and promises
older than the immediately preceding admission from the final reachable graph.
Each consumer retains only its current pending service, with the latest frame
holding the previous outcome and current decision.

The final worlds contained 694–738 carriers and 7,901–8,344 relations. Whole-run
temporary allocation was approximately 254–294 MB for 110 arrivals. These are
cumulative process allocation readings around admissions, not simultaneous
live memory or controlled CPU benchmarks. The financial arithmetic, current
claims and transfer witnesses are still constructed at admission. Reusable
source graphs do not mean every part of this new experiment is allocation-light.

Only selected full native witnesses and final worlds were saved. The initial
18 result files total approximately 3.8 MiB, before optional rendered images.
The four JSON replays are observer projections, never learner history. The
visual demo replays those records and performs no selection, prediction or
payment logic of its own.

## Recommended next small change

Before population growth, make exploration respond to a current unmet
obligation, changed source evidence or a relevant changed offer. Preserve a
small explicit allowance for genuinely new possibilities; do not require every
new candidate to have earned a history already. Test whether a flat period can
become quiet and whether the returning pattern wakes useful computation.

Then compare an alternative against the consumer's existing service, including
its contact and switching costs, rather than only against zero. Where two
consumers contend for one provider, a bounded joint quote comparison would test
the proposed auction idea without building a global auction system.

Both are experimental policies to express in connected Gojo structure. No
shared-core primitive was changed in this pass: existing equations, exposed
ports, live publication, claims, material transfer and attachment lifecycle
were sufficient for this bounded experiment. Autonomous interface discovery,
quality adaptation, growth, merging and economical dynamic execution remain
unproven next capabilities, not implied by this result.
