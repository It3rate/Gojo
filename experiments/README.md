# Experiments

This folder holds source snapshots that are useful while exploring Gojo integration,
but are not Gojo kernel code.

## Conceptual scratchpads

[ConceptScratchpad](ConceptScratchpad/README.md) explores qualitative comparison,
capacity, resolution, incomplete reads, and executable preservation.
[PosterScratchpad](PosterScratchpad/README.md) generates native Gojo/GojoCAD
designs, then records subjective judgments and performs contextual comparisons.
The host authors interpretations and invokes reads; neither experiment
demonstrates autonomous language understanding or learned aesthetic judgment.

[PoetryScratchpad](PoetryScratchpad/README.md) uses the supplied *Snagged* and
*Upon leaving the clockworks* for six connected examples: open referents,
contextual faces, partial metaphor, repetition, live cross-level reads, and
allusion. It records both useful native behavior and unresolved capability gaps.

[StreamingScratchpad](StreamingScratchpad/README.md) probes live represented and
remainder channels, pooled tension routing, and receiver capacity. It documents
the remaining communication work and a staged light/heat prediction experiment;
it does not claim autonomous model discovery.

## Next thinking experiments: protocol fixed before encoding

Designed on 2026-10-01. These four trials have **not been run**. Their purpose is
to discover what Gojo helps us express and revise, where it adds friction, and
where its current structures cannot support the reasoning. No preferred answer
or numeric success target is specified. Freeze each prompt and its staged update
before constructing its graph; do not tune the prompt after seeing the result.

### Common working agreement

- Use connected Gojo carriers, relations, unit shapes, and read surfaces as the
  working representation. Prose labels document those connections; they cannot
  stand in for a missing relation, implication, or calculation.
- The host may introduce a stated interpretation, construct candidate structure,
  choose a read, enumerate supplied alternatives, and inspect or render results.
  Record these interventions as supplied assumptions or proposals. Domain
  arithmetic, comparison, constraint propagation, candidate evaluation, and
  conclusions must have native Gojo support. Do not solve outside Gojo and attach
  an explanatory graph afterward. An unsupported step stays unsupported.
- Start with the ordinary-language question, not a convenient equation. There
  is no requirement to turn every judgment into a score. Keep incompatible
  perspectives and alternative explanations available without forcing a winner.
- Retain the original numerator and denominator, paired face, and signed motion
  wherever numbers are introduced. Record what the denominator measures.
  Resolution, uncertainty or variation, known coverage, and observer confidence
  are different contexts. A finer display is not new evidence; do not invent
  likelihoods or claim confidence from grain alone.
- State the scope and exclusions of each read. Intentionally irrelevant detail
  may be omitted locally. If a later question needs it, leave that read open or
  obtain new input; do not reconstruct discarded evidence by guessing.
- Keep the first attempt, then introduce the stated update to the same connected
  situation and re-read it. Prefer live source connections over answer tables.
  Retained earlier answers are dated observations, never authoritative inputs
  to the current calculation merely because they were saved.
- Allow 20 minutes for the first modeling pass, then 10 for the update and
  inspection. This is a work limit, not a correctness threshold. Record where
  work stopped. Changes to the kernel belong in a separately identified follow-up
  after recording the first result; do not silently improve the tool mid-trial.

### 1. Sharing a community hall

**Initial prompt.** A neighborhood has a main hall with room for 45 people and
a side room with room for 12. They share one entrance. On Friday from 17:00 to
21:00, a repair group wants two uninterrupted hours and expects 10-20 visitors;
a children's craft group wants 90 minutes and expects 15-25; a reading group
wants a quiet hour for about eight people. Furniture can move, but changing
room use takes roughly 15-30 minutes. Two volunteers are available initially;
after 19:00 only one can stay. No staffing minimums or noise-transfer measurements
are supplied. Suggest a workable arrangement, explain whose needs it serves
poorly, and identify what should be checked before advertising it. More than one
arrangement is acceptable, including a conditional or incomplete proposal.

**Later update.** The divider does not block repair noise, and the craft
organizer says several families can arrive only after 18:30. Reconsider the
arrangement without treating the first proposal as a commitment.

**What to learn.** Can practical alternatives and stakeholder needs remain
connected without collapsing everything into a weighted sum? Can we separate
capacity, timing, uncertain demand, preferences, and missing staffing policy?
Record any scheduling insight supplied by the host instead of derived by Gojo.
An infeasible or conditional result is useful if the reason is inspectable.

### 2. Why are the greenhouse plants drooping?

**Initial prompt.** Six pots share a greenhouse bench. Four droop around noon
and look better by evening; two appear unchanged. All soil feels damp at the
surface. A fan was moved yesterday. The gardener also changed the watering time
this week, and the pots came from two deliveries. There are no temperature logs,
root inspections, or delivery-to-pot records. Develop plausible explanations
and choose one observation to make next. Explain what different outcomes of
that observation would and would not tell us. Do not assume a single cause.

**Later update.** A simple shade screen reduces the noon drooping in two of
the four affected pots. The other two still droop. The screen also changes air
movement; no other measurements were taken.

**What to learn.** Can observed co-occurrence stay distinct from proposed causal
direction? Can Gojo compare what an observation would distinguish without
inventing probabilities or equating consistency with truth? The host may propose
mechanisms as hypotheses; supplying a causal link is not discovering it.
Record where native support for intervention or explanatory comparison is absent.

### 3. Reading a small poem from different perspectives

**Initial prompt.** Read this invented text: "We left the gate unlatched. / By
morning, nothing had returned." Explore two or more defensible interpretations
of the speaker's attitude. Explain what in the text supports each and what
must be supplied by the reader. Consider welcome, carelessness, loss, relief,
or an interpretation not listed here. Do not choose an emotional reading merely
because it has more supporting annotations.

**Later update.** An editor says the lines appear immediately after an account
of a storm. Revisit the interpretations, then consider a different edition where
they follow an account of an argument. These are alternative contexts, not two
facts to merge into a single story.

**What to learn.** Can mutually plausible meanings share literal evidence while
keeping different contextual support? Does pairing welcome/closure or hope/fear
help, or distort the interpretation? Which implications actually execute in Gojo,
and which remain supplied literary associations? A connected notebook that
cannot perform the requested inference is a representational result, not proof
of interpretive reasoning.

### 4. A library as a garden or a railway station

**Initial prompt.** A small library wants first-time visitors to feel welcome,
regular visitors to find things quickly, and people to discover unfamiliar work.
Staff suggest two metaphors: "a garden" and "a railway station." Use each to
propose one concrete change to the same library. Explain a useful transfer from
the metaphor and a place where that transfer breaks. Compare the proposals from
the perspectives of a hurried visitor, an unhurried browser, and a staff member.
Additional proposals or an unresolved comparison are welcome.

**Later update.** A visitor uses a wheelchair and finds the proposed route
confusing; the staff cannot move shelves this month. Revisit both proposals.
No floor plan, route widths, budget, or measured accessibility results are given.

**What to learn.** Can an analogy carry a limited relationship across contexts
without importing all source properties? Can candidate generation be separated
honestly from Gojo evaluation? Can unmeasured physical constraints remain open
instead of being assigned convenient scores? This trial may reveal that present
native comparison is much stronger than native analogy or invention.

### Record after each trial

Keep the original prompt, initial graph, attempted reads, first interpretation,
update, and revised graph or readings. Report:

1. What was expressible and what conclusion, if any, the native reads support.
2. Which assumptions, candidates, and causal or metaphorical links the host
   supplied; what the graph did beyond restating them.
3. An unresolved alternative, missing observation, or failed read that mattered.
4. How the answer changed under the update, with the responsible path visible.
5. Where Gojo helped and where it imposed awkward structure or misleading
   precision. Include reachable graph size, read time, and modeling effort as
   observations, not a combined performance score.
6. One concrete improvement suggested by the attempt, including the possibility
   that better local inspection or less encoding is preferable to a new kernel
   capability.

After the natural reasoning pass, perform a separate preservation check: save
and reload the circuit, read it, change a connected input, and read again. Keep
this engineering check separate from the conceptual outcome; a perfect round
trip does not establish good reasoning. Test artifact checks against relation
structure, not equality with a preselected natural-language answer.

## ScratchMill

`ScratchMill` is a vendored local fork of
<https://github.com/It3rate/ScratchMill>. It is copied into this repository so
the KiCad-to-G-code pipeline can be converted experimentally without changing the
upstream project.

The current goal is to keep the real KiCad parsing and real G-code generation
available while moving the intermediate representation toward Gojo carriers,
relations, unit shapes, and inspectable tension.

The active build surface is intentionally pruned to:

- `KiCad/**` for parsing `.kicad_pcb` files.
- `GojoImport/KiCadGojoImporter.cs` for converting parsed board geometry into
  Gojo carrier/relation structure.
- `Pipeline/ScratchMillPipeline.cs` for the current parse/import/toolpath bridge.
- `GCode/**` and small `Utils/**` helpers for preserving real G-code output.

The old UI, GRBL adapter, and deleted Numbers/Foom references are not part of
the active ScratchMill experiment build. They can remain as source/reference
material where useful, but they should not own new Gojo meaning.

## Foom

`Foom` was initially vendored only because the original ScratchMill project
referenced it. The pruned ScratchMill experiment no longer references those
projects.

## Solution

Use `Gojo.ScratchMill.slnx` when working on this experiment. The normal
`Gojo.slnx` remains focused on the Gojo kernel and existing Gojo projects.
