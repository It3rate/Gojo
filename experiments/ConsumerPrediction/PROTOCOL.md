# Consumer-specific prospective usefulness: frozen first protocol

This is an exploratory experiment, not a new kernel learning law. It tests
whether one connected consumer can distinguish help, harm, redundancy, absent
opportunity, absent demand, and an unresolved outcome. Do not tune the protocol
to obtain a win after seeing the results.

## Native shape and retained state

```text
three current input sites + presence spans
  -> persistent difference/product equations
  -> observed turn claim
  -> preissued next-change baseline and candidate
  -> same later observation
  -> paired signed errors on the consumer's basis
  -> interval-response witness
  -> calibrated signed support change
  -> next-issue connection participation
```

Keep one current three-level trace, mutable sites, bounded support and one
outstanding issue. No training table, successful-value archive or replay lookup
may feed the predictor. Recompute current predictions through persistent native
equations. Retain the issued numeric prediction (or sufficient frozen premises)
until its one subsequent outcome so later adaptation cannot rewrite it.
Observer CSV/report history must remain outside the learner. Save only selected
native witnesses/final worlds, not a full native graph for every arrival.

Input coordinates can be structurally retargeted. Changes to support and live
publication enter through native arithmetic and tension admission. All arithmetic
and claims that decide learning must use Gojo structures; code schedules and
traverses the supplied experimental structure. Display names and generated
waveform phase labels never enter the learner.

## Candidates and consumer

From current levels compute the most recent delta d and its predecessor a.
The baseline predicts the next delta as d. An observed turn is the native
opposite-sign claim a*d <= -1; its candidate forecast is issued only for the
following observation, not the already observed turn.

Compare these supplied structures in separate matched runs:

1. Redundant turn-then-continue: after a turn, propose d. This must add no
   predictive benefit over the baseline.
2. Turn with restoration of previous scale: after a turn, propose -a. This
   hypothesis may help after a large transitional step followed by a run at the
   previous scale; it may harm when the new, larger slope persists. It is not a
   learned primitive or a forecast of when a turn will happen.

The consumer wants the next change, with an error interval fixed before the
outcome: initially [0,0], with a paired tolerance control [-4,4]. This interval
is a chosen tolerance, not a measurement confidence interval. For one later
actual change y, use the SAME interval to compare errors d-y and candidate-y.
The signed response is demand_without - demand_with. The candidate's actual
contribution is candidate-d in a native sum; keep that transition connected so
the two errors differ only by the proposed contribution.

Also inspect direction agreement and absolute-scale error separately using
native claims. They do not alter this first consumer's reinforcement rule.
An inverse forecast may preserve scale without satisfying a directional need.

Issue readiness/turn eligibility, current external consumer demand, actual route
participation, and later evaluability must remain separately inspectable. A
forecast on an eligible frame with no improvement is distinct from no eligible
frame; missing data is not a numeric zero utility. Demand is supplied externally
for this experiment, not manufactured by a reader being wrong.

## Response and bounded exploratory participation

Initially the candidate route is detached and support is zero. Compute a
prospective shadow candidate on eligible demanded issues, so a bounded probe
can be assessed without pretending it was already delivered. An evaluable
shadow improvement can seed participation for a later issue. Report shadow
utility separately from delivered utility.

Map one signed error-reduction tick to one support tick through an explicit
native calibration. Clamp support to [0,8]; positive support attaches the
retained candidate publication, zero support withdraws it. Clear pulses before
changing attachment. Support changes only on demanded, eligible, evaluable
issued opportunities. Harm is a negative change even if the baseline already
satisfied its obligation. Zero reduction neither strengthens nor weakens.
There is no elapsed-arrival fade in this pass.

This rule intentionally tests opportunity-qualified response. It does not yet
price maintenance, exploration, or computation and must not be advertised as
the complete memory/recalculation pressure. Accurate unused structures can
retain ability here; future capacity pressure may put them dormant or forget
them without declaring their predictions wrong.

## Fixed inputs and controls

- Ordinary zigzag: repeated +2 runs of four changes followed by -2 runs of four,
  16 cycles. Both candidate variants should expose redundant cases naturally.
- Short episodes with a transitional turn: levels [0,2,4,-2,-4,-6], repeated
  eight times with alternating mirrored sign and an explicit unknown separator.
- Adverse episodes where the new slope persists: levels [0,2,4,-2,-8,-14], with
  the same mirrored/separator treatment. Run eight helpful episodes, then eight
  adverse episodes, then eight helpful episodes without resetting support.
- Compare zero versus 24 additional flat known observations after each episode
  has settled and before its unknown separator. Preserve the ordered eligible
  cases; longer silence should not create predictive failures or extra credit.
- A fixed-seed shuffled-increment control for the continuous ordinary zigzag,
  preserving its increment multiset while changing temporal order.
- Repeat the mixed episode trial with tolerance four, and with external demand
  withdrawn through the middle block and restored for the final block.

Unknown separators deliberately reset short input readiness while retaining
ability/support. They are disclosed experimental boundaries, not learned event
segmentation. The learner sees only observations, their order and its exposed
consumer demand/tolerance; no episode names, phase labels or future samples.

## Evidence required

Report baseline/candidate/actually delivered demand, signed marginal response,
direction and scale reads, eligible/no-demand/unresolved counts, support and
attachment changes. Show both successful and damaging cases, and compare the
complete ordered eligible support/response traces for quiet-gap controls.

Focused checks must prove pre-outcome issuance, no hindsight update, same-event
ablation, delayed recruitment, real attach/withdraw behavior, native arithmetic,
unknown versus zero input, no support change from repeated reads, bounded live
state, persistent equation identity, and no access to observer history.
Do not turn an expected experimental outcome into a required pass condition:
failures of the supplied learning hypothesis belong in the report.
