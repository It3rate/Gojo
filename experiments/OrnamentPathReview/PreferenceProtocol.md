# Can a small judge predict which path someone would keep?

The first tournament favored several thin courses with excellent geometric scores. We have twenty pairs of line drawings chosen before the first blind assay and two sealed visual ballots. The observers saw anonymous drawings and design briefs. Both are AI observers from the same model family. At the time of this registration the votes have been committed to files and their contents have not been joined to machine identities.

We will compare the full brief's fixed score against the observers' side preferences, including ties and disagreements. Keep every vote. For each of the six perspectives compute the mean of its criteria (missing reads contribute zero). Each per-pair feature is left minus right. Fit six coefficients by ridge least squares to the mean vote, coding left as +1, right as −1 and tie as 0. Use a fixed penalty of 0.25, no intercept, and no scaling or hyperparameter tuning. Leave all pairs of one brief out together during the exploratory cross-validation. Use sign as the prediction, with absolute values below 0.000001 tied. For each model report exact agreement on cases with an agreed non-tied direction separately from ties and disagreements. The volunteer gallery ballot remains separate.

Then train one model on all twenty pairs and lock its coefficients. Preselect a second panel of twenty fresh paired drawings **before opening reserve outputs**. One family per brief/replicate uses the untouched reserve calibration at index (3 × brief index + replicate) mod 12. The full finalist will face a previously unseen finalist pairing: craft+field at replicates 0 and 2, seam+rhythm at 1 and 3. Use the second realization of that input, and independently shuffle the sides. These four search replicates reuse some visual priors, so the twenty trials are not independent art traditions.

Freeze the panel specification, the finalists and the judge before viewing the reserve. Predict that the fitted judge agrees with a majority of unanimous non-tied reserve choices and correctly ranks at least two more such pairs than the registered full brief does. Require at least eight unanimous non-tied pairs to issue a directional verdict. Fewer than eight or a margin of one pair either way is inconclusive. A deficit of at least two, or a strict minority correct, is contrary. Never update weights on this panel. Compare observer agreement and visually inspect failures before recommending any reward.

This judge is a training-side experiment. Neither model nor drawing score accompanies a saved generator. Successful prediction of these model observers' choices would support a particular visual read. It would leave preference among human viewers open.
