One connection asks two paths to have a particular gap. Another asks their far endpoints to line up. Each can change only one extent of the lower path. Can those small responses cooperate without being given the finished shape?

The two connections, opened up

Each connection reads its own relation, chooses a coarse response, and checks for an earlier pending adjustment. The actuator receives a signed step only when its response is admitted.

Gap claim → height extentMove the selected lower section toward its requested separation.
Endpoint claim → width extentMove the lower endpoint toward agreement on the selected axis.

001 offers a decrease, 010 equality, and 100 an increase. 000 is an open read; 111 retains alternatives. At the gate output, 010 means hold, which does not assert measured equality. “No command” differs from a known zero-step command. The displayed tensions belong to these two extents, not every carrier in the graph.

What this does—and what remains open

Two local responses can share a construction

  • The selected path words and endpoint ports carry the measured geometry.
  • A supplied correspondence explains how one extent step moves the observed part.
  • A finite response law turns comparison support into an allowed signed adjustment.
  • The construction changes as the extents settle; the claims inspect it again.

A useful limit is still a result

  • A desired gap may lie between the construction’s available steps.
  • Capacity or loss of section overlap can prevent a claim from being satisfied.
  • Uncertain or missing evidence does not authorize a guessed correction.
  • This does not discover new paths, choose new controls, or supply a general shape interpolator.
