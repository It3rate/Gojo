Construction · subdivision · enrichment

The detail serves the design.

Jones's hierarchy of construction and enrichment guides these plates: keep a clear distant arrangement, give the repeated unit its own character, and let finer ornament reward a closer view. Repeated prototypes, geometric conventionalisation, stems carrying leaves, balanced empty spaces and separating outlines give us a small reusable design vocabulary. Strong colour is concentrated in selected roles; other levels provide quieter support.

Each of the three versions is a separate native enrichment program. They share their source skeleton and retain its repetition, public landmarks and seam obligations. The added rosettes, leaves, insets and veins are finite Gojo paths, attached through actual public ports and retained offset witnesses. Colour, stroke width, edging, filled contours and the rectangular crop are display choices. Curved paths are an optional presentation over actual native guides, with fixed ends, gap ends and selected connection landmarks. No native curvature, occupied face or automatic beauty judgement is claimed.
