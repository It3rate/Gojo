How many ideas make a motif?

A construction review of the ornamental vocabulary in one repeating unit. Reuse matters: eight copies of a petal can be one shape plus an arrangement rule. A single outline can also contain several meaningful substructures. The ranges below keep that ambiguity visible.

Refined galleryAll designsAll native records · JSONMethod and limits
Denominator: one unit's reusable ornamental construction vocabulary. The count includes proposed shape families, internal articulation, symmetry/repetition and attachment rules. Repeated instances do not each add a concept. The underlying lattice, palette and renderer's fillets are outside this motif estimate. These are authored ranges, not measured cognitive load. Field and intermediate variants have native statistics in the JSON, with their conceptual estimates left unreviewed.

12 is a review trigger. A range extending above 12 asks whether a simpler grouping or a more coherent hierarchy would help. It is neither a mathematical limit nor an automatic aesthetic failure. A larger motif can remain simple; a small vocabulary can still look crowded if placement, colour or layering is poor.

The separate encoding column comes from reopening each exported native packet and reading its actual enrichment/prototype correspondences. Counts are per unit and agree with the saved execution records. “Prototype identities” means retained identity sharing, not inferred similarity or discovered human concepts. Unknown ancestry remains unattributed.

From declared groupings to discovered substructure
