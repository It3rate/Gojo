Two claims, one construction

What this does, in plain language

The previous experiment could make the spacing between two paths correct while leaving their endpoints apart. This one connects two small controls to the same shape. One changes the lower path's height to get the requested gap. The other changes its width to bring the far endpoints together.

Neither control is told the finished width or given a target outline. Each receives only a coarse correction from its own observation. The interesting part is that changing height can also move an endpoint: one control changes the problem that the other control sees.

Both controls settled the six ordinary starting cases. They also exposed three distinct limits: insufficient capacity, a gap between available increments, and a change which removes the section needed by the gap reader. The last two are particularly useful directions for further work.

The endpoint law has six rows: three discrepancy signs and two permitted motion polarities. The input support can be 001, 010, 100, open 000, or a set such as 111. These are alternatives, not a magnitude and not three simultaneous flows. A 010 output from the pending gate means hold; it may be holding an unsatisfied request. It does not prove measured equality.

Observed geometric discrepancy is claim residue. It is not automatically material tension. Only an admitted actuator response introduces a signed adjustment, and each actuator accounts independently for:

introduced adjustment = visible extent change + change in retained tension

Neither actuator reads the other's private state. The claims observe their advertised path surfaces, and the next construction read observes both extent ports. This is a small example of coordination through shared consequences.

Inset cap Moves selected section by three reaches per height step and also moves endpoint Moves endpoint horizontally Repeated role uses change both response magnitude and reachable gaps

These consequences are present in the native phase construction. The local response correspondences are supplied, not inferred: one positive height step moves the observed section downward by one shared reach for cardinal and chevron, and by three for the inset. The controls receive only direction support, so the stronger inset effect can step over a goal.

An after-the-fact algebraic check explains the reachable chevron example: upper endpoint is at 8 + 2×2 = 12; gap 6 requires lower height 4; the lower endpoint is width + 2×4, so width 4 would match. This equation is an audit of the result. It is not an input or a solver used by the control loop.

In total, 57 of 176 runs ended in joint agreement without held extent tension. The gap-only success is the cardinal starting case whose endpoint already agrees. The two successes in the larger-gap probe are also cardinal cases: their height change does not move the endpoint horizontally. Keeping that contrast is more useful than making every difficult condition fail.

The diagnostics can observe agreement while a control's message is withheld. That establishes a property of the recorded geometry, not that the receiver has learned or certified that property. A first observed match also does not by itself prove convergence beyond the recorded horizon.

The failures teach different things

Capacity leaves debt. With the chevron width limited to 3, the gap reaches 6 but the lower endpoint stays one reach short. One positive width adjustment remains pending. The gate holds repeated requests; it does not relabel the endpoint as equal. Limiting height instead leaves a positive height debt and an unmet gap even after width brings the endpoints together.

Resolution causes continuing correction. For the inset's requested gap 10, the simultaneous controls alternate late in the run between gap 9 with width/height 5/2 and gap 12 with width/height 6/3. The endpoints alternate between one reach short and two reaches too far. Neither extent carries debt: every step is legal, but the goal lies between available readings. This is different from a blocked actuator and would need a different remedy.

A response can remove an observation's premise. In the large-gap chevron case, width reaches zero. Its selected horizontal section is then zero length, so this gap reader cannot measure the requested relation. The recorded gap becomes open, not zero. The endpoint is still four reaches too far right and the width actuator holds a negative adjustment it cannot absorb. The inset case differs: repeated height uses keep its selected section nonzero even when the width role is zero. Its gap can remain measurable at 24 while its endpoint still fails. The role value alone cannot determine observability; the connected construction matters.

Saved structure now supports the next read

The preceding pass exported and decoded geometric graphs but still needed the original in-memory frame wrapper for an executable geometric read. This pass adds exact frame rebinds. They inspect the decoded two-axis grammar and retain its actual directions, opposite pairs, cancellation/exchange laws and shared reach. They do not invent fresh axes and substitute them for the old ones.

A composed test saves both claims, both actuator archives, their gates and the observed geometry in one graph. After decoding, both claims read the same differences and drive the decoded actuators. The original world is unchanged. Malformed or incomplete frame structures are rejected. Reboxed locations retain shared tick identity; merely equal coordinates do not become aliases.

This is executable continuation of retained observations and local controls. It is not yet an archive of the whole experiment scheduler or a persistent material geometry engine.

The selected full witness archives are much larger: 1,344,219 bytes for the reachable chevron, 2,323,183 for the off-grid inset, and 1,350,525 for the incompatible chevron. They retain repeated native observations and histories. Those byte counts should not be confused with the size of a minimal reusable machine. What remains supplied, and what comes next

We supply the phase constructions, choice of section/endpoint, response correspondences, goals, capacities, native finite-law rows and pulse schedule. These mechanisms do not choose where to attach themselves, learn their own motion effects, or judge ornament quality. Each path is a new read of the role-bound construction, rather than a persistent physical path deforming. The renderer receives recorded points and contributes no control decisions.

The most concrete next experiments are:

  1. Retain a bounded alternative when a read fails. A zero section can leave an endpoint relation or a broader envelope available. Switching observation must be an explicit connected choice, with the failed claim still visible.
  2. Refine only where the legal steps cannot settle. The inset gap conflict is a small place to test a finer rung or tolerance read, preserving the original coarse evidence rather than quietly changing the goal.
  3. Observe response correspondence. Compare a published extent adjustment with the resulting port motion, then retain competing local effect claims. That would begin replacing the supplied motion word with learned evidence.

These are tests of local mechanisms. They would move us closer to a useful interpolation system while making the necessary guidance and remaining uncertainty inspectable.
