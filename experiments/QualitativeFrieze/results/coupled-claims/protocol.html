Two geometric claims: prospective protocol

What this experiment is for

The previous experiment could make the gap between two paths correct while leaving their endpoints apart. We will give the lower path two small controls: one changes its height to regulate the gap; the other changes its width to bring the endpoints into agreement. Neither receives a completed target shape.

This protocol is written before the campaign. The constructions, connections, response laws and conditions are supplied. This tests their composition, not whether Gojo has discovered those connections or learned ornament quality.

Connected mechanism

upper/lower section ports -> gap comparison -> pending gate -> height extent
upper/lower endpoint ports -> axis comparison -> pending gate -> width extent
height/width exposed ports -> captured publications -> next native path read

Both claims observe the same before-state. Each publishes only decrease, hold or increase support to its own actuator. An explicit motion correspondence connects a positive actuator step to its geometric effect. Observations do not transfer material; an admitted actuator response introduces signed tension. The actuator clamp absorbs it or retains the unaccepted part.

The endpoint claim compares along a selected cardinal axis and separately retains the transverse difference. Axis agreement is not full coordinate agreement, and neither creates shared identity, a material weld or a flow law.

Conditions and predictions

Three supplied constructions use the same controls: a cardinal cap, a diagonal chevron cap, and a seven-phase inset cap. Each has an upper route and a reflected lower route. We vary the initial lower width/height and compare gap-only, endpoint-only, simultaneous and alternating response schedules.

  • Reachable: both controls should be needed for general simultaneous agreement. The cardinal construction separates the roles; in the other two, changing height also moves the endpoint, so width has to compensate.
  • Height capacity: the gap request should remain unmet with held tension.
  • Width capacity: endpoint agreement should fail when the permitted width is insufficient, even if the gap settles.
  • Missing gap / missing endpoint: withhold that observation for beats 1–8. The corresponding control must not treat absence as equality or a command.
  • Ambiguous endpoint: offer all three response alternatives for beats 1–8. No single direction should be invented.
  • Incompatible demand: increase the requested gap so that the chevron and inset would require a negative lower width to meet both claims. They should retain a failure or open read; the cardinal construction may still succeed.
  • Off-grid inset: request gap 10 when one height step moves the selected section by three shared reaches. Expect continuing correction or another explicit failure, never a fabricated exact gap of 10.

Each condition runs for 36 supplied beats. Two initial states and four response modes give 176 recorded runs. A finite horizon is not a convergence proof. Alternating schedules receive fewer responses per wall beat; report both timing and actual admitted changes rather than declaring one universally faster.

What must stay visible

Record both extents and their held tensions, each claim's input support, the gate's output support, requested and accepted deltas, native route reads, gap and endpoint residuals. A response of zero can mean a gate held a request; it must not be relabeled as measured equality. A zero width can remove the nonzero section needed for a gap read; that is unavailable evidence, not gap 0.

The displayed geometry and after-state diagnostics are observations. They must not feed a renderer's coordinates or an analytic target width into either actuator. Native and compiled finite-law execution must agree. Save selected native graphs and verify decoding. Separately verify that a decoded frame and claim can resume actual observation and response, retaining their aliases.

This remains small integral extent control with unary shared-reach drawing words, not continuous interpolation or a change to the primitive participation bit. Each displayed path is a new observation of the same role-bound construction; moving persistent material vertices is outside this pass.
