What can a machine become?

A square is one result. Its construction tells us which changes are easy, which are forbidden, and which need a different machine. Here we compare whole finite ranges of outputs, then examine repetition, feedback and neighboring constructions.

Three different questions: What outputs does this machine admit? What happens when a transform reuses a different source? What must change to reach a nearby shape? Similar pictures do not give the same answer to all three.

Why the corner repeat may not add rectangles

A four-step walk with independent horizontal and vertical reaches already permits rectangles of different aspect ratios. Rewriting those steps as a corner plus its half-turn changes the program and its correspondence. It need not enlarge the geometric range.

The stronger tied-square machine has an additional equality. Releasing that equality really does admit new bindings.

Why a hidden notch is still structure

A notch whose reach reads zero can retain extra sites and phases. Giving the retained notch a nonzero reach makes the detour visible. A four-step program without those phases cannot acquire them merely by changing a width.

A participating zero means zero at this resolution. It does not delete the section or establish absolute absence.

The action is only half of repetition

A mirror or translation must act on something. Reusing the original, reusing the previous result and reusing the whole accumulated group are different connected machines.

These are copied observations, not conserved material fanout. A reported phase step is one placement slot in the supplied rule, not necessarily one native reach or one drawn section. An orange ×n label marks n coincident section drawings in the same direction; it does not merge their identities.

Order and phase: compare the recorded outputs

These comparisons use explicit correspondences. A finite crop has a beginning and an end; a phase shift may change those boundaries even when the interior has a recurring pattern.

Phase is a declared read. The cyclic case checks the actual slots, placement action, period and inverse wrap, together with an explicit permission to read modulo that period. Forward continuation alone does not grant this permission. The matching correspondence does not make finite endpoints disappear or prove all possible continuations.

“Nearby” can mean several different edits

A rectangle, a rotated square, a rhombus and a pentagon are nearby pictures. Reaching them can require a released binding, a geometric word substitution, a different connection pattern, or a different boundary construction. The substitutions here actively create transformed copies; they are not merely observer changes.

Try the two orders: stretch an axis-aligned square, then apply the diagonal substitution; compare that with applying the diagonal substitution first, then stretching horizontally. One can preserve perpendicular joins, the other equal section lengths. The two machines retain different promises. The diagonal map includes a √2 scale as well as a 45-degree turn.

Read the property names narrowly: equal section lengths need not mean equal maximal sides. Perpendicular joins include concave 270-degree interior corners as well as convex 90-degree corners; this read does not certify convexity.

    What the results do—and do not—establish

    The candidate programs, finite bindings, action schedules and comparison correspondences are supplied. The native structures execute and retain evidence; this page only renders their recorded reads. No generator was learned from the pictures, and no observed source is automatically rewritten.

    Boundary equality, topology, flow, timing, history and aesthetic quality remain different questions. Directed line agreement does not establish a common stroke order. Equal screen coordinates do not establish a shared site. A better hypothesis grants no permission to mutate the source.

    The next useful evidence would come from testing predictions beyond this input envelope, finding a correspondence under an additional contract, or learning which machine change is useful in context. None of those future behaviors follows merely from a similar outline.
