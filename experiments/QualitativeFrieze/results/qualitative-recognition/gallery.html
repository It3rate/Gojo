GOJŌ · HISTORY-BLIND QUALITATIVE RECOGNITION

Reading a field
without its recipe

The observer receives only fresh sites, connected segments, coarse headings and explicit join / crossing witnesses. Construction faces, names, positions, extents and source history are withheld. A local reader induces motifs; another reader tests larger flow and arrangement questions.

Several readings can be true at once. A two-channel grid can explain part of a triangular field, leaving an additional direction and its circuits visible. An alternating flow can explain staggered rows more precisely than a constant direction. No single noun is chosen as the answer.
