S01Ordered rhythm

A repeated span establishes order before additional shape or detail.

S01.AOne span

A directed span connects two distinct endpoint landmarks.

S01.BRepeated chain

Corresponding spans continue through recurring landmarks.

S01.CAligned rows

Several chains add cross-row landmark correspondences.

Keep readable: Retain the ordered landmarks and their repeat correspondence; equal spacing is an additional promise.

S02Cross-lattice

Two crossing line families create cells that can be grouped or selected.

S02.ACrossing families

Two families of paths define a simple grid.

S02.BOblique view

The same incidence appears in a slanted representation.

S02.CSelected cells

Grouped cells reveal a secondary pattern on the shared scaffold.

Keep readable: Preserve the crossing incidence and cell adjacency; changing the view need not preserve angles or proportions.

S03Selected-edge fret

Selecting and interrupting lattice edges produces turns, returns and interlocking paths.

S03.AOne turn

A selected path turns on an exposed lattice.

S03.BStepped return

Repeated turns return along a distinct lane.

S03.CFacing paths

Two selections occupy one scaffold without silently merging their identities.

Keep readable: Retain path order and edge participation; a return on another level differs from retracing the same edge.

S04Staggered repeat

Neighbouring rows relate through an alternating placement phase.

S04.AAligned repeat

Identical units recur in aligned rows.

S04.BAlternating phase

Alternate rows shift through a declared half-period correspondence.

S04.CDiamond scaffold

Neighbour connections reveal the staggered field's underlying skeleton.

Keep readable: A claimed half-drop needs a shared period: two half-shifts correspond to that whole period.

S05Triangular compounds

Shared triangular cells combine into lozenges, hexagons and stars.

S05.ATriangular cell

Three edges close one cell with exposed shared-edge roles.

S05.BCompound centre

Six triangular cells meet around one common centre.

S05.CSelected field

Select hexagons and lozenges from a shared triangular scaffold.

Keep readable: Retain shared edges and compound-cell correspondence; equilateral lengths and turns require explicit additional promises.

S06Mixed-cell network

Different boundary cycles cooperate to close the intervening field.

S06.AOne octagon

An eight-edge boundary encloses one cell.

S06.BIntervening cells

Neighbouring octagons expose four-sided gaps that also need boundaries.

S06.CClosed mixed field

Octagons and squares complete a repeated adjacency network.

Keep readable: Account for the gap cells as well as the principal cells; local cycles alone do not prove a global tiling.

S07Radial partition

Rays and enclosing contours organize a field around a shared centre.

S07.AShared centre

Several rays attach to the same actual centre.

S07.BClosed sectors

A closed contour connects the rays in cyclic order.

S07.CSector bands

Nested contours cross the same radial structure to create related bands.

Keep readable: Retain the common centre, cyclic order and contour correspondence; equal angular sectors are a stronger promise.

S08Independent-thread interlace

Separate paths can cross while retaining independent continuity.

S08.ATwo threads

One ordered crossing keeps two identifiable paths separate.

S08.BCrossing order

Successive crossings expose a declared over/under sequence.

S08.CAngular woven band

The crossing sequence repeats while each thread remains traceable.

Keep readable: A projected crossing is not automatically a junction; over/under order belongs to an additional local relation.

S09Nested boundaries

An enclosing boundary can contain bands, subordinate divisions and an inner court.

S09.AOuter boundary

One closed boundary distinguishes an inside from its scoped surroundings.

S09.BInner court

A second boundary creates an intervening band and a retained centre.

S09.CSubdivided bands

Additional divisions enrich the band without erasing its principal organization.

Keep readable: Retain containment and boundary adjacency; visual nesting alone does not establish equal gaps or scaling.

S10Counterchanged partition

The occupied motif and intervening field can each support a coherent pattern reading.

S10.AStepped division

A stepped boundary divides a bounded field into two regions.

S10.BTwo coherent roles

Repetition makes both the selected region and its complement legible.

S10.CRole exchange

Exchange the roles through a supplied correspondence while retaining the partition.

Keep readable: Retain the common partition and an explicit role correspondence; equal significance does not imply equal physical area.
