# Machines in the two-claim experiment

## The simple picture

A shape is observed by two small readers. One asks for more or less separation;
the other asks for more or less horizontal reach. Each reader talks to its own
extent machine. Changing one extent changes the shape that both readers will
see next time. That shared consequence is their coupling.

There is no machine in this experiment that receives “make a stretched hexagon”
and calculates its vertices. The recognizable outline is the result of a
supplied path construction plus these local adjustments.

## What is actually present

| Machine or structure | What is connected | What it does | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Role-bound cap construction | Ordered direction uses to shared width/height roles | Expands published extents into native path words | Does not discover a shape or move persistent material vertices |
| Reflected companion | A vertical-frame action to the second path | Creates the lower read while preserving traversal order | Reflection is not reversal of path order |
| Gap claim | Two section ports, desired-gap port, selected normal, actuator port and response word | Publishes a coarse correction for separation | Does not ask the endpoints to meet |
| Endpoint claim | Two endpoint ports, selected axis, actuator port and response word | Publishes a coarse correction for endpoint projection | Agreement along one axis does not remove transverse residue |
| Pending-adjustment gate | Coarse response to the actuator's advertised pending-tension port | Holds another response in the same direction as existing debt | A held response does not mean the geometry is correct |
| Extent actuator | Sign support, finite law, signed effect outlet, unit correspondence and attached clamp | Imports a signed step; absorbs it or retains tension | Does not read a target picture or another structure's private geometry |
| Publication binding | Actual exposed extent port to a captured sample, then a role binding | Makes the next shape read correspond to actual extents | Rendering is not settlement |
| Two-control composition | Both claims see the shape produced by both extent ports | Couples the consequences of two local decisions | Does not guarantee convergence or discover how to route claims |
| Exact frame rebind | Decoded frame surface with its retained axes, opposites, laws and reach | Restores the reader for the same native grammar | Does not synthesize a fresh frame and pretend it has the old identity |

The request/acknowledgement channel and response arbiter from the previous
experiment remain available. They are not inserted here: these two claims
control different receivers, and delivery is immediate. The alternating
schedule supplies fewer actuator pulses; it is not a learned priority policy.

## Is there a rectangle or hexagon machine?

There is a **cardinal cap construction**, and there is a **chevron cap
construction**. Their identities are their connected phase structures, not
their names. With a reflected partner and endpoint agreement, the cardinal
pair outlines a rectangle; the chevron pair outlines a six-sided shape when
all required extents participate. Equal upper/lower roles would impose further
symmetry. Independent extents leave that symmetry open.

The inset construction has seven phases and uses the height role several
times. Its endpoint and selected gap are affected by those repeated uses.
This is why the same height control has a different geometric effect there.
That effect is supplied as an explicit correspondence in this experiment.

“Stretched hexagon” can therefore describe a resulting outline, but should
not be presented as a newly discovered reusable concept. To earn that stronger
claim we still need a retained definition, evidence for its allowed variations,
and a reusable surface which exposes the relevant roles and constraints.

## The endpoint claim in native form

```text
(frame -> Closed(first role, second role)) -> Closed(
  selected axis,
  extent port -> (moved role -> positive motion word),
  Closed(measure tick -> shared reach,
         actuator tick -> positive motion word),
  Closed(sensor, receiver, comparison basis, comparison domain),
  measured basis,
  polarity basis,
  finite response law,
  opaque claim/evidence identity)
```

Each observed endpoint is `site -> position word`. The reader retains
`second position − first position`, then separates the selected-axis word
from the transverse word. The first gives the correction; the second remains
read residue. It is never silently discarded or converted into a second
command.

The law has six rows: three comparison states times two response polarities.
The polarity comes from which endpoint is moved and from the positive motion
word's component along the selected axis. Reversing the observation axis does
not reverse the physical correction when the correspondence is transformed
consistently. Moving the other endpoint does reverse the needed response.

The outward publication carries comparison support, receiver contract, a fresh
opaque event identity and the retained claim/evidence identity. The event is
what prevents replay; the claim identity can recur across observations. Its
private witness retains the actual ports,
reduction, measurement, response correspondence and law result. This separates
the small communication surface from the richer explanation of its origin.

## The three comparison bits

| Support | Meaning at a claim | Meaning after the pending gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `001` | Request a decrease | Permit a decrease |
| `010` | Claim agrees in its selected read | Permit a hold; possibly because earlier debt blocks another request |
| `100` | Request an increase | Permit an increase |
| `000` | No resolved response is supplied | No resolved response is supplied |
| `111` | All three responses remain alternatives | A set of possibilities, not three commands |

These are **support bits for three alternatives**, not a new ODV element and
not a signed magnitude. The actuator has a distinct signed step. Magnitudes
in this experiment are the current kernel's small integral carriers, connected
to unary shared-reach drawing words. No exact amount is squeezed into the old
participation bit.

## Useful conceptual vocabulary

- **Coupling through a consequence:** one extent changes something another
  claim reads, without handing that claim its private state.
- **Compensation:** the endpoint control changes width after a height change
  moves the endpoint. It is a response to the new observation, not a solved
  target width.
- **Loss of an observation:** reducing a selected section to zero can remove
  the gap reader's premise. The geometry is still present in another sense,
  but this particular claim can no longer answer.
- **Resolution conflict:** legal increments can step across a requested gap
  forever without ever reaching it.
- **Agreement without joining:** matching coordinates allow a later explicit
  connection; they do not perform that connection.

These labels summarize tested mechanisms and limits. They are not additional
runtime classes of meaning. The connected structure and its reads must remain
the evidence behind each label.
