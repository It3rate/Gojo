Four connections that
change the machine

A small machine can keep asking for something it cannot absorb, act on an old message, or receive two incompatible requests. This study adds one inspectable connection at a time, then brings the same mechanisms to a measured gap between paths.

These are supplied structural laws tested on recorded runs. They are not learned rules, a global shape optimizer, or a browser simulation. Every displayed intermediate state comes from the executed Gojo structures.

What remains true when a rule helps

Responses have different meanings

  • A comparison observes a relation; it does not transfer an amount.
  • A pending gate can stop another same-direction request without erasing the earlier debt.
  • An acknowledgement confirms processing of one request. It does not certify that the goal was reached.
  • Priority chooses a response. The incompatible request remains visible; it has not been satisfied.

The geometric step is deliberately local

  • A gap claim inspects selected paths in a declared basis.
  • Only an exposed, permitted extent adjustment can change the construction.
  • The measured gap is the outcome checked in the gap experiment. The actuator's number alone is insufficient.
  • General collision avoidance, arbitrary shape interpolation, and discovering a new construction are not supplied by these rules.
