Gojo · partial decisions and complete passes

Act, observe, make another pass

A machine can have enough information to act without knowing exactly which explanation is right. This experiment compares shared responses, explicit guesses, and coordinated trials. Each complete pass leaves an actual changed world and a small record that can guide the next one.

1 · Keep the possibilitiesAgreement can justify one response. A provisional choice permits one continuation while its alternatives remain.
2 · Make a bounded trialAsk a neighbor for a quiet interval, try an available change, and inspect what actually moved.
3 · Evaluate the whole passRetain or change a preferred option using local goal discrepancy. A better pass does not prove a motion hypothesis.

Observation and action are different

These geometric measurements are recorded diagnostics. A withheld control observation can remain visible here without being available to the machine. Landmark separation is a different claim from clearance between overlapping sections.

End of the recorded run

A shared response is conditional on all surviving supported candidates. A provisional response is an admitted choice, not evidence that the selected explanation became certain.

Two local controls, two retained uncertainties

Candidate counts are not confidence scores. A quiet hold can suppress movement while the goal is still unmet. A requested change can remain as tension rather than becoming visible extent.

What this does—and what remains supplied

The finite candidate words, construction, observation roles, goals, trial possibilities and evaluation law are supplied. Native structures retain competing explanations, admit responses and record trial and pass evidence. The browser only replays those results. This is not autonomous sensor discovery, an aesthetic judge, or proof that every run converges.
