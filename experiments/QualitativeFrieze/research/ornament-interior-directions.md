# Ornament that occupies the compartments

This pass responds to the B08–B14 refined atlas. These are authored design
targets and visual observations, not discovered Gojo laws or an automatic
aesthetic score. The native source, introduced paths, source correspondences
and selected presentation remain distinct.

## What the first comparison shows

The preceding fine treatments place a few small leaves at edge stations.
They preserve the lattice but leave its large interior areas substantially
unarticulated. The intermediate treatments of B10–B14 similarly put a tiny
cross at a selected location in an otherwise empty compartment. A source
anchor establishes correspondence; it does not, on its own, establish
convincing ornamentation.

Detail is a role in the design, not a promise of small physical scale.
An eight-petal flower can occupy most of a compartment while supporting the
larger alternating or diagonal disposition. The lattice may become placement
structure in that selected read. Deliberate covering of an internal guide is
therefore allowed; accidental collisions between unrelated petal tips,
collapsed necks and false joins remain different problems.

## References used

The existing cached scans of Owen Jones's *Grammar of Ornament*, Moresque
plates [XLII](https://archive.org/details/grammarornament00Jone/page/n142/mode/1up)
and [XLIII](https://archive.org/details/grammarornament00Jone/page/n144/mode/1up)
were inspected for this pass. Plate XLII provides both large repeat masses
with internal foliage and stronger radial centres; plate XLIII makes
alternating coloured compartments and narrow separating outlines particularly
clear. We use these as construction and colour-role references, not copies
or claims of historical colour accuracy. See the more detailed source
context in [Jones colour notes](jones-colour-notes.md).

## Family targets

| Source | Ornament construction to try | Larger rhythm to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B08, B08.B | A long lozenge can carry a broad opposed spray or flower, with veins, a centre and distinct side lobes. Use the diagonal guide to orient the whole ornament rather than attaching tiny leaves to every rail. | Long inclined courses, intervals between diagonal families, and the small diamond crossing figure in B08.B. |
| B09 | Fit a returning palmette or scroll into each detached S unit's return area; use colour or orientation alternation between neighbouring S units. | The actual detached source ends and horizontal border progression. It need not pretend to be a two-dimensional cell lattice. |
| B10 | Use broad quarter-related leaf or petal panels with a smaller central articulation. | The diagonal interlocking course and larger compartment silhouette. |
| B11 | Fill the wide arrow or arm interiors with a fitted inward-growing palmette, trefoil or branching spray. Put fine veins within that silhouette. | The two diagonal flow families and their repeated return arms. |
| B12 | A broad eight-part rosette, with four dominant petals alternating with four shorter or narrower petals, a clear centre collar, and subsidiary internal lines. Let it cover internal source guides intentionally. | The large diagonal/X disposition and a recognisable turn relationship between neighbouring centres. |
| B13, B14 | Use four consistent shape/turn colour classes, with fitted internal ornament and occasional bright highlight flowers at the more open intersections. | The repeated horizontal/vertical interlocks. Colour should reveal those relations rather than scatter accents. |

The three modes should remain useful alternatives. Broad architectural
panels, intermediate compartment flowers and richly articulated ornamental
detail can each be dominant. Changing opacity or simply replacing many small
marks with a few equally small marks is insufficient geometric variation.

## Review criteria

1. At thumbnail size, the field still reads as the original kind of diagonal,
   interlocked or border disposition.
2. At middle distance, the new motif has a clear silhouette occupying a
   meaningful fraction of its compartment, rather than a dot on a guide.
3. A closer view rewards inspection with veins, nested petal articulation,
   smaller lobes or another relation that belongs to the larger motif.
4. Colour alternation follows repeat, turn or shape correspondence. Four
   contrasting shape classes are appropriate for B13/B14 when they remain
   balanced by outlines and quieter subsidiary colours.
5. Intentional source coverage is judged by the resulting hierarchy, not
   automatically rejected as overlap. Unrelated ornaments should not collide
   at their tips or create an unexplained dark central blot.
6. Construction views retain the native source even when the decorative
   reading covers it. Smooth rendering does not add a native connection,
   analytic curve or occupied-area guarantee.

## Render review

The first executed B08 and B08.B fields now have broad, stretched flower
medallions inside the larger lozenges, alternating with a much smaller
four-part accent in the narrow counterfield. Their long opposing petals and
layered inner contours make a real second and third level. The close-up shows
intentional petal layering rather than collisions between neighbouring units.
The diagonal course still reads clearly at a distance.

B09's opposing return fans occupy the two sides of each S unit. Their broad
silhouettes make the border fuller without claiming continuity across the
actual detached source ends. Overall, intermediate and detail treatments now
differ in fan articulation, source weight and colour role.

B13 and B14's four broad filled source classes are particularly convincing. Cap fans
follow the horizontal and vertical units, narrow bar veins run with those
units, and the larger multicoloured flower makes a separate focus over the
interlock. Its coverage of the guide is intentional and supports the repeated
field. No unwanted neighbouring motif collision was found in the inspected
field.

The first B10/B11 detail previews were more conservative. Their four-petal
flowers are placed in actual chambers and have useful colour alternation,
but still leave substantial gaps around isolated spokes. Short subsidiary
petals or inward buds within the same outer envelope were recommended for
a richer fine reading. This was an aesthetic improvement request, not a
failed native attachment or a general containment claim. The revised B10
detail has eight petals with longer major petals, nested subsidiary contours
and fine veins. It now occupies a meaningful part of the chamber, deliberately
covers some internal guide lines and remains separate from neighbouring
flowers. The pointed diagonal disposition survives the cover. B14's final
detail likewise keeps its broad interlock and individual ornamental units
readable without unwanted neighbouring collisions.

B12's final field and executed unit make the requested hierarchy especially
clear: four long diagonal petals dominate four shorter axial petals, while
nested veins and two centre collars supply finer articulation. The four bloom
colour roles repeat with the actual source units. The nearest diagonal tips
retain a visible interval with the displayed edging, and the stepped/X source
disposition remains legible between the flowers. Internal guide coverage is
purposeful rather than a failed attempt to fit a small mark on every edge.

B11 needs a separate neighbour-spacing check because its chamber centres are
not equally spaced. The superseded enlarged eight-petal treatment used twice the
selected chamber reach. Its close lower pair met at their major-petal tips,
and a staggered sibling overlapped that pair in the repeated field. That
three-flower cluster was not merely deliberate covering of source guides.
The replacement uses approximately one-and-a-half chamber reaches for the
major petals and three-quarter reaches for the diagonal petals, preserving
the eight-part structure and inner veins. A native finite-field regression
checks the four blooms and their neighbouring period images. The final
imported execution passes visual review at field and unit scale: the close
vertical pair now has a clear interval, the staggered sibling remains
separate, and the strong flower silhouette still purposefully covers local
construction lines.

This counterexample separates two different reads: a local compartment can
supply a useful centre and reach without supplying a guarantee about
neighbouring decoration. Intentional source-guide coverage is permitted; it
does not remove the need to inspect the remaining counterfield between
independently placed ornaments.

All 24 revised designs were inspected: overall, intermediate and ornamental
detail for B08, B08.B and B09–B14. Repeated fields were compared in source
triples, with targeted executed-unit views for the broad B08, B11 and B12
flowers. The final set retains distinct border, diagonal-lattice, pointed
chamber and horizontal/vertical interlock dispositions. No remaining unwanted
neighbouring-motif collision was found in the inspected final fields.

Finite visual review can establish that a shown composition is more
convincing; it does not certify all possible parameters or prove a general
containment/clearance theorem.
