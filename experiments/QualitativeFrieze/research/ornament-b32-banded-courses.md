# B32: banded courses with fourfold inlay

This revision is an authored native refinement, not a newly discovered law of
figure-ground organization. The original four open diagonal paths, their
public ports, diagonal half-drop and declared neighbour seams survive by
identity. Their separate free ends and coincident stroke histories remain
separate. A broad rendered line does not create a native filled ribbon or
authenticate a new join.

All three versions now select those courses in the field layer alone. The
intermediate and fine layers no longer redraw the same source with thin detail
ink. The former flower, stem and eight-lobed silhouette are removed. Instead,
one quiet diamond unit is attached through an actual source port and a native
offset witness. Reading the finite words in the recipe's `Source.Frame`, its
equal corner rays lie on the two frame axes and its sides run parallel to the
actual diagonal source courses. The contour has exact fourfold symmetry in
that shared frame. Diamond/square appearance still depends on the program's
selected placement and display frame. Nesting
that same construction provides the three scale readings:

| Reading | Inlay | Total native chords per unit |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Overall | One diamond | 20 |
| Intermediate | Two nested diamonds | 24 |
| Fine | Three nested diamonds | 28 |

The largest inlay is 16 shared reaches across, smaller than the preceding
36-reach floral silhouette. Its fourfold symmetry belongs to its actual native
path words. The inlay is deliberately subordinate: line width, palette and
separating keylines provide the principal field/figure experiment without
adding more native adornment. They remain presentation choices over the same
inspectable paths, rather than claims that a filled-face or optical-duality law
has been implemented.
