# Fitted ornament with a limited curved presentation

This round refines the existing 36 Bourgoin skeletons into three distinct
designs each. It retains the previous atlas and the ten detail studies as
comparison material. The aim is a more convincing fitted ornament vocabulary,
not a new universal aesthetic law or a claim that the native geometry has
become an analytic curve system.

Read with [the three-scale directions](ornament-design-directions.md),
[the detail-study fit notes](ornament-detail-directions.md), and
[the Jones source notes](jones-colour-notes.md). Dresser's distinction between
directed sprays and radial ornaments remains useful: an elongated woven strip
and a centred hexagonal compartment should not receive the same free-floating
decoration.

## What the curve changes

The native source, finite ordered paths, actual landmarks, offset words,
refinement correspondence and gap witnesses remain authoritative. An explicitly
selected presentation may soften suitable ornamental contours. A smooth
display is neither evidence of an exact native tangent nor a new connection.
Independent paths retain their identities even where they look tangent.

Use the smallest useful curved treatment:

- Round the shoulders and sides of a leaf while preserving its pointed tip
  and exact attachment. Keep its vein aligned with the same source guide.
- Give a flowing stem or returning curl an intelligible sweep. Retain actual
  branch stations rather than fitting one spline that wanders past them.
- Soften petal sides in a rosette while retaining separate necks and the quiet
  centre. Preserve the actual radial correspondence and compartment clearance.
- Leave lattice intersections, square and diamond corners, star tips, fret
  elbows, polygonal panels and source interruptions exact. They are part of
  the design's construction rather than defects to smooth away.

Prefer bounded local rounding or curves constrained to a declared chord
corridor over an unrestricted interpolating spline. A nice centreline is not
enough: dark edging, pale keylines and filled contours need clearance too.
Closed leaf contours must not develop loops or switch apparent winding near
their narrow bases.

## The three detail examples

**D02, running vine.** The main stem benefits from a continuous sweep through
its alternating branch stations. The leaf stalk should seem to depart in
the direction of growth, rather than collide with the stem at an arbitrary
angle. Preserve the branch's actual root; a separately drawn stalk may share
the apparent tangent without claiming a stronger native joining law.

The first finite stem also bulges into two leaf shoulders. Replacing its
intermediate bends `(1,-4)` and `(-1,4)` with `(-1,-4)` and `(1,4)` keeps
the roots `(0,-8), (0,0), (0,8)` fixed while placing the stem on the opposite
side from each emerging leaf. This small native correction should precede
the curved display.

**D07, corner acanthus and returning curl.** The first source has an unwanted
crossing: curl chord `(4,-1) -> (6,1)` crosses parent chord
`(4,0) -> (6,0)`. Presentation smoothing cannot repair that structural layout.
A proposed native replacement keeps the same actual root `(1,-1)`, then
passes through `(1,2), (3,4), (5,4), (5,2)` to the open terminal `(3,1)`.
This puts the curl on one side of the parent and left of the right leaf's
region beginning at `x=6`. The pearl at `(3,2)` can remain secondary. Review
the resulting paths and displayed edging before accepting the layout; the
coordinate proposal is not itself a general clearance proof.

After that correction, soften the curl and the turning parent while keeping
their root fixed. Keep the leaf tips sharp. A broader curl with fewer abrupt
turns is preferable to hiding an intersection under a large bead.

**D09, interrupted guilloche.** Both strands benefit from rounded wave
shoulders. Each actual open path must be processed independently. Preserve
its endpoints and the nonzero native gap: no interpolation across the gap,
no background-coloured mask concealing a claimed continuous strand, and no
paint-order claim of over/under topology. The continuous lilac strand remains
stronger than the thinner copper interrupted portions. Check the gap with
all edge widths enabled, not only the coloured centreline.

## Family-specific fit

| Sources | Refinement that follows the source | Keep exact or quiet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| B01, B19, B23 | Elongated veined leaves or graded palmettes in actual panels; fine sprigs follow the course | Bond offsets, panel corners and gaps |
| B02 | Upright and quarter-related axial sprigs or one feather leaf, contained in each actual strip | The emergent square counterfield; strip outline |
| B07 | Narrow along-band vines, small linked pearls and alternating collar leaves | Crossing organisation; no implied new interlace order |
| B03, B06, B06.B, B18 | Distinct radial flower and quieter inner veins, or large polygonal window in the overall mode | Octagon/square boundaries, separate petal necks |
| B04, B05, B20, B25 | Sixfold flower or inward buds with justified native turn maps | Triangular counterfield and open centre |
| B24, B29 | Smaller flowers or buds follow each slid compartment's local frame | Sliding offsets; do not regularise the field by decoration |
| B26 | Leaflets and short veins follow actual star rays; a quieter small central flower | Flat star tips, subsidiary hexagonal windows |
| B08, B08.B | Narrow leaf chains follow each diagonal ribbon family | Diamond intersections and the interval between ribbon families |
| B15, B16, B17 | Small contained leaves, simple seeds or architectural grain aligned to the mesh | Mesh junctions; avoid cumulative filled bands at shared corners |
| B21, B22, B27 | Long leaves or short sprays follow the actual inclined course | Chevron turns and pointed compartment tips |
| B09–B14, B31 | A small returning leaf, bud or branch at an actual arm station; restrained fitted corner fleuron | Detached ends, T joins, closed interlocks and round source markers |
| B28 | Graded lotus or palmette lobes grow from the fan base | The shell's major ribs and separation between fans |
| B30 | Small paired leaves or lotus in arch valleys, with a smooth subsidiary vine | The scale arrangement and visible overlap rhythm |
| B32, B33 | Short return leaves and buds follow the diagonal courses | Row slide, T joins, free ends and continuous white channels |
| B34 | Rounded subsidiary lobes and a restrained central blossom | Actual loop identity; no inferred knot order |

Overall designs still need broad architectural silhouettes; intermediate
designs need a recognisable flower, spray or palmette; fine designs need a
distinct enriching role within the same construction. That role does not
require proportionally tiny marks. A large rosette or leaf can accent the
overall pattern if its placement, repeated orientation and counterforms
strengthen the source rhythm. Improving the motifs must not turn every mode
into the same flower with different opacity.
At least two differences should be geometric: silhouette and placement,
branching and density, or panel construction and fine internal articulation.
Judge hierarchy by structural role and what the viewer reads first, rather
than imposing one absolute decoration-size limit across unrelated sources.

## Review plan

Start with B02, B05, B07, B08, B15, B26 and B32, because they expose strip fit,
radial fit, diagonal flow, tight-mesh density and displaced-row organisation.
Then review all 108 designs in source triples: overall, intermediate and fine.
Small three- or four-by-four fields are sufficient if they show several
complete units and their neighbouring counterforms.

For every triple, inspect the major disposition at thumbnail size, the motif
at a middle distance, and branch roots or narrow gaps close up. Specifically
look for accidental shared-corner masses, leaf tips crossing source panels,
rounded lattice corners, collapsed petal necks, disconnected-looking stalks,
and newly closed native interruptions. Check occasional one-ink comparisons:
colour should clarify distinct constructions, not provide their only difference.

## Outcomes

The three limited-curve examples were compared with their angular versions,
both as close-ups and repeated fields. C-D02 now keeps its parent stem outside
the alternating leaf shoulders. C-D07's native reroute removes the parent
crossing; its displayed curl and both leaves remain separate, with pointed tips
and the original branch roots. C-D09's wave shoulders become smoother while
the actual interruption and both path ends remain visible. Faint angular
guides are still available as construction evidence. These are visual checks
of the selected finite examples, not a general tangent or clearance theorem.

The initial seven-source atlas fit review covered B02, B05, B07, B08, B15, B26
and B32 in all three modes. B02's feather leaf, finer axial sprig and broad
panel are especially clear alternatives, all retaining quiet square gaps.
B05's inward buds and outward flower give distinct radial readings while
preserving triangular counterforms. B07 and B32 follow their actual band or
diagonal course organisation. B15 avoids the previous shared-corner mass;
B26 leaves flat star tips and subsidiary hexagonal windows readable.

The initial B08 intermediate preview exposed a small tangle at the paired leaf
bases. Its corrected native construction uses opposed leaves on one common
strip axis; the replacement render separates their interiors cleanly. Some fine variants,
notably the outward B05 flower, are bolder than their intermediate counterpart.
Treat these as alternative focus scales rather than claiming every fine design
is visually quieter.

The final visual review covered all 108 designs: three modes for all 36 source
skeletons, including B06.B and B08.B. The
[refined atlas](../results/ornament-refined/gallery.html) retains the earlier
collection for comparison, and the
[curve studies](../results/ornament-curves/gallery.html) show selected angular
and curved readings separately.

### Fit is not sufficient richness

A direct comparison with the prior B03, B28 and B32 triples exposed a separate
design problem. The initial generic open-course recipe placed a few narrow
leaves in all three modes. It preserved the source and fitted the additions,
but lost the broad architecture, distinctive flowers and layered enrichment
of the earlier atlas. B32 made this particularly clear: three sparse variants
of edge leaves are not a satisfactory replacement for a diagonal brocade,
flower chain and finer course enrichment.

The revised direction is therefore to retain family-specific silhouettes as
well as better local fit. B32's overall reading needs actual angular companion
contours and a modest geometric return panel; its intermediate reading needs
a visible short stem carrying a flower and subsidiary leaves; its fine
reading can keep the restrained edge foliage. B28 needs broad fan panels
versus graded lotus or palmette lobes. B03 benefits from larger eight-part
medallions alternating with smaller four-part units, instead of one identical
small flower in every compartment. B30 similarly needs broad cloud bands,
directed valley growth and fine course pearls as different constructions.
Increasing line width or colour contrast alone cannot recover these missing
differences.

The refreshed B03, B28 and B32 triples were checked after those family-specific
changes. They now recover alternating eight-/four-part medallions, graded
lotus fans, and a recognisable diagonal flower-chain reading respectively.
B32's overall companion contours and return lozenges are clearly different
from its flowers and its fine edge foliage. The close-up retains the detached
return ends. One spray is deliberately offset slightly from the parent course:
its native attachment witness is an exact offset from a public source port,
not a claim that every visible stalk starts at that point. The image and notes
must preserve this distinction between fitted enrichment and a physical join.

The collection should be presented as fitted alternatives, not a universal
increase in richness. For example, B06 and B06.B deliberately use restrained
four-petal crosses in both intermediate and fine treatments, at different
scales; their earlier eight-petal medallion and corner-fleuron alternatives
remain worth comparing. A quieter design can be useful without making the
earlier richer design obsolete.

### Final field review

The source triples were inspected in contact sheets, with targeted close-ups
for the revised vines, curls, interrupted strands and flower sprays. No new
curve-induced collisions or closures of the inspected detached source ends
were found. Sliding triangle and hexagon dispositions, diagonal return courses,
outward arms and separate crossed-loop paths remain legible. This is a visual
review of the finite displayed fields, not an exhaustive geometric intersection
proof or an inferred native occupied-area model.

B17, B18 and B20 are useful examples of the user's detail-role clarification.
Their fine leaves are conspicuous enough to create a larger diagonal rhythm,
yet the source mesh and its counterforms remain readable. Their success does
not depend on keeping every enrichment proportionally tiny. B27's ridge-rooted
spray and B30's paired valley leaves likewise make the decoration's relationship
to the source easier to see than a generic isolated flower would.

One limit is particularly visible in B12's overall treatment. Scaling a
nonconvex source contour about a selected centre does not guarantee containment
inside the original contour. Some added lines cross the source at re-entrant
elbows, producing layered overprinting. Those contours should be described as
scaled companion readings, not certified contained insets or evidence of
filled-face clearance. The display fillet does not cause or resolve that
layout. A future containment-aware refinement would need an explicit local
boundary/occupancy read and its remaining uncertainty, rather than relying on
the builder's method name.

The useful advance is therefore narrower and more concrete: a richer reusable
ornament vocabulary can be attached through actual source surfaces, refined at
a declared basis, and optionally given a restrained curved presentation while
the original construction and its limits remain inspectable. Source anchoring,
convincing visible growth, containment and preferred aesthetic hierarchy remain
different questions. The atlas provides examples and counterexamples for those
questions rather than collapsing them into one successful attachment flag.
