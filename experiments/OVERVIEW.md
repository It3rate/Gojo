# Gojo experiments: a reviewer's guide

This guide brings together the completed work in `experiments/Minds/` and `experiments/`
through **14 October 2026**. Each entry describes the question, the recorded
outcome, and what that outcome supports. Follow its links for protocols, controls,
measurements and retained failures.

The work progresses from exchanging uncertain information, through prediction
and local service markets, to sensing, action, learned composition and renewal.
Some studies test a supplied mechanism; others let a learner select or adjust
parts. A working mechanism and an improvement over a simpler control are
different results throughout this guide.

The later qualitative-pattern sequence is indexed in the
[experiment catalog](README.md). Its latest
[hierarchical recognition report](../Gojo/docs/GojoHierarchicalRecognition.md)
and [interactive gallery](QualitativeFrieze/results/hierarchical-recognition/gallery.html)
follow motif observations into row programs and higher arrangements, then trace
specific unresolved questions back down through local services.
The [executable ornament format](../Gojo/docs/GojoOrnamentProgramEncoding.md)
then separates reusable native generation programs from their parameters and
sampled patches, including save/load and standalone execution.
The [Bourgoin construction study](../Gojo/docs/GojoBourgoinEncodingStudy.md)
then exercises source-derived geometric fields with explicit public-landmark
seams, separate finite boundaries, and imported programs that can sample new
windows without an authoring factory. Its
[source comparison gallery](QualitativeFrieze/results/bourgoin-patterns/gallery.html)
states reconstruction limits alongside each example.

The [relational shape study](../Gojo/docs/GojoRelationalShapeStudy.md) tests
100 sources reconstructed from anonymous joints, spatial comparisons and
separate equivalence and symmetry streams. Its
[four-context gallery](QualitativeFrieze/results/relational-shapes/gallery.html)
preserves open and conflicting reads; a local extent-feedback follow-up tests
whether those relations can guide revision as well as assess a finished sample.

The [evidence calculation fixture](../Gojo/docs/GojoEvidenceCalculationFixture.md)
then separates compatibility, conditional model comparison, empirical support
and output resolution. Its
[square-continuation examples](QualitativeFrieze/results/evidence-calculation/gallery.html)
test supplied statistical laws rather than learning construction mechanisms.

## Minds: feedback, limits and prediction

### 1. Two-space feedback · Minds 01 · 15 September

**Tried:** Connect two local estimates and let each respond to the other's
message. **Found:** Moderate responses brought them together; full responses
could make them exchange values repeatedly while their average stayed constant.
Changing message timing or the selected reference changed the result.
**Conclusion:** Local agreement, ongoing internal activity and agreement with an
external reference must be observed separately. This demonstrates feedback under
supplied rules, rather than discovered consensus or truth.

[Results](Minds/results/two-space/Results.md) · [Visual replay](Minds/results/two-space/index.html)

### 2. Feedback limits · Minds 02 · 15 September

**Tried:** Vary resolution, response size, message availability and resource
limits across 24 conditions, each repeated in three fresh worlds.
**Found:** All 72 runs matched the independent exact calculation. Some pairs
stopped visibly changing while still disagreeing because the requested changes
were smaller than their available tick. Missing messages and exhausted resources
could also produce quiet output for different reasons.
**Conclusion:** Quietness alone does not establish settlement of the intended
goal. The independent comparison checks the chosen mathematical contract; it
does not show superiority over conventional arithmetic.

[Findings](Minds/docs/FeedbackLimitsFindings.md) · [Visual replay](Minds/results/feedback-limits/20260915-230957/index.html)

### 3. Predictions before outcomes · Minds 03 · 16 September

**Tried:** Predict the next value using a line through the last two observations,
compared with repeating the last value, on 12 sequences.
**Found:** The line rule won on ramps but lost on alternating and step sequences.
Missing outcomes remained unscored, and later observations did not rewrite old
forecasts. All cases matched the independent exact calculation.
**Conclusion:** Prospective evaluation works and exposes where a rule fails.
Fitting this supplied rule is not learning which kind of model to use.

[Findings](Minds/docs/PredictionFindings.md) · [Visual replay](Minds/results/predictions/20260916-001834/index.html)

## Meaning and interpretation

### 4. Concept scratchpad · 26 September; corrected 1 October

**Tried:** Construct small connected meanings, including relative-height
statements, and inspect what could be inferred, left open, saved and restored.
**Found:** The initial probe exposed missing-input and archive-preservation
problems. Follow-up corrections preserved directed structure and kept incomplete
calculations unresolved; supplying missing information could make an existing
calculation readable.
**Conclusion:** Connected premises and their gaps can remain executable and
inspectable. The meanings were constructed by hand; this did not demonstrate
language understanding or automatic conceptual discovery.

[Original findings and corrected status](ConceptScratchpad/README.md)

### 5. Poster interpretation, two series · 27 September

**Tried:** Express how different audiences might judge the same poster, then
compare alternative designs with explicit ranges and opposing contributions.
**Found:** Changing the supplied audience assumptions changed rankings. Some
comparisons separated clearly; others overlapped and did not justify a single
winner.
**Conclusion:** Gojo can preserve contextual judgments and unresolved
comparisons. The audience ratings were authored, not measured audience responses,
and their numerical resolution was not statistical confidence.

[First series](PosterScratchpad/README.md) · [Second series](PosterScratchpad/SERIES-2.md)

## Live input and prediction

### 6. Poetry and multiple interpretations · 2 October

**Tried:** Build six small examples of contextual meaning, substitution,
ambiguity and shared dependencies.
**Found:** Different interpretations could remain connected and be recalculated
without forcing them into one scalar answer.
**Conclusion:** These are demonstrations of how to represent supplied meanings.
They do not show automatic poetry interpretation, discovered metaphor, or a
mechanism that makes competing interpretations improve one another.

[Examples and limits](PoetryScratchpad/README.md)

### 7. Streaming and unresolved change · 2 October

**Tried:** Feed changing inputs into live structures, preserve change that had
no available route, and continue from a saved native structure.
**Found:** Inputs could settle together; unhandled change remained available and
could flow when a route became available. Restored structures could continue
independently.
**Conclusion:** This establishes useful communication and execution machinery.
The routing structure was supplied; discovering sensors or learning a useful
response remained the next problem.

[Results and structural checks](StreamingScratchpad/README.md)

### 8. Predictor network · 2 October

**Tried:** Combine seven supplied prediction rules, adjusting their participation
from errors on two input streams.
**Found:** The mixture improved on some controls, but simpler individual rules
often did better. Continuing adaptation did not reliably improve on freezing the
weights, and a running mean won on shuffled input.
**Conclusion:** Useful prediction is possible, but more candidates and continued
adjustment do not automatically help. The system also lacked a good response
when all available candidates were poorly supported.

[Results and comparisons](PredictorScratchpad/README.md)

### 9. Prediction after failures, then timing and attention · 2–3 October

**Tried:** Let failed predictions recruit help, then add event cues, timing at
different resolutions, selective inspection and dormancy.
**Found:** Learned timing improved regular zigzag prediction from 85/112 to
105/112 correct forecasts. Leading event cues could help; late or unrelated
cues did not reliably help. Coarse timing could be right while finer timing was
wrong. Suspending unwanted forecasts saved work but sometimes missed a useful
returning event. The final cadence revision still failed on alternating
intervals and increased allocation in most conditions.
**Conclusion:** Timing, demand and whether an old model still applies need
separate treatment. These were bounded adaptations among supplied possibilities,
with meaningful failures as the task became less regular.

[timing resolution](BreakDrivenPrediction/TIMING.md),

## Connections, usefulness and markets

### 10. Connection assembly and its engineering follow-ups · 4 October

**Tried:** Let six supplied small readers recruit, retain and withdraw useful
connections as input patterns changed.
**Found:** Connections formed and changed, but rare useful helpers could fade
before their next opportunity, and the broad definition of “help” could reward
the wrong contribution. Follow-ups introduced actual withdrawal and reused
existing equations without changing the recorded behavior; one diagnostic
comparison reduced cumulative allocation by about 44%.
**Conclusion:** Changing participation and reusing live structure work. Those
mechanisms did not by themselves discover larger temporal concepts or solve
how usefulness should be credited.

[Original experiment](ConnectionAssembly/README.md) ·
[Withdrawal](ConnectionAssembly/lifecycle-update.md) ·
[Equation reuse](ConnectionAssembly/dynamic-update.md) ·
[Allocation audit](ConnectionAssembly.Audit/README.md)

### 11. Consumer-specific prediction value · 4 October

**Tried:** Reward a helper only for improving a particular consumer's next
prediction beyond what that consumer already had.
**Found:** Redundant advice earned no support. Helpful advice attached, harmful
advice lost support, and a retained helper could return when useful again. In
the designed mixed trial, adaptive delivery left 16 ticks of unacceptable error,
versus 64 without help and 32 with help always enabled.
**Conclusion:** Usefulness can depend on the recipient's needs and tolerance.
This is a controlled demonstration with a supplied helper and supplied boundaries,
not general discovery of useful services.

[Results and controls](ConsumerPrediction/README.md)

### 12. Negotiated ports · 4 October

**Tried:** Let different consumers buy predictions or intermediate information
through limited local negotiation and a shared finite resource pool.
**Found:** Services delivered prospective predictions and conserved resources.
Errors improved over the no-service references, but duplicate suppliers,
encounter order and weak price adjustment led to inefficient choices.
**Conclusion:** Local paid exchange works. Conserved accounts and successful
transactions do not establish an efficient market or a superior predictor.

[Assessment](NegotiatedPorts/ASSESSMENT.md)

### 13. Longer market runs and memory costs · 4 October

**Tried:** Continue the market through changing inputs while retaining enough
state to resume, without keeping every completed diagnostic record.
**Found:** Small checkpoints supported continuation; a separate paired audit
removed about 71% of encoded size without changing the tested continuations.
However, buyers kept spending during flat periods and did not consistently
prefer cheaper equivalent services or more valuable services at equal prices.
**Conclusion:** Compact retained state and continuation are feasible. Selective
spending remained weak, and small checkpoints did not imply cheap computation.

[Market assessment](MarketTraining/ASSESSMENT.md) ·
[Resource audit](MarketResourceAudit/README.md)

### 14. Earned support · 4 October

**Tried:** Replenish spending capacity when services produced measured benefit,
instead of letting a fixed starting pool simply run out.
**Found:** Funding extended useful activity, but also prolonged waste. Lucky
positive bursts could earn funding for an overall harmful random service, and
flat-period purchases could exhaust funds before useful demand returned.
**Conclusion:** Funding and a positive-benefit trace are insufficient to decide
when work deserves continued support.

[Assessment](EarnedSupport/ASSESSMENT.md)

### 15. Local service growth · 4 October

**Tried:** Let a buyer construct and validate its own equivalent service, then
stop buying upstream while continuing to serve its downstream customer.
**Found:** Under the declared costs, substitution reduced spending by roughly
22–27% without changing prediction error. Public popularity hints did not improve
choices, and irreversible promotion could retain a poor service after lucky
early results.
**Conclusion:** Reusing and internalizing a service can pay. The result depends
on supplied construction choices and costs; it is not discovery of a new skill
or proof that popularity identifies useful knowledge.

[Assessment](LocalServiceGrowth/ASSESSMENT.md)

### 16. Failure subscriptions and adaptive contracts · 5 October

**Tried:** Replace repeated purchases with short contracts that could renew,
cancel or resume; then use reviews to separate paid trials from trusted advice.
**Found:** Cancellation alone did not stop repeated harmful purchases. The
follow-up protected the consumer from bad advice and helped on recurring
patterns, but still spent on unhelpful trials. Longer or adaptive contracts
traded some accuracy for lower purchasing overhead.
**Conclusion:** Learning whether to trust an answer is different from deciding
whether another trial is worth paying for. Neither version solved both reliably.

[Initial assessment](FailureSubscriptions/ASSESSMENT.md) ·
[Adaptive follow-up](FailureSubscriptions/ADAPTIVE_ASSESSMENT.md)

### 17. Change subscriptions and wake-up signals · 5 October

**Tried:** Let direction and amount consumers judge the same predictor separately,
back off after failures, and optionally wake early when a coarse change appeared.
**Found:** A source could help one consumer and harm the other. Backoff reduced
waste but delayed recovery. Wake-up signals sometimes found useful change
earlier, but also reacted to chatter and reduced aggregate benefit in the tested
cases.
**Conclusion:** There is no single useful/unhelpful label for a source. The
tested wake-up rule did not justify enabling it by default.

[Subscriptions](ChangeSubscriptions/ASSESSMENT.md) ·
[Wake-up follow-up](ChangeSubscriptions/WAKE_ASSESSMENT.md)

## Phototaxis: swimming, control and execution

### 18. Swimming and adaptive circuit markets · earlier Phototaxis passes

**Tried:** Put native Gojo controllers in an external simulated world, charging
for work and allowing circuit changes, inheritance and reproduction.
**Found:** The sequence moved from a food-dot prototype to photosynthetic
swimmers and contact mating. Circuits grew and changed, but the controlled
composed-program trials had lower survival and harvest than fixed controls;
an obstacle trial reached extinction.
**Conclusion:** The ecological machinery and structural changes work. These
runs did not establish reliable learned light-seeking, obstacle escape or an
advantage from the market's credit rules. Results from the different worlds
should not be pooled.

[Early scope](Phototaxis/PROTOCOL.md) ·
[Photosynthetic trials](Phototaxis/README.md#measured-pass-before-stationary-predation) ·
[Composed-program results](Phototaxis/PROGRAM-RESULTS.md)

### 19. Authored swimming controllers · 9 October

**Tried:** Build and inspect three successive rounds of controller designs,
then test the selected design on two unused seeds.
**Found:** The selected controller improved total creature lifetime by 42.7%
over straight swimming in that validation. Native memory supported switching
between seeking food and departing when full.
**Conclusion:** Gojo can express effective control in this world. People designed
these controllers, and the earlier learner's available parts could not recreate
the complete winning program. Fixed computation charges also left extra design
complexity underpriced.

[Design rounds and validation](Phototaxis/DESIGNED-RESULTS.md)

### 20. Motion, prediction, breeding and social responses · 9 October series

**Tried:** Add motion estimates, prediction mismatch, recovery and inherited
modules; then compare vision, progress-escape and courtship responses.
**Found:** Breeding recombined modules but showed no clear survival advantage
over fixed inheritance. Spatial vision materially helped the authored pilot.
Adding both escape and courtship increased births from 14 to 20 across two seeds,
mostly through one seed; enlarging the arena reduced feeding and births.
**Conclusion:** Specific information and authored responses can matter, while
module variation alone does not establish useful evolution. Brief external
Jev advice probes tested an interface, not a demonstrated learning advantage;
the paired social trials used no Jev calls.

[Cognitive comparisons](Phototaxis/COGNITIVE-RESULTS.md) ·
[Social and arena trials](Phototaxis/SOCIAL-PROGRESS-RESULTS.md)

### 21. Compact execution, SIMD and parallel trials · 9–10 October

**Tried:** Execute the same native controller structures more efficiently while
checking that observations still matched.
**Found:** Compact execution reduced allocation and improved runtime on the
measured workloads. SIMD had no convincing overall benefit. Running four
independent worlds concurrently improved batch throughput by 2.45× on the
tested machine, while each individual world slowed under contention.
**Conclusion:** Execution can improve without changing the tested behavior.
These are workload-specific engineering results, separate from evidence of
better learning; the comparisons held resource charges fixed.

[Compact execution](Phototaxis/COMPACT-EXECUTION.md) ·
[SIMD and parallel results](Phototaxis/SIMD-PARALLEL.md)

## Sensing and primitive control

### 22. Sampling before measurement · 11 October

**Tried:** Build distinctions using only signal presence and requested ordering
comparisons, adding metric calibration separately afterward.
**Found:** A finite set of landmarks supported coarse intervals, and rescaling
the private source preserved the ordinal results. A tiny increase could be
detected without knowing its size; an explicit later calibration supplied that
size.
**Conclusion:** Order and useful distinctions need not begin with measured
amounts. The experiment did not discover units, equal spacing or probabilities,
and its landmark limit did not bound all retained history.

[Study](SamplingEmergence/README.md) · [Recorded report](../artifacts/sampling-emergence/report.md)

### 23. Primitive agency and receiver sweep · 11 October

**Tried:** Make a small authored creature seek charge and avoid overheating,
buying a finer comparison only when a coarse read could not settle an action.
**Found:** Paid comparisons eliminated starvation in the default trial, although
they cost resources. Computed and lookup implementations agreed. A subsequent
sweep of 12 sensing/interpretation combinations found no safe improvement over
the simpler reference.
**Conclusion:** Coarse information plus selected refinement can support action.
This was supplied control, not learning; the longer sweep validation reused the
same periodic world rather than testing a new one.

[Results and sweep](PrimitiveAgency/README.md)

### 24. Relative signals and fast/slow memory · 11 October

**Tried:** Control movement using comparisons with recent and older observations,
without exposing absolute brightness.
**Found:** Behavior was unchanged by the tested increasing transformations of
private brightness. Fast and slow memories sometimes disagreed, but combining
them did not improve pursuit: the fast-only policy did better in this world and
the larger observer required many more comparisons than previous-only memory.
**Conclusion:** Relative history can guide action, but more history does not
automatically produce a better response. These response rules were authored.

[Results and limits](RelativeSignals/README.md)

### 25. Primitive forager · 11 October sequence

**Tried:** Combine missing/present signals, orientation, recent and longer
history, coarse body readings and selected precision in one controller.
**Found:** Removing orientation prevented successful charge/cool cycles, and
selected precision used far fewer fine readings than always requesting them.
However, recent-positive signals could lag a fresh decline, and the more
elaborate full controller completed fewer feeding cycles than some simpler
versions.
**Conclusion:** The information distinctions affect real decisions, but correct
local arithmetic and conservative safety rules do not guarantee useful overall
behavior. This remained an authored controller.

[Study](PrimitiveForager/README.md) ·
[Closer reading of results](../artifacts/primitive-forager/analysis.md)

## Learning, composition and renewal

### 26. Primitive learning · 11 October

**Tried:** Let four small learners acquire distinctions, fit predictions, choose
paid observations and select responses for a two-lane routing task.
**Found:** Across eight fresh seeds, the combined controller routed about 97.7%
correctly while buying observations on 55.9% of episodes. It earned 3.258 resource
units per episode versus 3.000 for always buying the exact observation. A
repetition follow-up showed that prediction's benefit disappeared when the
initial prior already fit the task.
**Conclusion:** Learned local parts can combine to make paid information more
selective. The learning laws, ports and candidate grammar were supplied;
general hierarchy discovery and closed-loop survival were not tested.

[Report](PrimitiveLearning/REPORT.md) · [Protocol](PrimitiveLearning/PROTOCOL.md)

### 27. Composite emergence and service reuse · 11–12 October

**Tried:** Learn a relationship between two separately uninformative direction
signals, publish it through a restricted interface, and let another learner use
it for action.
**Found:** All eight runs learned the relation and answered every held-out
source trial correctly. Reusing that service accelerated a new action task,
though it required earlier source training. A further learner could manage
paid inspection, but frozen managers often stopped retrying after missing input.
**Conclusion:** Learned composition and transfer work within this supplied
hierarchy. The result does not establish invented hierarchies, lower total
training cost, or reliable recovery from unfamiliar missing information.

[Short results](CompositeEmergence/SUMMARY.md) · [Full report](CompositeEmergence/REPORT.md)

### 28. Retry, continued learning and lifecycle · 12 October UTC

**Tried:** Investigate the previous missing-input failure, then separately
demonstrate paid maintenance, withdrawal and funded replacement.
**Found:** Clean-trained frozen managers could act only 2.5% of the time under
missing signals. Continued learning, with additional feedback, raised that to
97.4%; training with missing signals before freezing reached 99.0%. Once
appropriately trained, continued exploration reduced return. The separate
lifecycle demonstration could withdraw
an unfunded connection and create a replacement when resources allowed.
**Conclusion:** Retry is learnable here, while replacement provides another
mechanism for change. Their combination has not yet demonstrated autonomous
architectural evolution, and construction costs remain incomplete.

[Short results](AdaptiveLifecycle/SUMMARY.md) · [Full report](AdaptiveLifecycle/REPORT.md)

### 29. Utility ecology · 12 October UTC

**Tried:** Combine locally learned services, narrow public answers, paid work
and changing demand in one small service economy.
**Found:** Withdrawing learned answers substantially reduced group return.
Learning only when errors called for it cut updates by 85.7% and improved return
over always learning. Retaining dormant useful knowledge helped, while an added
opportunity gate slightly hurt; payment and renewal refinements had no clear
overall advantage.
**Conclusion:** Useful learned services, selective work and retained knowledge
matter in this setup. Not every additional market rule helps, and noise and
newly useful information remain difficult cases.

[Confirmation report](UtilityEcology/REPORT.md) · [Offline review](UtilityEcology/review.html)

### 30. Learning progress alongside error · 12 October UTC

**Tried:** Spend according to whether learning appeared to be improving, rather
than responding to error alone.
**Found:** The progress observation distinguished improvement, regression and
missing answers, but its spending rule did not beat error gating. It reduced
work on noise while also restricting useful learning; most learning opportunities
still came from scheduled retests.
**Conclusion:** An interpretable progress signal is available, but the tested
way of using it did not improve the overall policy. Fresh confirmation did not
reproduce the slight advantage seen in a development pilot.

[Results](UtilityEcology/PROGRESS-REPORT.md) · [Offline review](UtilityEcology/progress-review.html)

### 31. Protected candidate trials · 12 October UTC

**Tried:** Give an alternative a finite starting budget, test it alongside the
existing service, and admit it only after a paid comparison.
**Found:** All eight runs retained useful common-source candidates and refused
noise-source candidates; admitted candidates answered their next 128 requests
correctly in total. Nevertheless, the existing error controller earned more
overall. Trials were slow, handled returning rare demand poorly, and did not
finish for the later useful source.
**Conclusion:** Protected trial and refusal mechanisms work. Their selective
success did not translate into a better overall learning policy; acquiring
enough relevant experience is still a limiting factor. One invalid development
run is retained and explicitly excluded from the evidence.

[Results](UtilityEcology/SEED-REPORT.md) · [Offline review](UtilityEcology/seed-review.html)

## What the collection supports

The strongest positive findings are specific: uncertainty and missing information
can remain visible; local contributions can be evaluated before receiving trust;
small learned services can combine and transfer; and selective information or
learning can improve return under declared costs.

The recurring failures are equally informative. More structure can cost more
without helping. Rare knowledge can disappear too soon, while harmful services
can keep attracting trials. Missing input can trap a frozen policy. Funding,
prediction error and learning progress each reveal something useful, but none
has yet provided a generally successful rule for allocating effort.

The collection does **not** establish open-ended intelligence, autonomous
invention of arbitrary hierarchies, or a general advantage over conventional
real-number methods. Most comparisons isolate a policy or information boundary
within Gojo. The Minds exact references check agreement with a specified
calculation, not a contest between number systems.

## Finding the evidence and running it

- The linked reports are the easiest material to review on GitHub. HTML viewers
  are saved presentations; download/open them locally where supported, or follow
  their project's serving instructions. Minds also has a
  [public notebook](https://gojo.inklingai.ca/minds/).
- [The experiments index](README.md) has setup, runtime requirements and archive
  restoration instructions. Outputs live beside some studies under `results/`,
  and for others under the repository's [`artifacts/`](../artifacts) folder.
  Use a new output directory when repeating a study.
- Protocols describe what was supplied and what was allowed to change. Results
  describe a captured version; later code fixes do not retroactively replace
  those outcomes. Test counts in historical reports are historical checks, not
  current totals or additional scientific replications.

Supporting folders are not additional learning experiments: `*.Tests` verifies
contracts; the Phototaxis browser/runner and the ConnectionAssembly and
NegotiatedPorts viewers expose their parent studies; the two resource audits
are included with their studies above. [ResearchArchive](ResearchArchive/README.md)
preserves results, including the compressed large forager record.
Private engineering integrations are excluded from this public experiment index.

For a conceptual synthesis of the sensing and control work, see
[Primitive numbers and control](PRIMITIVE-NUMBER-ANALYSIS.md).
