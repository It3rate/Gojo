# Frozen owner exchange

This is a post-hoc diagnostic on the primary test cohort whose results are already public within the local experiment. Its question is whether one owner's learned signal is useful, harmful, or interchangeable when it is plugged into a different writer. It selects no winner and makes no new held-out claim.

1. Take every primary random-search and evolutionary winner, across four specialist briefs and the mixed brief, four replicates each. Keep their identities even when their bytes coincide. Cross each of these 40 sensors with each of the 40 writers. Only the sensor rows move. The receiving writer keeps both its scan frame and its wire delay.
2. Each writer receives the full test bank for its training brief. Mixed writers receive all four brief banks, reported separately. Each panel has six seed families, twelve parent recipes per family and two sibling forms per parent. No training inputs, reserve inputs, newly selected samples, transfer calibration, or optimization are used.
3. Reproduce every diagonal in bytes and every recorded native test field, including the material mask, utility, idle comparison, and trace. Compare each transplanted pair's result to the writer's original partner, to that writer with its wire cut, and to idle. The cut and idle controls use the same episode and public receiver contract.
4. Report mean utility per panel (0–1000). Count a parent as improved/lost when the mean over its two sibling forms improves/drops by at least 20 points. Mark a panel decisive only if at least 48 of 72 parents move in the same direction. Distinguish a changed material result (final ink, final stock, accepted transfers) from a mere difference in the wire trace. Record the number of distinct material-result sequences per writer/brief across the 40 sensors, plus the numbers of swaps that match the original writer or cut across every episode. SHA-256 over ordered, fixed-width observation records provides an equivalence check.
5. For a stronger test of beneficial reuse, count foreign owners that beat the original **and** the cut decisively and have positive mean gain over idle. Report the number of donor owners and distinct donor byte tables meeting this criterion, plus recipients and evaluation briefs. Also report degradations and neutral cases. All 40 x 40 identities count, and self-pairs are excluded from foreign-transfer totals.

This measures compatibility within one learned public bit interface. It cannot distinguish all internal semantics from behavior on these seeds. The two forms of the same recipe are paired observations, and crossing many compatible tables on already seen data cannot establish an out-of-sample success rate.
