# What the path judges read

Two machines can draw the same contour even when only one actually joins its paths. The experiment asks separate questions about the ink a viewer can see, the history that produced it, and the join between one repetition and the next. Each judge returns its own observation. Different briefs can favor a sparse procession, a running meander or a branching ornament. A high score measures agreement with that brief. Human preferences for these particular outputs remain unmeasured.

## Where these questions come from

- **J — historical design prescription.** Jones's propositions 7, 9–12 discuss main lines before enrichment, relative scale, contrasts of directions and attachments to a parent stem. His prescriptions motivate a hierarchy and a variety of joints. Here we restrict drawing to straight segments. A sharp corner can be purposeful. [Jones, *The Grammar of Ornament*, propositions](https://www.readingdesign.org/grammar-of-ornament).
- **B — historical construction examples.** Meyer's *Bänder* describes ornament used for enclosing, separating and connecting. Plates 81–90 distinguish frets and interlaced bands. A continuous border can have a through-running path or a series of separate motifs. These are different seam contracts. [Meyer, *Handbuch der Ornamentik*, 1895, band chapter](https://digital.ub.uni-paderborn.de/download/pdf/7739354.pdf).
- **O — objects.** An Athenian Geometric krater combines meanders in registers with a frieze running around the vessel. A fourteenth-century textile fragment uses repeating zigzags to delimit diamond-shaped ground and enclose another form. These objects motivate tests of repetition, register, clearance and scale. The experiment neither reconstructs their imagery nor represents their cultural meanings. [Metropolitan Museum, krater study and photographs](https://resources.metmuseum.org/resources/metpublications/pdf/Ships_on_a_Wine_Dark_Sea_in_the_Age_of_Homer_The_Metropolitan_Museum_Journal_v_35_2000.pdf), [Metropolitan Museum, textile 90.5.429](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/443915).
- **S — mathematical correspondence.** A field study identified seven frieze symmetry types in the architecture of Pirgi and documented visually significant distinctions beyond the group label. We test particular transforms separately on finite drawn supports. Checking one nominated pitch and mirror axis does not classify an infinite frieze. [James, Kalisperis and James, *The Mathematics of Color-Reversing Decorative Friezes*](https://archive.bridgesmathart.org/2003/bridges2003-135.pdf).
- **P — measured preference under particular conditions.** Friedenberg et al. found that the two classes containing a horizontal mirror were especially preferred for the tested horizontal friezes. Larger features and visible global organization also mattered. In a separate study, designs varying in symmetry and complexity elicited different individual preference maps. The present experiment uses several briefs, with independently reported measurements and no fixed universal best symmetry or density. [Friedenberg et al., 2022](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/20416695221131112), [Mather et al., 2023](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-47835-w).
- **C — perceptual grouping.** Field, Hayes and Hess measured path detectability among distractors as alignment changed. This motivates inspecting how a line can be followed. It supplies no human rating for a particular Gojo joint or ornament. [Field, Hayes and Hess, 1993](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8447091/).
- **G — visual balance.** Hübner and Fillinger measured balance and preference separately on visual compositions. We keep vertical balance, distribution of visual mass, mirror correspondence and relative density as separate columns. [Hübner and Fillinger, 2016](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2016.00335/full).
- **A — authored trial convention.** Some questions test the mechanics directly, such as avoiding redundant deposition or identifying material contact at a joint. Numeric targets, weights and bounds below are experiment choices. These sources support asking the questions. They do not supply the constants or the results. [Earlier Gojo preference profiles](../../Gojo/docs/GojoPatternAestheticRules.md), [historical ornament rules](../../Gojo/docs/GojoHistoricalOrnamentRules.md).

## How to read the 28 scores

All scores are in [0,1]. An unavailable read is retained separately. The selection rule gives unavailable evidence zero earned utility while recording its availability count. A blank drawing has no aesthetic measurements. The nontrivial-drawing gate used for selection requires at least two edges in at least two different line axes.

A trace records ordered sites, their identity, owner, cell of emission, path direction and resolution. The observer receives a concrete calibration for each run. Its declared horizontal frame is 0 to number of cells × pitch. A line is split for measurement into exact collinear half-step intervals with direction-insensitive support. This accounts for a reversed stroke, partial duplication or a different segmentation of the same line. Diagonal intervals contribute their Euclidean length. The machine draws complete vectors and never sees the read table. Width or ink color of a rendering does not enter.

| Perspective | Score | Read at this resolution | Rationale and limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Craft | no-overdraw | Unique deposited length / all deposited length, including ink outside frame. | A. A reversed partial overlap counts in proportion to its length. Deliberate double inking can serve other media. |
| Craft | no-crossings | Fraction of edges untouched by an intersection strictly inside both participating segments. | B, A. Interlace may welcome crossings. There is no above/below read. |
| Craft | no-false-joins | Fraction of edges free of geometrically touching an independent endpoint. | A, C. Distinguishes an unbound T contact, a shared site and a proper crossing. Positive-length overlaps are handled separately. |
| Craft | forward-turns | At consecutive same-owner strokes sharing a site, a straight, 45° or 90° turn earns 1, 135° earns ½, reversal earns 0. | C, B, A. The tolerance deliberately admits angular frets. This is a local proxy for following a line. |
| Rhythm | period-match | Finite drawn geometry matched under translation by one nominated pitch. | B, S, O. Tests observed neighboring ink. A machine repeating the wrong motif can still score well. |
| Rhythm | turn-recurrence | Fraction of distinct (cell, two successive signed turns) observations whose turn phrase occurs in another cell. Two straight continuations alone do not qualify. | J, B, O. Independent owners can realize the same phrase. Incidence order matters. |
| Rhythm | turn-variety | Number of distinct signed turn increments, capped at three and divided by three. | B, P, A. Different programs can produce identical turn vocabularies. A lone repeated right angle can make an excellent fret. |
| Rhythm | extent-contrast | Variation among drawn step extents, favored in a moderate window. | J, P, A. Chebyshev step extent reflects a common slot calibration. Orthogonal and diagonal physical lengths can differ. |
| Rhythm | accent-alternation | Fraction of connected stroke triples whose middle step is strictly longer or shorter than both neighbors. | B, O, A. Equal-length sequences can be valuable under a different brief. |
| Field | band-reach | Union length of horizontal projection inside the nominal frame, divided by frame width. | P, O. A single horizontal stroke can reach every column. |
| Field | register-balance | Length-weighted vertical centroid near the midpoint of the visible vertical span. | G, O. A well-balanced pattern can have no mirror symmetry. The axis here is the drawing's occupied span. |
| Field | breathing-room | For each pair of adjacent visible crossings of a transverse section, gap / (0.18 pitch), capped at 1; average over observed pairs. | O, B, A. Read at x = integer + ½. Vertical strokes running parallel to the sections do not contribute. |
| Field | gap-evenness | 1/(1 + coefficient of variation) of these transverse clearances. Requires at least two measured gaps. | O, A. This is a selected perspective on whitespace. A large single unoccupied region offers no paired clearance. |
| Field | density-window | Unique length in frame × pitch / (frame width × max(visible height, pitch/2)); a trapezoidal window favors an intermediate range. | P, G, A. Measures line density, independent of SVG pen width. |
| Field | weight-contrast | Variation of unique stroke length among consecutive quarter-period bins. A moderate window rewards accents separated by quieter regions. | G, O, A. This is contrast of **spatial activity**. The pen has a single line weight and color. |
| Field | frame-respect | Fraction of unique drawn length inside the nominated horizontal frame. | B, A. The machine still draws overhanging segments in full. |
| Symmetry | parallel-mirror | Match against reflection in the horizontal center of the occupied band. | S, P. The enclosing vertical span must be positive. |
| Symmetry | end-mirror | Match against reflection through the vertical center of the complete declared frame. | S. A mirror at a different phase can remain undetected. |
| Symmetry | half-turn | Match against 180° rotation about the two nominated midlines. | S. A different rotation center can give a different result. |
| Symmetry | half-glide | Match after reflection across the occupied horizontal midline and translation by half a pitch. | S. Direct support comparison makes translation alone insufficient. |
| Topology | connected-run | Fraction of successive draws by the same owner that share a tip/start site. | J, C. Revisiting the same coordinate through a different site breaks this read. |
| Topology | branch-use | Fraction of cells with a witnessed branch leaving a site of degree ≥3 in the final undirected material graph. | J, A. Multiple identical traversals do not manufacture three neighbors. |
| Topology | merge-use | Fraction of cells with an explicit merge reaching a destination site of degree ≥2. | A. This counts activation of the actual merge contract. A contact without a binding cannot satisfy it. |
| Topology | loop-use | Fraction of cells that complete at least one undirected cycle when laying down edges in time order. Distinct site pairs count once. | O, A. A cycle can be geometrically degenerate; this read makes no positive-area claim. |
| Topology | owner-dialogue | Fraction of cells in which an edge touches a site already incident on another owner. | A. This measures material contact. A timing signal between private owners can influence a drawing without sharing a site. |
| Seam | boundary-match | Pair outgoing ink from one period with incoming ink from the touching side of the next actual period, by height and multiplicity. | B, O. One-sided offers score zero. No transverse offers means unavailable. A repeated detached motif may need no continuation. |
| Seam | tangent-match | Same comparison, additionally requiring the same unsigned supporting slope. | C, B, A. A deliberate corner can preserve the joint while changing the tangent. |
| Seam | shared-continuation | Same height and the same nonvirtual endpoint identity on the two sides. | A. Two cuts through the interiors of separate lines can align geometrically without a shared site. |

The symmetry and period scores use length-weighted Sørensen–Dice agreement. For translated reads both sides contribute only their mutually observable window. The horizontal reflection is through the midpoint of the *clipped* support's Y extent. A shift by half a pitch can land between native steps; the half-step comparison retains that distinction. A line lying on the seam has no transverse direction and supplies no seam port. The seam denominator counts all observed offers on both sides across internal boundaries. Pairing is one-to-one at each boundary. External ends are not joined by fiat.

The contrast windows ramp linearly between four breakpoints (zero, maximum, maximum, zero). For extent variation these are 0, .22, .75, 1.4. For density they are 0, 1.4, 3, 6.5. For quarter-period mass variation they are 0, .22, .75, 1.6. These choices are falsifiable in the ablations. Changing shared extent slots can perturb them while preserving the drawing's topology.

## Calibration checks and open questions

The separate JudgeChallenge fixtures compare drawn traces constructed independently of training. They measure partial reverse overlap, half-step diagonal crossing, matched ink with and without a shared fork, opposite edges of a seam, explicit versus coincident closure, a half-glide, transverse gap variation and an empty field. Each row retains the two independent observed values and its expected relation. They test whether the instrument reads what it claims. They supply no human preference labels.

The largest risk is circular evaluation. Training on a density window and reporting higher density-window scores establishes that the machine can respond to that window. Favoring a family, removing it during training, or testing a frozen machine on a different realization helps identify behaviors attributable to those pressures. A verdict about which looks better calls for independent comparison of drawings. The gallery includes disagreements and failures for that reason.
