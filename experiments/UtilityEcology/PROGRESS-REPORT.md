# Learning progress alongside error: results

Completed study: two retained development pilots, then **eight fresh paired
seeds, four variants and 65,536 world tasks**. Confirmation took 172 seconds,
including output and archive checks. No paid Jev calls or changes to world,
prices, buyer probing, renewal permission or initial funding.

## Conclusion

The native progress observation functions and stays explainable. It distinguishes
missing answers, known mistakes, improvement, regression and unchanged evidence.
The tested spending controller **does not improve on error gating alone**.

It cuts learning on noise, but also restricts learning where it is useful.
Against a control with the same budgets and retests but no progress connection,
the progress signal adds very little. We should keep the observation for further
experiments and revise this rule for withholding learning.

## What changed

Each provider has one local observer of its exposed prediction contract:

```text
progress = G(source contract, resource/error frames,
             older completed block, recent completed block, partial block,
             comparison version, observation count, finite receipts,
             mean/difference equations, coverage and loss witnesses)

permission = existing error request
             AND G(bounded bootstrap, fresh progress episode, paid retest)
```

Each block contains **16 actual bought physical observations**, including open
predictions. Known error is the existing native equality discrepancy: **0** for
agreement, **2** for disagreement. An open prediction has no numeric error; it
is retained as missing interpretation coverage. Unknown is not converted into
a successful zero.

Conditional mean error stays open until at least **eight known errors** exist
in the selected block. Native affine reads expose mean error on a 256 scale,
including projection residue. Progress is older conditional mean minus recent
conditional mean. Coverage change is recent availability minus older availability.
The two blocks within a comparison are disjoint; successive comparisons can
share a block and are not independent tests.

Decisions read completed prior blocks before current action and feedback. A
positive change of at least **128** can request a continuation, provided coverage
has not fallen. That is roughly a 25-point drop in conditional mismatch rate.
A coverage gain of 128 can independently request one: eight additional supported
answers out of sixteen. It establishes availability, not correctness or value.

The observer continues while learning is paused. Source calibration and the
normalized direction contract remain fixed when a model refines, so captured
pre-update errors remain comparable across the internal edit. No raw sample
class, desired-action label, phase or analyst noise classification enters it.

### Spending law

- Initial bootstrap: at most **16 actual paid grants** under the error gate.
- A fresh eligible comparison: one episode of at most **four grants**.
- Paid retest: at most four grants, with a new opportunity every **eight paid
  inspections** in confirmation. Pilot 01 used sixteen.
- One comparison version cannot repeatedly refill an episode through rereading.
- Error request, eligibility and funding are separate native reads. Eligibility
  is applied before charging the learning unit.

An episode can open while the error gate currently declines learning. Its
opening is therefore different from a funded grant and from an admitted update.
The old low-error schedule remains part of the error request. A progress value
does not rewrite a prediction, mint money or automatically construct a model.

All four variants contain the passive observer. Its abstract work is folded into
the existing one-unit inspection price; this is not a measurement of additional
CPU cost. Models, observer blocks, gates, episode budgets, aliases, equations and
payment routes remain native Gojo structure. C# constructs, reads and schedules
them; evaluation labels and display projections remain outside learner ports.

## Controls and protocol

| Variant | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Error only | Existing matched error gate; monitor cannot affect permission |
| Signed progress | Bounded controller with positive progress and coverage routes |
| Cycle only | Same bootstrap, budgets and retests, with progress routes removed |
| Change interest | Also permits a fresh negative error change, under the same coverage guard |

The change-interest variant tests whether regression should invite recovery. It
is an interest signal, not evidence of improvement. All variants use upfront
fee 2, buyer probing every eight source requests, renewal enabled and the earlier
opportunity gate disabled.

Seeds **800–807**, four unchanged phases of 512 world tasks. Paired variants
receive the same action-independent event tape. Primary endpoint is signed
physical gross minus burned work per **all** tasks, including holds, startup,
unsuccessful investigations and maintenance. Internal fees transfer resource
and are not charged again as group loss.

The two primary comparisons are signed versus error only, and signed versus
cycle only. Eight seed means receive descriptive unadjusted Student t(7)
intervals. These are not simultaneous significance guarantees or confidence in
individual native progress readings. Parameters were frozen before confirmation.

## Fresh results

| Variant | Net/task | Gross/task | Burn/task | Action coverage | Learning updates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Error only | **2.720** | 5.203 | 2.483 | 68.8% | 2,134 |
| Signed progress | 2.302 | 4.683 | 2.381 | 66.5% | 1,691 |
| Cycle only | 2.287 | 4.670 | 2.383 | 66.5% | 1,699 |
| Change interest | 2.302 | 4.683 | 2.381 | 66.5% | 1,691 |

| Paired change | Mean net/task | 95% descriptive interval | Positive seeds |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Signed minus error | **−0.418** | [−0.746, −0.090] | 1/8 |
| Signed minus cycle | +0.015 | [−0.020, +0.051] | 1/8 |
| Change minus signed | 0.000 | [0.000, 0.000] | 0/8 |

Signed progress saved about **21% of updates**, but only **4% of total burned
work**. Gross physical benefit fell by about 10%. Other work, such as purchased
interpretation and comparisons, continues even when training is paused.

Signed and cycle-only have equal net return in seven seeds. Only seed 807
improves return under signed progress. Their funding states also differ in seed
802 without changing its return. Signed and change-interest make identical
decision/resource traces in all eight seeds; no additional negative-change
episode is admitted in this campaign. That does not demonstrate a useful
regression mechanism.

## Where work was saved, and where learning was lost

### Noise

On source 2, which remains noisy throughout, paid grants fall **507 to 332**.
On source 3 before it becomes useful, they fall **272 to 204**. Combined, that is
**779 to 536**, about **31% less learning work on noisy tasks**.

Cycle-only achieves exactly the same noise decisions and return. Thus this saving
is accounted for by throttling and retesting in this trial, not a demonstrated
ability to recognize irreducible noise through progress.

### Returning rare utility

| Result after the rare source returns | Error only | Signed progress |
|---|---:|---:|
| Correct/answered, pooled | **887/888 (99.9%)** | 563/599 (94.0%) |
| Correct on first sixteen requests of each seed | **55/128** | 29/128 |
| Mean allocated net/rare request in final phase | **10.263** | 5.341 |

Both variants retain dormant knowledge. The difference arises under their
learning/purchasing dynamics, not a use-based model deletion. Coverage and
startup errors matter: high accuracy on the few answered tasks can hide the
failure to participate on returning requests.

### Newly useful information

Source 3 changes from noisy to ordinally useful without notifying learners.

| Final-phase result | Error only | Signed progress |
|---|---:|---:|
| Mean allocated net/request | +0.037 | **−0.497** |
| Profitable seeds | 5/8 | **0/8** |
| Action coverage | 26.5% | 18.9% |
| Paid learning grants | 213 | 84 |

Error only is itself inconsistent here; a near-zero mean does not establish
reliable discovery. The progress controller makes re-entry worse. All **1,691
signed learning requests were funded**, so direct attention refusal is not the
explanation for its deficits in these fresh runs. It suppresses **557** error
requests before funding is considered. Buyer acquisition, inherited evidence,
construction/reset behavior and restricted learning still interact.

Source-local net allocates shared maintenance and other burns to the world task
when incurred. It is not a causal marginal service profit. Whole-world net and
the directly attributed grant/update counts avoid that interpretation.

## Did progress itself do anything?

Signed opens **six positive-error-progress episodes**, **four coverage episodes**
and **298 retests**. Cycle-only opens 310 retests. Most opportunities to learn
therefore arise through the schedule, not the progress connection.

Nine of the ten progress/coverage trigger steps make the same funded choice as
cycle-only: seven both grant, two both decline because error does not currently
request work. A trigger with no immediate grant is still an inspectable native
event; it cannot be counted as an extra useful update.

The exception is **seed 807, common source, tick 563**: signed grants while cycle
pauses. Its common adaptation reaches a first run of sixteen correct requests
at tick **627**, versus **680** for cycle-only. This is a concrete example where
the information connection changes useful behavior. It occurs in one seed and
does not establish an overall benefit.

Two of six positive episodes occur on externally known noise:

- Seed 804, source 3, tick 1047, before the useful transition: progress **137**.
- Seed 806, source 2, tick 753: progress **154**.

Another coverage episode occurs on noise. A short-window improvement or more
confident answer is not proof that the underlying signal has become learnable.
The controller's finite budget prevents a single favorable comparison from
funding indefinite work, but does not remove sampling or selection effects.

## Development runs kept separate

Pilot 01 used seeds 700–701 and retest interval 16. Signed mean return was
2.092 versus error-only 2.472. Noise work fell, but returning and newly useful
sources lost learning opportunities.

The predeclared single adjustment was retest interval **16 to 8**. Pilot 02
reused the same paired tapes to isolate that adjustment: signed mean 2.481,
error-only 2.472. Signed, cycle-only and change-interest had identical behavior
on both seeds. Those pilot means do not establish a signal benefit and receive
no confidence interval.

We froze interval 8 and every other setting, then used fresh seeds. Confirmation
did not reproduce the pilot's slight advantage. Both pilots, their manifests,
source hashes, saved models and full traces remain in their original folders.

## Verification

- **2,298 full-solution tests passed**, zero failures, including **98 experiment
  tests**. Unique per-project TRX files and the complete console log are retained.
- Fifteen observer tests cover open versus zero, signed units, completed disjoint
  blocks, coverage changes, incompatible reads, finite duplicate exclusion,
  transport, finite projection residue and bounded observer history.
- Ten gate tests cover paid bootstrap/episode limits, stale versions, retesting,
  observation during pause, funding and coupled native replay.
- Integration tests show the passive observer preserves existing actions, costs
  and funding; private monotone sensor encodings still produce the same behavior.
- Every one of **65,536 monetary identities**, trace/row sum and paired tape check
  passed. Independent reconstruction agrees with every published progress,
  recent-error, coverage, version and observation-count value.
- All **four actual saved first-seed graphs** pass signature, money, alias,
  retained-anchor/presence publication and paid gate-continuation checks.

Saved replay uses a declared ordered fixture for retained samples. It does not
reconnect novel physical samples to a restored private instrument dictionary.
The progress observer rejects duplicates only inside its finite 64-receipt
window. Provider-level asynchronous replay admission is still a broader gap;
the synchronous study delivers each physical event once.

Bounded observer history does not prove bounded total provenance for an entire
indefinitely running economy. Work prices remain abstract. No wall-clock speedup,
generalized noise detector or optimal value-of-computation policy is claimed.

## What I would try next

**Keep the progress read; revise the gate that uses it.**

This spending law can withhold the work needed to generate measurable progress.
Even when improvement is observed, it may arrive after the error gate already
considers the response sufficient. That is consistent with these traces; it is
a design diagnosis, not a separately isolated causal experiment.

A stronger next study would:

1. Give a new structural hypothesis a protected, paid trial episode before
   requiring improvement to continue. Connect the trial to unresolved tension
   and a candidate edit, so it can be inspected and compared with the current
   structure. Avoid blanket suspension of every high-error context.
2. Compare old and candidate interpretations on the **same retained opaque
   experiences**, with grounded outcomes and paid comparison queries. That
   controls more of the changing sample mixture than adjacent windows do.
3. Use measured **decision benefit minus work** to admit the candidate, while
   allowing a coarse unchanged model to keep operating. A decreasing prediction
   error alone need not justify spending or structural replacement.

These are future tests, not changes made after confirmation. The present result
helps separate a useful observation capability from an unsuccessful controller.

## Research and runnable artifacts

[Oudeyer, Kaplan and Hafner](https://www.pyoudeyer.com/ims.pdf) motivate local
learning-progress observations rather than raw error maximization.
[Baranes and Oudeyer](https://www.pyoudeyer.com/ActiveGoalExploration-RAS-2013.pdf)
motivate interest in changes of competence, including recovery. The
[same-history comparison discussed by Schmidhuber](https://people.idsia.ch/~juergen/driven2008.pdf)
is stronger than this consecutive-window trend. We do not implement those full
systems or inherit their guarantees.
