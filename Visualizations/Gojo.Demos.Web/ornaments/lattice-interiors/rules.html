Hard rejection rules

Every actual atomic face has exactly one owner. Each merged interior is connected through shared edges, has one closed simple boundary, and is composed of valid convex atoms whose interiors do not overlap in the declared embedding. Shared contacts require actual shared edges and sites. The native packet retains the participating atoms, boundary darts, binding basis and rejected claims.

This is stronger than checking that two lines do not retrace. The coloured interiors must be a partition of the supplied atomic domain. Finite frontiers are retained; coverage of the entire plane is a separate claim.

Preferences that still need judgement

Broad turns can define a new cell. Small repeated excursions that quickly return to the same heading can refine an old edge without establishing a new lattice shape. The shape reader retains both kinds of turning evidence; a many-pointed star is not rejected merely for alternating convex and reflex corners.

The gallery marks a boundary-detail cue when there are at least three fine same-heading returns and their fraction exceeds 35%. It does not convert this exploratory threshold into a hard geometry law. Human ratings and notes can help revise it.

Seams are a two-sided question

Shape-first cases retain neighbours' competing atomic claims. Each contested atom needs an explicit selected owner; the losing proposal remains blocked. The accepted ownership is checked again for connected cells and sound embedding. An accepted choice does not by itself prove a valid shape.

No numeric carriers, bitmap segmentation, global pattern recognizer or hidden geometric join inference is used here. Formal counts and shape ratios are derived observation reads.
