Sheet A · row 1 · A1

Running square key

Authored interpretation

Short inward hooks repeat between two separate border rails. Their outside reaches advance the band while the hooks turn back into each repeat.

View reference sheet A

All recorded paths. Select a group or a colored line to isolate its contribution.

One way to construct it

Build one angular reach word: advance, rise, advance, descend partway, return inward, and descend to the next handoff. Repeat it by translation. Keep the framing rails separate from the ornament course.

Other possible engines

  • Place repeated hook cells and connect their exposed ends.
  • Read alternating upper and lower hooks as two coordinated collections; their handoffs must still be supplied.

Sheet A · row 2 · A2

Opposed short hooks

Authored interpretation

Shallow hooks alternate between upper and lower portions of the strip. Short returns and longer horizontal reaches give a compact interlocking rhythm.

View reference sheet A

All recorded paths. Select a group or a colored line to isolate its contribution.

One way to construct it

Make a short hooked path, place an opposed partner at the next horizontal phase, and repeat the pair. The partnership can be read independently from the top and bottom border rails.

Other possible engines

  • Execute one continuous word containing both upper and lower hooks.
  • Use two translated, vertically opposed streams with an explicit phase relation.

What the diagram leaves open

A vertical reflection alone may not align the two hooks; translation or a different start phase may also be needed. Connectedness belongs to actual handoffs, not close-looking corners.

Sheet A · row 3 · A3

Key with four-window inserts

Authored interpretation

Interleaving angular key routes alternate with small square inserts containing four white windows. The hooked routes have visible crossing sites, and long horizontal rails frame the composition.

View reference sheet A

All recorded paths. Select a group or a colored line to isolate its contribution.

One way to construct it

Repeat one ordered key word in two courses separated by half its horizontal repeat. Use a separate placement stream for the square inserts. Construct each insert as an outer square with a vertical and horizontal divider, retaining those material contacts without duplicating the dividers.

Other possible engines

  • Repeat a compound group containing both phased key routes and their divided-square inserts.
  • Drive the key pair and the inserts from separate streams tied to the same repeat surface.
half-period course pairindependent insert streamdivided enclosure

What the diagram leaves open

The chosen through-routing at key crossings is not established by black ink alone. Four white windows do not imply four independent square pens. Exact attachment of inserts to nearby key strokes remains a chosen reconstruction premise.

            Straight-line ornament: parts, pairs, and possible engines

            What this study is trying to show

            The same ornament can be read in several useful ways. Follow one strand and there is a sequence of turns. Select a second strand and a mirrored pair may appear. Read the space between them and there may be a diamond, a long hexagon, or a winding corridor. None of these reads needs to erase the others.

            This study breaks down every wholly straight-edged pattern in the three supplied reference sheets. There are 18 selected rows and 10 explicitly deferred rows. Each selected row has an authored centre-line interpretation, a short verbal construction, alternative generating ideas, and a statement of what the image does not establish. Duplicate-looking rows remain in the source index so that coverage is visible; they are not counted as independent discoveries.

            Start by selecting CR4, the angular weave, then isolate its outer pair and inner pair before examining all four strands together. Next compare the simple battlement B5, the zigzag CL5, the continuous winding A5 and finite coils CL2, and the crossing bands B4 and CL6. These show different uses of repetition, opposed orientation, start/stop participation, and multiple paths.

            The reference sheets are evidence supplied by the user. The reconstructions are declared idealizations, not automatic image traces, exact measurements, recovered pen histories, or proof of the original designers' intentions. Source colors, printed thickness, watermarks, and small raster irregularities are not part of the centre-line grammar. The source images remain available intact in the web study for comparison.

            What is being encoded

            A centre-line construction retains connected frames, ordered sections, shared endpoint handoffs where intended, separate path ownership, and repeat or transform correspondence. The public coordinates and path labels are presentation reads. Their labels do not supply native topology.

            A useful schematic is:

            source frame -> ordered path sections -> exposed start/end sites
                           |
                           +-> selected pair -> transform correspondence
                           |
                           +-> larger group -> repeat correspondence

            A pair is an additional read over the same participating sections. It does not copy the material or weld the two paths. A visible crossing remains different from an actual junction. An exposed end can be a real termination, a crop boundary, or an unresolved continuation; those interpretations must remain separate.

            The centre lines can later acquire ribbon width. That expansion is a further construction with side correspondence and clearance requirements. It can close gaps, introduce overlaps, or alter the connected white regions, so a thin-line symmetry is not automatically a proof about every possible ribbon thickness.

            Vertical reflection is a particularly useful shared construction

            Take a path advancing through a band. In a chosen frame, one partner can use the same ordered instructions while reversing the vertical basis. A rise becomes a fall, while horizontal advance remains horizontal advance. In a display frame with middle axis at zero, this reads as (x, y) -> (x, -y).

            That operation is different from reading the path from its end back to its start. It is also different from reversing flow on a carrier or swapping the over/under order at crossings. Those changes can be combined, but each needs its own correspondence.

            For the angular weave, reflected members of each pair are the strongest same-instruction comparison. The outer pair and inner pair belong to a similar construction family, but their detailed reach and turn words are not asserted to be identical. A coarse read can retain their common pattern of advance, turn, return, and repeat while leaving their additional detail available.

            A horizontal run directly on the reflection axis is fixed by reflection. If two independently owned paths both draw that run, their projected ink overlaps for a positive length. Avoiding such a run is therefore a useful anti-overdraw rule for a paired-strand engine. It is not a universal prohibition on horizontal flow at zero. A shared carrier, a non-draining observation, or a deliberately coincident read would be a different construction.

            Reflection plus that anti-overdraw rule does not alone guarantee diamonds or hexagons. The sequence of encounters, plateaus, turns, and matching endpoints must also support the enclosing boundary. These are strong candidate engines, not a substitute for checking their resulting structure.

            Changing a pair needs the same care. Moving one partner can break reflection about the original axis while preserving reflection about a shifted axis. Changing phase can replace a direct reflection with a glide correspondence. Changing only one extent may break exact matching but preserve the ordered word. A report must say which of these questions it checked.

            The source breakdown also reveals a useful neighboring family. In the selected through-routing reads of A3, A4, and B3, two copies of the same word in the same vertical frame interleave after a half-period horizontal translation. B8 uses a horizontal and vertical translation of one square-wave word, plus a separate spot stream. These pairs should not be forced into the vertical-reflection explanation merely because their combined silhouette has opposed-looking parts. They share the broader idea of a word, a partner correspondence, and a relative phase, while differing in the actual transform.

            How to read the alternatives

            The alternatives below are candidate descriptions, not a ranking of original construction histories. Several may reproduce the same visible support while retaining different path ownership, connections, controllers, or future possibilities. Before calling two engines interchangeable, compare their exposed ports, end conditions, crossings, and retained uncertainty as well as their picture.

            A compact description is useful when it pays for its shared word, placements, transform relations, and exceptions. It does not establish a probability by itself. Reuse across other patterns can eventually provide evidence for a useful default, but this source survey does not train such a preference.

            Descriptions of white regions are observations unless an explicit region reader has verified them. Selecting an outer or inner strand pair can reveal a simpler boundary candidate without proving that every visible opening has been mechanically extracted. This study does not report an exhaustive symmetry search, a learned beauty score, or a recovered historical machine.

            What the collection suggests

            Several reusable ideas recur without requiring one mandatory taxonomy:

            • A word can alternate between two vertical states, as in a battlement or zigzag.
            • Reflected hooks can be placed in a shared phase relation, making a larger word.
            • An inward turn sequence can stop; repetition then starts a new independent pen.
            • A motif and its insert can have separate placement streams while sharing a repeat.
            • Two diagonal families can form crossings and white openings without becoming one material junction graph.
            • A pair of paths can become the participant in a larger pair relation, as in the angular weave.

            These labels summarize connected constructions. They do not replace the constructions. The source survey is useful because it gives several places to try the same mechanism and several places where that mechanism is insufficient.
