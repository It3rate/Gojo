Gojo Read Spine

This note describes the current common read, witness, basis, and refinement shape in Gojo. It is meant to sit beside the ConceptSurface notes as a compact orientation document for the kernel direction.

The short version:

  • Gojo meaning lives in carriers, locations, relations, unit shapes, residue, tension, and timelines.
  • A helper can build or inspect structure, but it should not become the source of meaning.
  • A read is not only a value. A read is the selected structure, the surface or basis used to read it, the result, the unresolved residue or tension, and the provenance that explains how the result was obtained.
  • Incomplete reads are useful reads. A low-resolution read should preserve what did not settle instead of hiding it.

Why This Exists

Several parts of Gojo now need the same underlying pattern:

  • equation prediction and resolution search
  • concept focus reads
  • carrier axis, face, and lane reads
  • boundary-flow route projection
  • participation settlement and boolean-style reads
  • accepted and blocked transfer reads
  • target settlement and shared capacity timelines

Before the common shape, those areas were telling similar stories in local vocabularies. The goal is to make the story structurally common without making it code-owned. Equation coefficients, geometry parameters, lane transitions, capacity allocation, and later mechanical design constraints should all be able to say:

this source
read through this surface or basis
produced this result
left this residue or tension
with this provenance

That gives ML, search, RL, genetic algorithms, and hand-authored experiments a common target without forcing every domain concept into one symbolic model.

ReadWitness

ReadWitness is the most general read skeleton.

ReadWitness
  selected source
  read surface
  result
  residue or tension
  provenance

Use it when a local read should remain inspectable by other parts of the system. The selected source, read surface, result, residue, and provenance are ordinary Gojo elements. The helper only packages them as an ordered relation and reads them back.

The important behavior is not the class name. The important behavior is that a result can be traversed back to the structure and resolution that produced it. Two equal visible results can therefore remain different if their route, basis, residue, timeline, or source participation differs.

Examples:

  • An equation sample can expose a prediction witness and a claim witness.
  • A concept focus can expose the selected source, focus surface, read result, residue, and provenance.
  • A route projection can expose source-basis evidence, projection relation, target-basis evidence, and route residue.
  • A transfer can expose the accepted value and blocked value as separate witnesses.
  • A boolean-style support read can expose selected participants, scope, boundary evidence, occupied flow lanes, partitioned cells, settled result, and residue.

For the cross-grade geometry/concept direction, see ParticipationSettlementReads.md. That note treats boolean-style names as low-resolution projections over occupied flow or participation, not as operations on bare boundary objects.

BasisReadSurface

BasisReadSurface is the common read shape for basis-bearing structure.

BasisReadSurface
  selected source
  axis or face surface
  lane basis
  metric evidence
  residue or tension
  provenance

This is where the axis, face, and lane split becomes foundational.

An axis says which carrier-like dimension or domain is being read. It carries boundary and flow possibility at low resolution.

A face says which oriented participation is being encountered or exposed. Positive, negative, forward, inverse, whole, no-flow, and both-flow evidence should stay visible as structure rather than being collapsed into prose.

A lane says which combined boundary and flow participation is legal for a value. A lane basis can say that a carrier supports only positive-forward values, full signed forward values, a bounded whole with no flow, both flow directions, and so on.

Metric evidence is the bridge toward embodiment without jumping straight to CAD. It can be a point, span, projected coordinate, capacity amount, radius, angle, pitch, profile evidence, or an unresolved placeholder. The basis read can exist before the metric evidence is fully settled.

BasisReadSurface can project into ReadWitness when a caller wants the general skeleton:

selected source
read through axis or face surface
produced lane basis
left residue or tension
with provenance

TimeStepSequence

TimeStepSequence is the shared structure for ordered transitions.

TimeStepSequence
  clock origin
  initial evidence
  step*

Step
  time span
  before evidence
  transition surface
  after evidence
  residue

A step can be read as a ReadWitness:

before evidence
read through transition surface
produced after evidence
left residue
with time span provenance

This lets refinement, route projection, transfer, settlement, repeated operation follow, tournaments, and equation searches share the same temporal shape. The timeline is not only a log. It is evidence that can be inspected, compared, or used as provenance by a later read.

FocusSurface

FocusSurface is the first common foothold for structural selection. It is not a UI selection object. It records a local focus over connected structure:

FocusSurface
  source structure
  focus basis
  selected participation
  carry-forward complement
  replacement or anti-participation
  correspondence
  residue or tension
  provenance or timeline

The important use case is partial selection. A rounded rectangle corner does not only select two whole edges. It selects subspans near a corner relation, carries forward the rest of the edges, replaces the sharp participation with a transition, and preserves unresolved radius/tangent residue.

Focus reads are exact ordered surfaces. A relation does not become focus merely because nested structure happens to contain eight readable fields. This keeps selection from being simulated by nearby structure: the focus surface itself must be present and readable.

Focus can also be sequenced without inventing a separate sequencer. A timed step can use a focus surface as its transition evidence:

before structure
  read through focus surface
  produces after structure
  leaves residue
  during this time span

That lets a structure remain valid between partial refinements. One corner can settle before another corner, a field color can change between them, and each intermediate state can still be read.

Focus can project across ladder rungs when correspondence is available:

lower-rung focus
  + correspondence bindings
  + target source
  -> projected focus
  + projection residue

The projection is structural evidence, not a hidden lookup. Pieces that do not map remain visible in the projected focus and can be carried as residue or tension.

A focus can also create a result witness:

source structure
  read through focus surface
  produces result structure
  leaves residue
  with provenance

This result witness can be wrapped by RefinementRead, which makes focus the bridge from selection into structural ladders.

TransformSurface

TransformSurface is the first common foothold for reusable structural transforms. A focus surface says what is selected in one source. A transform surface says what kind of instruction can be applied to a selection without destroying the original historical source.

TransformSurface
  input ports
  operation structure
  output ports
  residue policy
  provenance

Operation structure is also ordinary relation structure. The first low-level operation reads are:

map / carry       input -> output
introduce         unknown -> output
remove            input -> unknown
split             input -> output ports
fold              input ports -> output
interpolate       first, second, grade/basis ports -> output

These are not symbolic operation kinds. They are relation shapes that imply correspondence. A transform application can derive bindings from them when explicit bindings are unresolved, while still recording the exact bindings that settled in the application and patch witness.

Executable operation surfaces can also be read through application bindings to produce an operation-output witness:

OperationOutput
  application
  operation
  bound input evidence
  operation read
  output target surface
  result
  residue or tension
  provenance

This is the first bridge from operation structure to settled output evidence. The creator applies the application's binding relations to the operation port surface, asks the core operation reader for the output, and records the output target surface as structure. A simple read records one target; a product expansion records an ordered surface of product-term targets. Dependency reads can then use any child target even when it is only one element inside a larger result frame. Static map/carry/fold shapes still derive correspondence; executable operation surfaces additionally produce a witnessed output surface.

Concrete example: a transform application can bind fused x faces and fused y faces through a product operation over an area carrier. The operation read can expose four term targets such as area^-:8, area^-:10, area^-:12, and area:15. A later application can consume only area^-:12; dependency reading can now find that exact term through the producer's operation-output witness.

The transform is the reusable instruction. The application is the concrete settlement of that instruction against a real focus:

TransformApplication
  transform
  focus
  parameter bindings
  result
  patch witness
  read witness
  residue or tension
  provenance

Applications can be replayed against a changed focus without treating the old after-state as the operation:

Replay
  previous application
  new focus
  new application
  stale patch or residue
  residue or tension
  provenance

When the previous patch no longer matches the new focus, stale evidence is recorded:

StalePatch
  previous application
  previous patch
  previous focus
  new focus
  residue or tension
  provenance

The old patch is not destroyed or overwritten. It remains evidence of what settled before, while the replay records the new application of the same transform instruction.

Partial replay records the slice of history that was reused and the slice that was replayed:

PartialReplay
  previous application frame
  carried applications
  replay frame
  current application frame
  residue or tension
  provenance

The current read orders the result through the previous application frame. Carried applications remain unchanged, replay records replace their previous applications, and unmentioned applications are pending. This can still express the old prefix case, but it can also carry an independent later application after a middle application has been replayed.

Dependency partial replay can leave a dependent consumer pending without hiding why it is absent from the current frame:

StaleDependency
  dependency
  producer replay
  stale consumer application
  residue or tension
  provenance

This is not a side-table marker. It is a relation witness that the dependency's producer has been replayed while the consumer is still the previous application. That witness can seed the next replay pass: a later consumer replay can resume from the stale dependency residue and rebuild the current frame without re-reading the original affected slice. Resume is not prefix-only: if several consumers are stale, a replay can match any stale consumer by structure and the unmatched consumers remain as stale dependency residue.

The first affected-slice read starts from focus-change evidence:

FocusChange
  previous focus
  new focus
  correspondence
  residue or tension
  provenance

AffectedSlice
  previous application frame
  focus change
  carried prefix
  affected application suffix
  residue or tension
  provenance

The affected slice is the suffix beginning at the first application whose focus matches the previous focus in the change. This is still a low-resolution dependency read. It does not know that a later application is independent; it preserves the conservative suffix so replay can proceed without losing evidence.

An affected slice can create partial replay directly. The carried prefix is read from the affected slice, and each replay record must replace the affected applications in order. Code does not have to restate the prefix once the slice has been discovered.

The first sharper application dependency read is output-to-input based:

ApplicationDependency
  producer application
  consumer application
  dependency evidence
  residue or tension
  provenance

The automatic creator is conservative. It derives evidence when producer output structure, such as a replacement or result, is consumed by the next application. The read now walks nested operation inputs, focus participation, patch participation, and correspondence bindings, so an output can be discovered inside a transform operation or a correspondence frame. It still does not treat a shared source as dependency evidence by itself; producer result frames are matched as whole results unless output-side structure is visible through the patch, output ports, correspondence, or an operation-output witness. The exact reader can also recognize explicit binding evidence from a producer output to a consumer input, which lets the structure say "this feeds that" even when the two sides are not the same object. This is still a local read over application, focus, patch, operation, and binding structure, not a global classifier or dependency side table.

Dependency-aware affected slices use that evidence:

ApplicationDependencyFrame
  dependency...

DependencyAffectedSlice
  previous application frame
  focus change
  dependency frame
  carried applications
  affected applications
  residue or tension
  provenance

The changed application and transitive consumers are affected. Applications in the frame that do not consume the changed chain are carried, even when they appear later in the previous frame. The focus change should point to the actual focus relation held by the application; a duplicate focus with the same interior values is only a lookalike, not the same structural site.

Concrete example: application A produces the four area terms above, application B consumes area^-:12, and later application C is independent. If A is replayed with a changed input so that the consumed term is now area^-:16, the dependency-aware affected slice reads A and B as affected while carrying C. Partial replay can then replace A and B in frame order and leave C unchanged. If only A has been replayed so far, the current frame can contain A' and C, while the partial replay residue contains StaleDependency(A -> B, A', B). Supplying B -> B' later can resume directly from that residue, producing A', B', and C with the stale dependency cleared. With multiple stale consumers, replaying only D first can produce A', C, and D' while B remains as stale dependency residue; replaying B later settles the whole chain.

Partial replay can also be read into a structural ladder rung. The previous application frame is the rung source, the partial replay itself is the focus and correspondence evidence, the current application frame is the rung result, and any stale dependency residue remains attached to the rung. That lets a ladder ask "what is currently settled?" without needing to know every replay detail, while still preserving unresolved dependent consumers as visible tension.

The patch witness is the temporal/change relation. It records the source read through the transform and keeps the selected participation, carried complement, replacement or introduction, correspondence, result, residue, and provenance visible:

Patch
  source structure
  transform
  focus
  selected participation
  carried complement
  replacement or introduction
  correspondence
  result
  residue or tension
  provenance

This separates "the reusable operation" from "what happened when it was applied." That distinction matters for editable structural histories: a later read can keep the transform and re-apply it to a changed focus instead of treating the old after-state as the operation itself.

StructuralLadderSurface

StructuralLadderSurface is the first executable relation shape for structural and conceptual ladders. A rung is a complete structure plus optional focus, result, correspondence, residue, witness, refinement, and provenance evidence:

Rung
  source structure
  focus surface, partial replay, or unresolved focus
  result structure
  correspondence
  residue or tension
  result witness
  refinement read
  provenance or timeline

A ladder then carries:

StructuralLadderSurface
  base rung
  rung frame
  history or timeline
  residue or tension
  provenance

The rung frame is an ordered relation of readable rungs. The timeline uses the shared TimeStepSequence shape, where each step carries transition evidence and an after-rung as the settled state. Transition evidence can be a raw focus surface for older focus-step reads, or a TransformApplication when the step should preserve the reusable transform instruction separately from the patch that settled.

The current implementation deliberately reads rungs and ladders as exact ordered surfaces. This is not a symbolic tag. It is a guard against false reads where a broad recursive ordered-group inspection mines nested participant structure and shifts fields. If the exact rung surface is not present, the read should fail or fall back to lower-resolution evidence rather than inventing a ladder from nearby structure.

The explicit ladder constructor takes base rung, rung frame, timeline/history, residue, and provenance. The shorthand for "base plus these rungs, with no timeline yet" is named CreateWithRungs so it cannot steal full constructor calls where all evidence happens to be relation-shaped.

Refined rungs should be created from focus when possible. Rung remains the raw relation-shaped constructor, but TryCreateRefinedRungFromFocus reads the source directly from the focus surface and then creates the rung, result witness, and refinement read. That path makes the focus/selection relation the entry point for structural adjustment instead of asking code to restate the source and selection intent separately.

Transform replay has a parallel entry point: TryCreateRungFromPartialReplay. It reads PartialReplay as the transition surface: previous applications become the source, current applications become the result, and replay residue remains rung residue. This gives structural ladders a current-state read over an in-progress replay without crystallizing stale dependencies away.

Rounded Corner Example

Rounded-corner refinement is now an example of the generic transform path, not a core surface. It does not introduce a special corner object. It builds a sharp-corner source, a focus surface, a reusable transform instruction, and a transform application:

sharp-corner source
  incoming edge
  corner join
  outgoing edge

focus
  source: sharp-corner source
  basis: corner join
  participation: two partial spans near the corner
  complement: carried incoming and outgoing edge spans
  replacement: rounded transition
  correspondence: tangent bindings
  residue: tangent/radius/transition mismatch
  provenance

transform
  input ports: selected incoming/outgoing spans
  operation: selected spans -> rounded transition with tangent evidence
  output ports: carried incoming, transition, carried outgoing

patch / result
  incoming carry
  rounded transition
  outgoing carry
  correspondence

The application produces the patch witness. A refined rung can then be created from that transform application, with the application stored as correspondence evidence. This keeps the transform replayable while the patch records the actual before/focus/result settlement.

RefinementRead

RefinementRead is the common relation-shaped refinement record.

RefinementRead
  parameter
  binding or candidate choice
  read witness
  residue or tension
  timeline or provenance

This is deliberately not a surface object in the same sense as ConceptSurface.

ConceptSurface is a larger concept-level foothold: selected structure, topology, focus, flow, parameterization, constraints, settlement, residue, and refinement timeline. It describes the shape of a conceptual read over connected structure.

RefinementRead is narrower. It records one refinement attempt: this parameter was bound this way, the read witness says what happened, this residue or tension remained, and this timeline or provenance explains the attempt.

The name is meant to keep the vocabulary crisp. This helper is part of the common read grammar, not a ConceptSurface-style topology/focus/flow surface.

The structural rule is the same either way: the refinement helper must not own refinement meaning. The parameter, binding, witness, residue, and timeline must remain traversable as ordinary Gojo structure.

How The Shapes Compose

The current mechanical/concept stack can be read as a chain:

CarrierAxisSurface
  -> BasisReadSurface
  -> ReadWitness

BoundaryFlowValueRoute
  -> source BasisReadSurface
  -> target BasisReadSurface
  -> projection ReadWitness

BoundaryFlowValueTransfer
  -> accepted ReadWitness
  -> blocked ReadWitness

BoundaryFlowTargetSettlement
  -> settlement ReadWitness
  -> TimeStepSequence

BoundaryFlowSharedCapacityTransfer
  -> allocation ReadWitness
  -> allocation TimeStepSequence

Equation resolution follows the same larger pattern:

candidate parameter binding
  -> prediction or claim ReadWitness
  -> RefinementRead
  -> TimeStepSequence

This is the "two ladders become one" idea. Numeric coefficients and geometric parameters should not need unrelated refinement stories. A coefficient binding, corner radius binding, lane transition binding, pitch binding, or capacity binding can all be read as parameter, candidate/binding, witness, residue, and timeline.

Encoding Mechanical Concepts

For a high-level statement such as:

a table has two square corners and two rounded corners

Gojo should not start by storing that sentence as the concept. The sentence is a requested read level. A useful encoded structure would expose:

  • the selected table surface or boundary structure
  • countable corner feature sites
  • edge/face evidence entering and leaving each corner
  • lane basis evidence for the local boundary and flow participation
  • metric evidence where available, such as angle, radius, arc span, or tangent transition
  • a constraint or settlement surface saying which feature reads satisfy the requested count
  • witnesses for each feature read
  • residue where the structure is ambiguous, underspecified, incompatible, or lower resolution than requested

A square corner might be read as an orthogonal meeting with unresolved or near-zero radius evidence. A rounded corner might be read as a radial transition between edge faces with radius or arc-span evidence. Both are still local feature reads over connected structure, not tags that replace the structure.

The same high-level claim may decode into many valid designs. That is not a problem. It is important training and search signal. Two designs can satisfy the same sentence while preserving different provenance, proportions, radii, edge participation, construction histories, and residue.

Round-Trip Levels

ML targets and experiments should name the decode level they are trying to produce.

Level Target
L0 Concept tags or low-resolution reads over existing structure.
L1 Parameterized concept, basis, feature, and constraint surfaces.
L2 Settled carriers, spans, metric relations, witnesses, residue, and timelines.
L3 External CAD, B-rep, mesh, drawing, or solver reference connected back to Gojo witnesses.

The long-term goal is not to replace CAD kernels with prose. The goal is to make a mathematical concept structure that can move between high-level reads and low-level embodiment, then connect to external CAD when the requested level requires it.

Experiment Shape

A real experiment should usually contain these structural parts:

  • import or generation provenance
  • selected structure
  • requested read level
  • basis reads for axes, faces, lanes, and metric evidence
  • feature inventory as connected repeated structure
  • constraint or settlement surface
  • read witnesses for successful, partial, and failed reads
  • residue and tension preserved as first-class evidence
  • optional external reference, such as CAD, mesh, drawing, text, or dataset id

Evaluation should reward structural settlement, not only final tags. Useful signals include:

  • how much of the requested read settled
  • which residue remained
  • whether equal visible outputs came from distinct provenance
  • whether a low-resolution read can refine without losing its earlier evidence
  • whether a decoded structure can be re-read at the requested level
  • whether incompatible states remain inspectable instead of disappearing

Working Rules

Build the connected Gojo structure first. Add local helpers only after the meaning is already present in carriers, relations, unit shapes, residue, tension, and timelines.

Keep axis, face, lane, metric evidence, and concept reads separate until a read explicitly binds them. Collapsing them too early makes high-level mechanical claims hard to refine and low-level embodied structure hard to recover.

Prefer local discovery. A carrier, relation, feature site, route, or capacity surface should carry enough structure to be read locally. Global classifiers can be useful around the edges, but they should not become the canonical source of meaning.

Treat unresolved structure as useful. Failed reads, partial reads, low resolution, capacity overflow, blocked transfer, projection residue, and ambiguous feature evidence are all part of the design state.

Preserve provenance for "equal but different". This matters for reinforcement learning, genetic search, dataset generation, concept compression, and round-trip testing.

When naming new helpers, prefer names that identify their structural role:

  • ReadWitness for the general local read skeleton.
  • BasisReadSurface for basis-bearing axis/face/lane reads.
  • TimeStepSequence for ordered transition evidence.
  • RefinementRead for parameter binding plus witness plus residue plus timeline.
