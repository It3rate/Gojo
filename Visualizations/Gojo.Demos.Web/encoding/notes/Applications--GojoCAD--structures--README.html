GojoCAD Structural Concept Shapes

This folder holds the first pass at unparameterized graphic structure. These shapes are not render commands and they are not code-side type labels. A helper such as ClothoidSection or Branch builds ordinary Gojo relation structure: ports, unresolved trace slots, local evidence, and tension/residue.

The base drawable primitive is a section: a start port, an unresolved trace, an end port, and tension. Straight lines, circular arcs, Beziers, clothoids, involutes, and splines are higher rungs over that same lower section idea. The curve family is represented by the shape of the evidence surface rather than an enum.

The current ladder is:

Rung Concept surface Added evidence
0 site / port unresolved position, flow, tangent, normal, residue
1 section two ports plus unresolved trace
2 curve family straight, circular, Bezier, clothoid, involute, spline evidence
3 endpoint behavior direction, endpoint tangents, orthogonal/normal evidence
4 connection join, tangent join, open path, branch, merge
5 boundary/body closed boundary, area, anti-area, thickened section, offset

Offset Surfaces

Offsets are structural requests over drawable sections or boundaries, not precomputed Cartesian polylines. GraphicOffsetSurface records:

  • source: the line, arc, Bezier, clothoid, involute, path, or boundary being offset
  • distance/radius evidence: the amount of separation, which may be exact, parameterized, sampled, or unresolved tension
  • side pair: opposed interior/exterior witnesses
  • direction: the positive flow used to interpret the side pair
  • closure: unresolved or explicit evidence for how an open or partial boundary should be completed when a fill asks for one
  • residue/tension: whatever the current read cannot settle

The side pair deliberately separates "interior/exterior" from "left/right". Every section has a natural positive flow, and a reader may also sample or read the inverse flow when a fill needs a consistent boundary direction. Once a direction has been chosen, the renderer can convert the section to a Cartesian tangent/normal field and place the red interior and green exterior samples on opposed sides. That conversion is not the Gojo meaning; it is only a display read.

Full closed shapes such as circles can settle interior/exterior against a center or area witness. Partial shapes can still have local side evidence. A letter C, for example, can expose an interior side for most of its arc and keep the open gap as closure tension. A render pass may visualize that closure as a faint line, gradient, clothoid, or other approximate bridge without claiming the source is fully closed.

Offset rules by curve family:

Family Structural offset meaning Render-boundary approximation
line / segment parallel section through the normal side evidence translate endpoints by the chosen normal
circle / circumference radial offset against center/area evidence radius plus/minus offset distance
arc / circular span partial radial offset with endpoint closure residue radius plus/minus offset over the span
quadratic / cubic Bezier offset through endpoint tangent/control evidence sampled tangent normals or fitted offset curve
clothoid offset of a curvature-transition section between Gojo elements sample/render the transition with a numeric clothoid or Bezier approximation, then offset normals
involute offset of an unwinding section from circular evidence sample the involute and offset its normal field
open path / partial boundary local side evidence plus closure tension offset segments/curves locally and render closure residue if requested

For filled offsets, the fill should read from the current offset boundary rather than from a separate copied polygon. If the offset outline changes, the fill must move with that outline as the same structural result. A temporary sampled polygon can still be used at the render boundary, but it should be treated as a projection of the outline read, not as independent Gojo meaning.

Area Participation Settlement Surfaces

GraphicAreaParticipationSurface carries fills and outlines as area participation and settlement evidence, not as code-owned set truth. It carries:

  • input area/fill participants in an ordered area frame
  • requested/result outline evidence
  • settlement evidence, either the older boundary-cancellation foothold or a richer participation-settlement witness
  • residue/tension for unsettled overlap, winding, holes, and boundary pieces

GraphicAreaSettlementSurface performs the current geometric area read by splitting boundary fragments, reading occupied positive/inverse participation, and emitting settled area frames, outline witnesses, residue, and fragment partitions. Area union surfaces should carry that participation-settlement witness whenever the occupied flow has been read.

Composite Shapes

Connecting concept shapes knowingly creates a new current concept shape. It does not have to wait for a later read to become true. A later read is useful for rediscovering the same surface from arbitrary structure, but construction can create the higher rung immediately.

A CompositeShape has:

  • exposed ports: the ports visible from the current rung
  • visible surface: the top concept surface to treat as current
  • lower structure: the child sections, prior composites, and other evidence
  • interior joins: the consumed joins or junctions hidden inside the current surface
  • previous rung: provenance for replay/refinement
  • residue/tension: unresolved compatibility, missing settlement, or ambiguity

The exposed-port rule is:

composite exposed ports =
all child ports minus the ports consumed by joins

Two sections joined end to end become a CompositeSection: four child ports, two consumed by the interior join, and two exposed ports. The visible surface is therefore section-like again, even though the lower child sections and the join remain inspectable.

Three or more sections meeting at a shared interior junction become a JunctionShape: the visible surface has three or more exposed ports. Direction is an elaboration over that junction surface, so branch and merge are directed multi-port composite shapes rather than separate primitive tags.

Resurfacing A Composite

A composite does not have to stay the same kind of visible surface as its lower parts. Two straight lower sections can first fold into a two-port composite section, and a later rung can resurface that composite as a single curve.

straight A + straight B -> composite AB
composite AB + curve evidence -> curved AB'

The lower straight sections and their interior join remain inspectable. The new current rung exposes the same two outer ports, but its visible surface can be a generic curve, circular arc, Bezier, clothoid, involute, spline, or another future section structure. This is deliberately separate from Join: joining establishes connectivity, while resurfacing changes what the current rung uses as the visible section.

Editing Inside A Composite

When a later operation focuses an interior join of a composite, the earlier composite is not mutated in place. The operation creates a new current rung.

Example:

A + B -> AB
AB exposes two ports and hides interior join J.

Attach C at J:
A, B, C remain lower leaves.
AB remains prior composite/provenance.
ABC becomes the current junction shape with three exposed ports.

This lets a transform work through a high-level shape, descend to a lower-rung join, and still produce a clean current surface. The prior surface stays available for replay, history, and structural ladders. A later crystallization step may choose to forget that provenance, but this structural concept layer keeps it by default.

Discovery From Completed Structure

Construction is not the only way to get a concept shape. A finished structure can also be inspected and folded into a fresh current concept surface.

TryDiscoverCompositeShape currently verifies this first step. It reads local section concepts and looks for:

  • shared endpoint port references
  • explicit PortJoin evidence
  • existing composite shapes

From that evidence it creates a new CompositeSection or JunctionShape. The new surface has lower structure and interior joins, but its previous-rung slot is unresolved tension unless real provenance was present. This is intentional: discovery should not pretend to know the edit history.

This is the path for importing completed geometry. For example, an SVG adapter can convert path endpoints and curve pieces into Gojo ports/sections first. Then discovery can infer joins, exposed ports, and junctions from the resulting Gojo structure. Later reads can extend the same pattern to tangent continuity, closed boundaries, area/anti-area, branch/merge direction, and curve-family evidence.

Elaborate creates a compact higher-rung shape by keeping the lower rung, adding evidence, and preserving unresolved residue. ElaborationLadder records the same move as a structural ladder rung, so the lower and higher structures remain independently traversable.

Most of this structure is intentionally ambiguous. The ambiguity is represented as placeholder/tension structure so a later reader can settle a concrete line, arc, tangent, branch, or area without pretending the current rung already knows coordinates or curve parameters.

Parameter Surfaces

GraphicParameterSurface is the first parameterization layer for these graphic structures. It still builds ordinary Gojo relation structure rather than a code-side parameter bag.

NormalizedValue records a reusable 0..scale parameter on a basis surface. The surface keeps both a coarse parameter/result and a refined parameter/result, plus a ResolutionLadder so the read can distinguish rough placement from later detail. The scale does not need to be decimal; the ladder levels carry whatever ratios the structure supplies.

The exact normalized-value reader requires a concrete 0..positive-scale span and coarse/refined parameter points on that same carrier face. A malformed or unbound domain remains structure without supplying this stronger read. The reader recognizes supplied evidence; it does not verify the result calculation. The syntax geometry exercise tests this distinction along with partial templates and curve constructions.

The helper can now build that resolution evidence directly from a refined tick and supplied level scales. This keeps sample code from owning the tick decomposition model. Sketch samples still compute concrete points for display, but their parameter evidence is anchored to basis surfaces such as the wall width, wall height, roof line, or leaf-local center/radius spans.

TemplateInstance records that a concrete result came from a reusable template under a binding frame. The read requires real placeholder/descriptor slots and bindings for those slots, so it is not just a four-field wrapper. In the SketchViewer house ladder, the three tree leaf blobs are instances of one leaf template. Their centers and radii are normalized parameters over local basis spans; the earlier tree crown rung uses coarse circular instances, and the later rung uses refined blob instances.

TemplateInstanceFromBindings is the convenience construction for the same surface when the caller already has relation-shaped slot bindings. It exists as a separately named helper so the binding-frame surface and the binding-list surface cannot be confused by overload resolution.

The same surface now carries the window, door, roof-overhang, and chimney examples. The opening templates bind center/size parameters against the house wall basis, with separate coarse X and Y center evidence rather than one shared coarse point. The window additionally refines a corner-radius parameter from a coarse rectangle to a rounded rectangle. The chimney binds a cap-rise parameter as zero for the body rung and positive for the capped rung. These are still ordinary structural ladder transitions: the drawn result is concrete, while the application provenance keeps the normalized parameter and template evidence available for reads and replay.

The SketchViewer house sample has begun using those basis surfaces as construction anchors too. The horizon, wall spans, roof overhang, chimney foot, tree root, tree junction, and branch tips are derived from local 0..1-ish basis spans before they are rendered. The tree split/curve/thickening/merge rungs now carry a reusable topology template with normalized root, junction, branch-tip, and crown-center evidence. This is still sample-side calculation for concrete pixels, but the corresponding Gojo structure records the basis and parameter surfaces needed for later replay or replacement.

The opening and chimney refinements now go one step further: the door rectangle, arched door, window rectangle, window cross, rounded window, chimney cap, and door knob are constructed from their center/width/height/radius/rise parameters. The rendered points are still concrete frame points, but those points are calculated from the same values bound into the template instance, reducing the gap between "evidence says this is parameterized" and "the shape was actually created from the parameters."

The cloud and tree foliage follow the same direction. The cloud seed and puffy outline are now instances of one anchor/width/height template, with the puffs constructed from a normalized local profile. Leaf blobs bind the same reusable profile surface alongside their center and radii, and branch curve controls are derived from junction/tip landmarks plus local bow values instead of independent control-point literals.

The final tree wood merge also smooths the offset branch/trunk construction. The earlier rung can still show offset pieces as a construction approximation, but the merge rung adds one closed cubic outline around the trunk and both branches. A soft sampled fill remains underneath because shared-frame area reads currently derive closed areas from segment boundaries only; a later read graph upgrade should allow cubic/arc boundaries to participate in fills without collapsing the curve offset back into straight segments.
