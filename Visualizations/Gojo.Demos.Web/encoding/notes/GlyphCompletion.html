Across a glyph gap

Two arrows can constrain a missing path. A construction can supply one. This experiment puts those situations side by side. Open layer 27

Try D first

Choose D · ends only. The left drawing shows the stem and the two headings. Its return already connects the top to the foot in the encoding. We know the whole ink has a loop. What remains open is how the pen travels on the return.

Choose Steer as you travel. In the right drawing the pen advances and turns. Switch Illustrated bow between shallow and deep. Both drawings obey the same encoded law. Choose Two corners. Now the pen goes right, down, and left. Choose Half a circle. This proposal adds a centre, common reach and two equal shares of a whole. All three obey the two arrows. One straight keeps the same heading throughout and fails the arrival condition.

The two trial rows bind candidate departure to observed departure and candidate arrival to observed arrival. The headings are compared in the same supplied upright frame. The check promises only endpoint identity pattern and headings. It does not compare outlines or imply a metric fit. Trying a proposal leaves the original steering support at 03 (either). The sketch supplies scale and placement for inspection.

Move a cut in O

Choose C from O. One cut stays anchored. Change Ink extent to place the other cut at one of seven interior stops on a radial circle with eight equal shares. The shares are landmarks for this experiment. The original one-arc O can still express the whole without them. The distance from centre to boundary remains unmeasured.

  • Ink only exposes an open walk. Its two tips alone leave the existence of a retained passage unresolved.
  • Ask owner inspects the owner's complete construction inventory. The owner can publish a one-bit answer. The diagram shows its construction in dashes so you can inspect the reason for that answer.
  • Try passage starts at the arrival tip and follows the complement's actual signed uses. Success requires arrival at the original start site.
  • Reverse reads both windows in the opposite direction. The tips swap, and the same retained spans are traversed in reverse order and direction.

Switch to Open C at the same extent. Its visible ink has exactly the same ordered site pairs. Its owner holds only the open spans. Asking now gives a definite absence within that owner's inventory. Withholding the reply leaves the reader uncertain in both cases. No query or attempted traversal inks the gap.

What the second reader learned

The existing two-choice Gojo query learner can pay for the construction reply or use the free ink observation. It sees which question it was asked: does the visible ink close, or can the owner traverse a retained return? It never sees the glyph's name, its private tables, or the correct choice. After the decision the owner walks the requested spans. A correct definite prediction earns four ticks, an incorrect one costs four, and an open answer earns zero. A query costs one tick. These are experiment settings.

Training used cuts at two, four and six parts. Evaluation froze the learner and used one, three, five and seven parts, with both directions, both owners and both questions. Each policy faced the same 32 trials.

Policy Correct Open Queries paid Net ticks
Frozen learner 32 0 16 112
Always ask 32 0 32 96
Never ask 16 16 0 64

This demonstrates learned use of an offered observation. The two questions, construction families and cost law were supplied. The trials are variations of those families. Invention of a new interpolation rule and transfer to an independent drawing remain open.
