Measured offsets with an open scale

This follow-up to the metric construction fixtures separates a known normal direction, the equation fixing its magnitude, and an observation at a selected resolution. A missing rational endpoint need not erase the construction that determines it.

Status: implemented selected reads. See the walkthrough and authored syntax.

Structural contract

Preparation consumes group(offset request, scale whole). The offset request is the existing edge, metric, edge outlet/reach calibration and relative side guide. The new S(scale,0,1) is a parameter frame: one whole scales the calculated, unnormalized normal once. It is not a physical length unit.

The prepared result retains four connected parts:

group(
  normal direction,
  group(scale whole, shared scale slot -> same scale slot,
        normal squared length -> requested squared distance),
  group(first endpoint interpolation, second endpoint interpolation),
  first output slot -> second output slot
)

The selected normalization law is normal² × scale² = distance², with a nonnegative scale. The repeated operand is the same slot. Its sign is not a second side decision: the original guide already selected the normal's side. Both endpoint requests are existing affine interpolations from an endpoint to that endpoint plus the unnormalized normal. They share the scale slot, unit frame, nonnegative support and coordinate basis. Neither endpoint is authored as the answer. The result's output slots are the actual targets of these requests.

The support is constructed as S(scale,0,ceil(sqrt(distance²/normal²))) using exact integer comparisons. This is a mathematical enclosure in the chosen parameter frame, not an assumed physical tolerance. It makes the branch choice visible: the existing interpolation reader refuses a negative scale. In the diagonal example the allowed coarse support is [0,2], and the attached square equation further restricts which value in that support can satisfy the requested distance.

A resolution request then connects the prepared witness to a subdivision:

root group(?prepared,
    span($fine_scale, 0, 100) -> ?prepared_whole);

The correspondence must target the actual prepared whole, and the fine carrier must have its subdivision unit promise. It does not mean “about two units” or authorize a tolerance around the source measurement.

SegmentOffset.TryPrepare returns the preparation witness. Its captured scale whole is bound into ?prepared_whole; the original live whole and an independently equal-looking copy are not that same captured relation. The observation request is handled by SegmentOffset.TryObserveAtResolution.

Before consuming a transported preparation, the reader checks its normal, square equation, actual source endpoints, calculated interpolation probes, shared scale/grade and output-slot correspondence against its captured input. A witness is not treated as a global correctness certificate.

A diagonal example

The edge (0,0) -> (1,1) has a selected normal (-1,1) in the identity metric. Its squared length is two. An offset distance of two therefore requires:

2 × scale² = 4
scale >= 0

The magnitude is exactly constrained even though the rational reader cannot return √2 as one rational scalar. At tenths of the scale whole, its fine count lies between 14 and 15. At hundredths it lies between 141 and 142. These are guaranteed closed enclosures, obtained by exact integer square comparisons. The equation remains attached and is stronger than either enclosure.

Both output endpoints depend on one shared bounded sample. Their coordinate expressions use the existing BoundedQuantity affine structures and readers. The endpoint enclosures are marginal views of that connected construction: they are not permission to choose unrelated points anywhere in two boxes. Subtracting the two endpoint expressions cancels the shared movement and returns the exact original displacement (1,1) at every selected resolution.

A fine read does not falsify an earlier coarse read. Each retains the captured prepared construction and its scale correspondence. Different read samples are linked to the same original scale slot; the quantity readers do not yet automatically reconcile separately produced resolution samples by ancestry.

The observation result is group(shared sample, endpoint expression edge, endpoint bound-witness edge). Its provenance connects the prepared scale slot to this sample through the actual subdivision, retains the exact square equation, and connects each original output slot to its bounded point expression. These are contextual read correspondences; they do not replace the original exact constraint with independent intervals or change material state.

Boundaries

This remains a selected flat metric construction with complete source geometry, distance calibration and side evidence. Missing metric or side discovery, uncertain physical distances, curve normals and material movement are separate. The exact offset reader and bounded miter retain their previous contracts. Preparation and bounded observation are explicit additional stages.

The open normalization does not become an approximate exact solution. No midpoint is silently chosen. If the scale lies exactly on the selected grid, the sample is concrete and its enclosure collapses. Otherwise the sample stays open, even when a finer or exact read could retain more information.

No new parser tokens or geometry opcodes are introduced. The host selects preparation and observation, and binds their actual surfaces into subsequent requests. A future compiler must preserve those selected laws and the correspondence between the exact constraint, coarse sample and output slots.
