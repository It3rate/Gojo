Complex interpretations and fractional powers

September 11, 2026. This work follows the user's clarification that complex arithmetic remains fundamental, while its construction is a selected Gojo interpretation rather than the mandatory meaning of every primitive pair.

Construction contract

Two coefficient roles and an explicit signed action establish the algebra:

coordinates = group_on(holder, S(real,0,1), S(imaginary,0,1))
one         = group_on(holder, L(real,1),  L(imaginary,0))
generator   = group_on(holder, L(real,0),  L(imaginary,1))
minus_one   = group_on(holder, L(real,-1), L(imaginary,0))
minus_gen   = group_on(holder, L(real,0),  L(imaginary,-1))
action      = group(one -> generator, generator -> minus_one,
                    minus_one -> minus_gen, minus_gen -> one)
basis       = group(coordinates, action, construction_evidence)
value       = group_on(holder, real_coefficient, imaginary_coefficient)

The edges share their actual intermediate nodes. A pair of visually opposed directions alone does not establish this action. Within the selected unital, bilinear coefficient read, multiplication follows J(1)=i, J(i)=-1 and linear extension: (a+bi)z = az + bJ(z). Four product contributions remain inspectable. This is ordinary complex arithmetic, with a connected explanation for the generator. The two coordinate spans calibrate scalar coefficients; this read is not a physical dimensional product or an implicit unit conversion.

In the opposed-face construction, imaginary = Dual(real). Positive participation on both faces remains present as two components. Drawing those faces left and right does not make the second component negative. Scalar negation changes both signed coefficients, conjugation changes only the generator coefficient, and applying the generator advances the signed cycle. The primitive face dual remains an involution; it is not multiplication by i.

In the contour construction, the already constructed circle, metric and ordered radial quarter landmarks justify the same cycle. Opposite and perpendicular rays are checked; an arbitrary four-marker loop is insufficient. The first ray is the unit reference and the next is the selected generator. The radius supplies a scale, not an intrinsic angle constant. Correspondence maps each radial landmark to its coefficient state. Reversing the selected orientation can choose the conjugate interpretation without changing the laws.

Both structures can therefore express the same algebra while retaining different provenance. A cross-basis read requires explicit correspondences for the two unit states. Equal numbers or similar drawings do not create that correspondence automatically.

Fractional powers

For a reduced rational exponent p/q with q positive, retain the constraint w^q = z^p. Reduction is essential: 2/2 requests z, not both square roots of z squared. Negative p requires a nonzero z. Repetition and inversion remain separate from choice among roots. This algebraic candidate-set contract does not silently choose the principal analytic power or a logarithm branch.

A root candidate must reproduce the target under the selected product law. When exact rational components are unavailable, keep an open coefficient pair connected to its repeated-power constraint. This is an incomplete numeric read, not evidence that the root is nonexistent. Alternative roots are not an uncertainty interval or a probability distribution. A reference can select the nearest exact candidate in the coefficient metric; a tie remains a tie. That local choice is not yet continuous branch tracking around a loop.

Ordinary complex identities and the distinction between general and principal powers are checked against NIST DLMF 1.9 and NIST DLMF 4.2(iv). Their analytic definitions provide conformance references, not a requirement to store radians or floats.

The existing fused carrier-face product and fold remain separate selected reads. Their law must not be silently changed into complex multiplication. Split-complex (j*j=+1) also requires a different action and is not an alias for this algebra.

What the two views share, and what they do not

For 3 + 2i, the opposed construction carries positive 3 on the forward face and positive 2 on the opposed face. Both are present. The contour construction reads those coefficients along its first ray and next radial quarter. The generator acts as follows in either view:

Repeated generator application Coefficients Opposed-face read
Original (3,2) +3F, +2I
Once (-2,3) -2F, +3I
Twice (-3,-2) -3F, -2I
Three times (2,-3) +2F, -3I
Four times (3,2) +3F, +2I

The general flow codex calls this a phase step; it need not describe a physical rotation. The circular construction additionally justifies the geometric read. Nothing here assigns universal screen-left or screen-up meanings to the roles.

The two-coefficient representation does not embed all complex arithmetic into one ordinary real coordinate. For example, the existing scalar fold sends 3F + 2I to 1F. It cannot reconstruct the pair. Under that fold i becomes -1, but folding i*i gives -1, whereas multiplying the folded values gives +1. This is a useful explicit example of a read which does not preserve the product. Here the scalar accounting read is forward minus opposed; the fold itself retains the resulting carrier face. Retaining the pair allows both readings without contradicting either selected law.

Core2's AlgebraTable.ApplyOpposition already distinguished the four-step operation from its Mirror operation. Its default product agrees with the new coefficient read: recessive/recessive contributes negative dominant; the two mixed products contribute recessive. Current FusedCarrierFaceReads instead exposes a different four-term participation law and a scalar fold. Both remain available; neither is silently renamed.

Implemented readers

The implementation is in ComplexFrame, contour construction and powers. These are local readers over ordinary relation surfaces. Their temporary rational arithmetic uses the existing ExactScalar; durable fractions use grounded numerator/subdivision structure. No new primitive element or host complex-number wrapper is added.

Reader Request structure Result and evidence
Opposed One concrete forward carrier Two unit spans, four shared signed states, the full directed cycle and face correspondence. A convenience builder; the fixture writes the same structure explicitly.
TryFromContour group(contour surface, coefficient coordinates) A complex basis with radial-to-coefficient correspondence, native contour and verified turn evidence.
TryAdd, TrySubtract group(basis,left,right) Exact or partial coefficient pair; signed coordinate calibrations distinguish addition from subtraction.
TryMultiply group(basis,left,right) Exact or partial pair and four source-product contributions; no scalar face fold.
TryApplyGenerator, TryConjugate group(basis,value) Calculated pair and the selected action or unit-state correspondences.
TryDivide group(basis,numerator,denominator) Conjugate, squared coefficient norm, numerator product and exact quotient; open or zero divisor remains unresolved.
TryReexpress group(source basis,target basis,two unit correspondences,value) Same coefficients on the explicitly corresponding target roles. Lookalike unit nodes do not substitute for actual connections.
TryPower group(basis,value,signed count span) Integer power with actual product compositions and inversion evidence. Binary composition is a derived evaluation strategy.
TryFractionalPower group(basis,value,p span,q span) group(power constraint,candidate witnesses), with reduced exponent, target-power trace, branch correspondence and reproduction checks.
TrySelectNearest group(fractional-power request,reference) Root witness, exact squared coefficient distances, and nearest candidate group. A tie remains plural and unresolved as a unique selection.

Count spans begin at zero. Numerator and denominator counts use the same concrete count carrier; q must be positive. Known zero to a positive power is zero. This selected domain leaves 0^0 and negative powers of zero unresolved. Signed integer powers are not limited to positive exponents.

All these reads are observations. They introduce no carrier value or tension. Numeric sites are captured before arithmetic, so a later retargeting produces a new result while the earlier witness remains readable. Within a transported world the graph preserves aliases; independently restored worlds still require explicit identity correspondence.

The first numeric implementation computes over rational complex coefficients, not every possible member of the complex continuum. Exact intermediates use wide integer arithmetic; each stored coordinate or fraction must fit the current signed-integer transport. Overflow retains an unresolved request, without wraparound, saturation or a floating-point fallback.

Fractional-power examples and boundaries

Request Current result
Solutions of w^2 = -1 Two exact candidates, i and -i; neither is automatically the chosen branch.
Solutions of w^2 = 3+4i 2+i and -2-i, each with a multiplication check.
Solutions of w^2 = 4/9 2/3 and -2/3, with exact subdivisions.
9^(3/2) under the candidate-set contract 27 and -27, satisfying w^2=729. This differs from a request for the positive principal real power.
(3+4i)^(2/2) Only 3+4i; reducing the exponent prevents inventing a second branch.
Solutions of w^2 = 2 Open real coefficient and known zero imaginary coefficient, attached to the square equation. No tolerance or decimal root is invented.
Solutions of w^2 = i Correlated open real/imaginary coefficients in one candidate whose square must reproduce i.
Solutions of w^3 = 8 Retained cubic equation. Returning only 2 would falsely omit the other complex roots.
Roots of zero with any positive integer degree The unique zero candidate.

Exact integer results and square-root families with rational components are implemented. Other nonzero root degrees currently retain their constraints; they do not claim an enumerated candidate set. A missing rational answer is different from a forbidden divisor and from a tie between exact candidates. Known input, exponent, target and the unresolved candidate remain available in the result/remainder so another reader can refine them.

There is no principal branch flag on a number. The fixture separately requests the candidate nearest -i among the roots of -1; that selects -i. With a reference at zero both candidates have the same distance, so neither wins. An open reference retains the already calculated candidates and leaves their distance reads open; it does not discard the root family. This read uses the coefficient norm of the selected complex algebra. It does not infer a physical distance metric for an arbitrary rendering of the faces.

Syntax and validation

The authored fixture writes the entire opposed basis, not a literal whose meaning is hidden inside the parser. Its circle request accepts the result of the existing constructed circle fixture. No new parser forms are needed. As with the other semantic fixtures, the driver selects the local reader. A future compiler must preserve that explicit read selection in addition to compiling the basis and request graph; bare parsing does not execute an overloaded group.

The conformance tests exercise independent integer arithmetic identities, associativity, distributivity, inverse reconstruction and exact Gaussian square roots across all quadrants. They compare the circular generator against the existing geometric rotation reader under ordinary and sheared charts and both choices of side. False bases, false shared cycle sites, non-quarter landmarks, zero divisors, independent unknowns, integer range, branch ties and two numeric restorations are included. Current full-solution verification passes 1,653 tests, including 35 focused complex cases and the new fixture inventory entry.

Next mathematical steps

  1. Refine correlated algebraic roots. Connect shared radical constraints to exact rational enclosures and available refinement reads. For sqrt(i), retain the shared magnitude and signs rather than independent error bars on its two components. An exact algebraic value need not become a float.
  2. Construct higher root families. Use intrinsic contour subdivision and repeated-product constraints, retaining every admissible branch. A cubic should account for all three branches before claiming a complete read.
  3. Follow branches through change. Add source samples, continuation correspondences, path/winding evidence and ties. A loop around zero can exchange square-root branches even though the source returns to its start; a nearest-current-sample rule alone is insufficient to encode that history.
  4. Extend selected algebras and quantities deliberately. A split-complex action, dimensional complex quantities, broader calibrations and analytic functions need their own laws. A two-cycle or positive square cannot be accepted by the present complex-basis reader merely because it looks similar.

The key recovered principle is that the algebra is fundamental enough to be constructed and tested, while its applicability and representation remain local choices. These two constructions are a starting point for those further reads.
