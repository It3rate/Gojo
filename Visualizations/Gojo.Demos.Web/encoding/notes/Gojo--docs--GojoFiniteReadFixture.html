Finite calculation and coarse publication

Gojo.Structures.FiniteReadSurface is a small, opt-in fixture for calculating with limited available information. It uses guarded long operations without Int128, BigInteger, an exact overflow remainder, or an exact-reader fallback. The long is the host storage envelope; each receiver can select a much smaller capacity. This fixture does not yet pack those domains into smaller lanes.

Native structure

The capacity is a BoundedWholeSurface:

G(axis, Point(axis,minimum) -> Point(axis,maximum), Segment(axis,0,quantum))

The domain must include zero and the quantum must be positive. Inputs and outputs use a shared exposed tick basis Segment(axis,0,1). Quantum selects the publication granularity in those ticks; it does not secretly rescale them.

Publication uses the existing QualifiedReadSurface / ReadWitness spine:

selected:   exposed source or operand publications
basis:      Segment(axis,0,1)
value:      frozen bounded nominal tick
variation: lower endpoint -> upper endpoint
coverage:  unresolved (no confidence inferred)
scope:     frozen capacity and quantum
excluded:  zero displacement if exact, otherwise source -> variation
provenance: native operation equation -> finite scope

Only the finite support reader gives the variation pair these endpoint roles. A Placeholder in the lower role means the lower bound is unavailable; one in the upper role means the upper bound is unavailable. Neither is numeric infinity. Arbitrary placeholders elsewhere acquire no new meaning.

The source and equation remain connected for correspondence. They are not queried to recover a lost answer. A producer can retain finer private data or discard it; the publication does not promise a refinement route. The fixture does not make a privacy boundary or remove externally retained source graphs.

Selected arithmetic law

Publish clips to the domain endpoints. An unclipped value is rounded toward zero to a multiple of the quantum. The support becomes a conservative rounding cell, not an exact discarded difference. A clipped endpoint is preserved even when it is not a multiple of the quantum.

Add, Subtract and Multiply consume already materialized finite publications. They do not execute pending supplier equations. Operand nominal ticks and any known support endpoints must fit the selected receiving domain and axis. Missing calibration, incompatible axes or inadmissible endpoints produce an unresolved native witness with the attempted contract attached.

Addition/subtraction propagate available interval endpoints. Multiplication encloses closed intervals using four guarded corner products. Open product inputs conservatively leave both endpoints open, except that an exact zero factor establishes zero. These are support rules, not distributions. They do not assume statistical independence or estimate probabilities.

Calculation in [0,15], quantum 1 Published nominal Exposed support
12 + 3 15 [15,15]
14 + 3 15 [15,?]
Previous publication minus 3 12 [12,?]
Two independent high-clipped publications subtracted 0 [?,?]
The same publication subtracted from itself 0 [0,0]

Clipping order is observable. (14 + 3) - 3 has nominal 12, while 14 + (3 - 3) has nominal 14. A compiler must preserve publication sites; exact-algebra reassociation is not generally valid for this selected law.

For signed capacity [-127,127], quantum 4, publishing 10 gives nominal 8 and support [8,11]; publishing -10 gives -8 and [-11,-8]. Publishing zero gives nominal zero and [-3,3]. The cell means the service no longer distinguishes those values, even if its supplier knows the original value. An explicit mathematical cancellation can still establish zero before a separate coarse publication, which can deliberately discard that specificity.

Sufficient local reads

QualifiedReadSurface.TryReadPublishedValue reads only a materialized nominal observation and its exposed basis. Unlike TryReadValue, it does not call ReadCurrent or evaluate a connected supplier. Support is a separate question.

FiniteReadSurface.TryGreaterThan inspects only the published support. A high-clipped product with support [15,?] settles > 10 as true. It cannot settle > 20; its nominal 15 alone is not evidence for false. The return value distinguishes a settled Boolean answer from unresolved demand. No supplier traversal or wider calculation is triggered by the unresolved answer.

This read-only fixture does not set carrier values, introduce physical tension, or mutate a creature. A later explicit Gojo response can connect exposed loss to demand, select another service, or request mutation. Those responses still need their own structure and tests.

Scope and verification

Focused conformance lives in Tests/Gojo.Tests/Core/FiniteReadSurfaceTests.cs. The existing exact equation, projection and bounded-quantity readers keep their exactness/range contracts. The phototaxis backend still uses those existing laws; it has not silently switched to finite arithmetic.

Division, explicit averages, general shared-variable interval solving, lazy arbitrary circuits, runtime budgets, automatic precision selection, packed storage and loss-driven evolution remain future work. An average should be built under its own finite contract; it must not silently recover a clipped sum by consulting an exact shadow calculation.
