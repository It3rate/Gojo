Approximately ten is a landmark with an open interpretation

In the user's example, a source reports “about ten.” Ten is the reported landmark. The actual quantity, expected deviation, source bias, applicability and even the shape of a useful expectation can remain unresolved. A reader's experience might support an expectation near two or twenty. Those are local interpretations of the report; they do not change what was reported.

The bare landmark supplies neither hard endpoints nor a normal distribution with a known spread. Even symmetry around ten would be an additional premise. “Nearby” can be useful before there is an agreed metric or sharp boundary. For a concept such as “warm,” the appropriate comparison may itself need construction. An exact record of an imprecise report is entirely consistent.

The structural separation to exercise is:

Participation What it contributes What can remain open
Report and event A landmark, its reporting basis and an exposed source correspondence The actual quantity and the report's relation to it
Local interpretation A proposed calibration or other relation connecting report to prediction Bias, spread, shape, domain and whether this relation applies
Supporting observations Why this space currently uses that interpretation Reliability, overlap with other evidence and future usefulness
Requested view The question, desired output detail and retained complement Whether obtaining more detail would help
Feedback and revision New participation that can challenge a prediction or its premises Which participant should change and by how much

These are roles in a connected experiment, not new primitive kinds or mandatory fields on every number. A source is an exposed relation surface, and an event needs correspondence to the quantity and time it concerns. A string saying “biased” cannot substitute for a relation that actually changes the read. Likewise, a numeric source identifier does not supply a calibration law.

This also separates two uses of approximation. A source can lack finer information, or deliberately leave known detail outside the communication focus. They can have similar outward expressions and different retained structure. Neither automatically calls for more attention.

Consequences for operations

Arithmetic applies to the selected quantities and relations. Adding two report landmarks can produce an exact total of the reported landmarks; it does not by itself establish the corresponding actual total or its uncertainty. A selected interpretation may supply a conditional prediction while its adequacy remains open. Revising that interpretation must preserve which version supported an earlier prediction.

The implemented bounded-quantity reader answers a narrower question: given explicit support and calibrations, what enclosure follows? Its bounds are conditional premises. They do not define the meaning of “about.” Unknown support parameters can be represented by partial patterns, but the current closed-bound reader does not thereby acquire an arithmetic for vague or learned expectations. Refusing that particular read need not prevent another, coarser or differently conditioned read.

Identity can settle some questions without estimating spread. Within an additive quantity domain, removing the very same quantity cancels it even if its value is unknown. Two uses of the same description do not establish that identity. The existing bounded fixture tests shared-slot cancellation with declared supports; it is not yet a general symbolic quantity reader for support-free expressions. A future fixture should explicitly test that further case rather than assume it follows from the parser's ability to store it.

More experience can justify a richer expectation: a source-specific calibration, several alternatives, soft compatibility, or an explicitly chosen distribution. Parameters of those descriptions can remain partial too. A soft compatibility read is not automatically a probability, and a learned spread is not a guaranteed tolerance. We should add the laws each selected read needs, without making every approximate value carry a universal interval or bell curve.

What the current syntax and kernel establish

The version-1 syntax constructs ordinary Gojo structure. Selected kernel readers inspect that structure; the test driver currently chooses which reader or action to invoke. Numeric graph transport preserves the retained graph. These are separate capabilities.

How far into dynamics we already are

The local blend already changes a filter parameter through tension. It observes a mismatch, applies a selected response, records a stalled step at the current resolution, and later uses a different response route. An alternative mixture remains present. The tests choose those actions; the system has not discovered the response strategy.

The curve replay already handles consequences of a changed premise. It knows which selected applications consume changed outputs, preserves unaffected work, and can pause while a current result is pending. It keeps earlier evidence readable and resumes after restoring the retained dependency context. Its replay helpers record supplied results; the caller still performs the mathematical reads and chooses the execution order.

Together these support the next experiment: a relation changes in response to new evidence, and the system can keep track of which conclusions depend on which version. The next missing link is a small explicit policy that selects a response from local evidence, with its own inspectable assumptions and failures. It need not be a universal learning rule.

Recommended sequence of experiments

1. Landmark, candidate interpretation and later feedback

Proposed fixture: landmark-calibration.gojo (not implemented by this document). Use a small count-reporting source so exact arithmetic and the interpretive uncertainty are easy to distinguish. Start with a landmark of ten and an open interpretation. Introduce a later observation through a separate exposed port. Its basis, event correspondence and supporting premise must be visible.

For an intentionally exact toy case, let the report be ten and the later reference observation be two. Two selected candidate relations fit:

proportional candidate: q = r / 5
offset candidate:       q = r - 8

at r = 10: both predict 2
at r = 20: they predict 4 and 12

These candidates are chosen experimental assumptions, not the definition of approximation or exhaustive explanations. Their reporting and quantity bases must be calibrated explicitly. Count-domain restrictions also remain visible: the offset candidate's negative prediction below eight would require a domain refusal or a revised model, not silent clipping to zero.

The first observation should leave both candidates available. A new report of twenty makes different predictions before further feedback arrives. A later observation near four could support the proportional candidate locally. If the feedback is itself approximate, its interpretation needs the same care; a hard interval should only be introduced as an explicit fixture premise.

This first fixture should establish:

  • Preserving the original report while interpretations and expectations change.
  • Representing missing interpretation and missing spread without inventing bounds.
  • Keeping distinct sources and events distinct even when landmarks coincide.
  • Retaining candidates that current evidence cannot distinguish.
  • Linking a prediction to the exact candidate version used before feedback.
  • Restoring the whole retained context without changing those correspondences.

The driver may initially propose the candidate relations. That gives a clean baseline before asking a policy to construct or select them. It also lets us test partial construction and useful predictions without a general executor.

2. One local adaptive response, with a declared law

Extend that fixture with one adjustable calibration and one selected local response law. Its input is a captured prediction, later feedback and the connection between them. Its output is a proposed parameter change, not a claim that the parameter caused the discrepancy. Parameter changes enter the actuator through signed tension; observing a mismatch alone does not mutate it.

First inspect the local effect of a small permitted perturbation. Record the response basis, remaining resolution residue and whether the perturbation improved predictions on later events. Compare against the unchanged initial calibration and another retained candidate. Avoid judging success solely by fitting the event that requested the update.

Exercise a constant source, changed source behavior, missing feedback, coarse actuator steps and a case where different parameters remain indistinguishable. Separate parameter responsiveness, prediction spread and confidence in the model's applicability. A single scalar should not stand for all three.

3. Two connected local spaces, then a feedback cycle

Give two spaces different exposed evidence and local questions. Let one publish interpretations that the other uses, then return feedback. Each space must see only its exposed inputs and correspondence; the test harness may observe more to evaluate the experiment, but must not secretly choose the local answer from hidden ground truth.

Begin with explicit event ordering and a small step budget. Add delayed, repeated and conflicting feedback. Replaying a known observation through a second route should not manufacture independent evidence. Unknown overlap remains a hypothesis; neither ancestry tracing nor statistical independence should be mandatory defaults.

Before treating the cycle as a reusable execution mechanism, expose exhausted budgets, oscillation, no-progress steps and pending dependencies. The curve fixture's strict-current-result rule is one selected contract: an explicitly historical, provisional or coarse result may still be useful under another. Do not assume all connected spaces must share a clock or converge to one answer.

4. Change participation and eventually structure

Once fixed connections have observable utility, experiment with introducing a candidate route, changing its participation, retaining an alternative and removing a route that no longer earns its cost. Deliberately change the environment and test whether a previously weak alternative can recover.

Start with explicit resource and retained-evidence contracts. Material routes must conserve participation; observational routes need dependency accounting, not an artificial material-drain rule. Utility, response strength and admission capacity must not silently become interchangeable numbers. Learned grouping of sources is revisable and does not establish exact reference equality.

Only then test deliberate history compression: what predictive relation was retained, what observations or dependencies were lost, and which later claims are no longer supported. Exact storage is available now; a useful forgetting law is a separate experiment.

Work alongside that sequence

Soft expectations and normal-shaped uncertainty deserve a separate mathematical fixture when a concrete read needs them. Center, scale, shared causes, shape and their unresolved parameters can be structural. Their operations need an explicit interpretation; a normal family should not become a universal definition of Gojo resolution or ordinary approximation. No floating-point probability engine is required as a prerequisite for the first dynamic fixture.

Keep authoring improvements driven by repeated examples. The existing constructors and ordinary relations should suffice for the first calibration experiment. If role order becomes hard to inspect, document its local contract before introducing shorthand. Shortcuts should expand to the same inspectable structure. A readable writer and reusable definitions will become useful, but neither needs to precede the next experiment. A generic evaluator or compiler would force execution choices too early for this particular step.

What counts as validation

Mathematical and structural obligations remain firm: exact results under declared premises, domain refusal, identity preservation, explicit remainder, material accounting where applicable, and historical/version correspondence. Test them before and after numeric restoration, including pending states.

Adaptive claims require different evidence: predictions made before feedback, performance on later events and changed conditions, comparison with simple unchanged alternatives, resource cost, and visible failure or ambiguity. A deterministic test can verify that a chosen policy was executed correctly; that alone does not establish that the policy is useful or generally converges. Agreement inside a closed feedback loop is not sufficient evidence of accuracy.

For each experiment, retain a short contract: exposed question and premises; authored structure; selected read; who chooses the action; permitted mutation; what remains unresolved; and what observation would count against the proposal. Add an illustrated explanation once the actual fixture supports those claims.
