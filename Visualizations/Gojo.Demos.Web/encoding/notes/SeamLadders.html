Two uses of a seam

In each house the roof and room meet at A and B. In the left house, both faces use the same seam edge. In the right house the room has its own edge between the very same sites. Add an interior landmark M through the roof and watch what the attached room reader sees. Open the two houses

        P
       / \
      A---B      roof walks A → B → P → A
      |   |      room walks B → A → C → D → B
      C---D

Both walks run counterclockwise in the drawing. They cross their common interface in opposite senses along its length. A house-local catalogue owns the edges. Each face takes an ordered range of signed uses from it. Each seam reader stays bound to its original use position as the owner adds a rung.

M is an asserted interior landmark. Its distance from A or B is unspecified. The rung adds a finer observation while both original A–B endpoint reads remain available. The room binding in the second house grants correspondence at M. The two edges still have independent state.

The whole-house boundary read adds one signed occurrence for each edge use, grouped by edge reference. In the first house the seam contributes +1 and −1 to the same entry. Both face walks and the seam itself remain intact. In the second house those contributions go to different entries. A projection that combined coincident lines could give the two buildings the same outline. That would answer a different question about their geometry.

A second meter distinguishes the operations

Choose the roof meter or source control. The room meter always keeps its frame. Each port reorients the same stored sample if its reference frame is opposed. Flipping a meter toggles that read again. Reversing the supplied source changes D on the roof edge.

In the last row, 110 has V=0. O and D describe the chosen frame and its encoding convention. The zero read alone supplies no evidence of physical motion. The playground supplies observations directly as experimental input. A material handoff would require its own passage, capacity and settlement. Sharing or binding a landmark supplies no such handoff.

An open stroke and a proposed O

Layer 21 uses the same range reader. Here the rung changes which edge uses participate. The catalogue supplies edges A → B, B → C, C → D, D → A. View 0 reads only the first three. View 1 includes the fourth, which spans the gap. Closure checks every interior join and compares the two end sites. Open the stroke and interpretation

Starting from the open stroke, D → A belongs to a proposed completion. The observed walk still ends at D. Starting from a supplied closed template, the derived variant omits the final interval. Its gap is an authored omission. Switching rung direction preserves both underlying windows. The renderer illustrates curved strokes. Recognition of a letter and selection of a physically allowed bridge would each need further evidence.

Fragmentation as a cue for attention

Repeated splits, moves and retargeting may leave many live ranges or retired slots. They carry different evidence. A heavily revised path may deserve a look at whether two alternatives should remain as rungs, or whether one frequently reused read has become stable enough to crystallize. A large number of ranges can also describe a perfectly stable branched structure. Retired slots can reflect earlier layout changes after the uncertainty has already settled. For a future experiment, compare local edit history and reread cost with the cost of keeping those alternatives available. Preserve each live port's correspondence during compaction. This pass records the hypothesis and supplies no automatic compaction or confidence policy.
