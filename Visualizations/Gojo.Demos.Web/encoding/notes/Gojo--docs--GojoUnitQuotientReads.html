Explicit unit quotient reads

Status: bounded unit-expression, stable tick and quantity reads, September 5, 2026. The user clarified that dimensional reordering should be an explicit read. Standards such as miles/hour and litres/100 km have chosen order and calibration; an algebraic read must not make that authored structure arbitrary.

The arithmetic cancellation law is established. Its placement in Gojo is a design choice: this implementation preserves the original expression and records a separate read with evidence. It does not make all unit shapes or ordered relations commutative.

The expressed structure

UnitQuotient constructs and reads:

group(ordered numerator factors, ordered denominator factors, selected standard)

Each supported factor is a one-tick point P(C,1) on a concrete unit-bearing carrier. The native UnitShape states what that tick means. Its carrier value is not a numeric operand in this dimensional read. Empty factor groups are explicitly present; placeholders and unsupported factors remain present too. An empty numerator with a nonempty denominator expresses reciprocal participation.

For example, the following uses existing syntax constructors:

gojo 1;
let $mile = carrier();
let $hour = carrier();
let $clock = carrier(unit_of($hour));
let $standard = point($mile, 1) -> point($hour, 1);
root group(
    ports(point($mile, 1), point($clock, 1)),
    ports(point($hour, 1)),
    $standard
);

The explicit dimensional reading of (mile/hour) * hour can leave one mile factor. It retains the two different time carrier identities and the standard. The ordinary relation used as standard is calibration evidence, not itself a new division opcode. The selected quotient context gives the three fields their roles; arbitrary nested groups are not searched for convenient factors.

Cancellation and its witness

TryCancel records a common ReadWitness:

Field Connected evidence
Selected source Original quotient expression with its authored factor order
Read surface Ordered group of one-tick numerator → denominator correspondences
Result New quotient retaining uncanceled factors in their original relative order
Residue Separate numerator/denominator groups of open or unsupported factors
Provenance The same selected standard

The read visits denominator factors in order and pairs each with the first unused numerator occurrence having the same complete native unit shape. Each occurrence participates at most once, even when an alias is reused at several positions. The deterministic pairing records one valid dimensional correspondence; it is not discovery of a physical producer-consumer connection.

The match preserves descriptor provenance, opposed-face structure, calibration, and composite unit order. Bare algebraic resemblance does not override different descriptors. This first read does not cancel a forward unit against an opposed unit or silently commute the interior of a composite unit factor. Any needed decomposition or conversion must be exposed explicitly first.

An unmatched known denominator remains part of the result; it is not an error. An open factor remains in its original role and is also exposed as unresolved read participation. Unknown factors do not cancel each other merely because they share a placeholder or a wildcard carrier. Known factors can still cancel around open factors in this selected commutative dimensional context.

TryReadNumeratorUnit derives the remaining ordered product only when the denominator is empty and every surviving numerator factor has a supported unit. When both groups are empty, IsDimensionless reads the absence of dimensional factors. It does not substitute Carrier.Unknown as a universal unit or create a new numeric value of 1.

These are observational reads. No carrier value or tension changes, no original factor disappears, and no native unit promise is rewritten. Witnesses retain source references; they are not a new immutable historical serialization contract. Durable evidence still needs an explicit capture boundary.

Standards and quantity arithmetic

For litres/100 km, the denominator can be a carrier whose native tick shape is ResolutionOf(kilometre,100). One kilometre tick cannot cancel that one hundred-kilometre tick. A quantity must first be projected through an explicit conversion, preserving its remainder, before its converted native basis is used. Matching hundred-kilometre factors can then cancel and leave litre participation.

The standard field is retained structural evidence. The helper does not execute arbitrary standard definitions, infer conversions from labels, or validate that an external quantity conforms to a named real-world standard. Native factor shapes govern this exact one-tick correspondence. The mph and consumption names in the fixture are authoring aliases; they have no runtime authority.

Dimensional cancellation also does not prove numeric division is defined. u/u can have no remaining dimensions while a particular numeric denominator is zero, unknown, or an interval containing zero. Those cases still need their numeric domain conditions and local claims. The quantity reader below refuses zero or unreadable denominators; interval arithmetic remains future work.

Stable quotient tick promises

UnitQuotient.TryReadUnit(expression,out unit) selects a complete expression and captures its ordered native unit surfaces and standard. It does not implicitly cancel factors. A separate cancellation witness's result may be selected for capture if that is the intended promise.

The resulting UnitShape contains ordinary Gojo structure:

group on Unknown(
    group on Unknown(ordered numerator unit surfaces),
    group on Unknown(ordered denominator unit surfaces),
    descriptor slot for captured standard)

The neutral holder avoids fresh expression-group carrier identities changing the stable algebra. Factor algebra and descriptors are merged using the existing unit-shape mechanism; the standard uses a provenance descriptor. This is a structural choice for this bounded implementation, not a new mathematical law or a new primitive. No division flag, exponent table or runtime type tag carries the denominator meaning.

UnitShape.TryReadQuotient exposes the two ordered unit arrays and a standard snapshot. A denominator-only shape is a reciprocal promise. Both groups empty are a dimensionless promise under that standard, distinct from Unknown's wildcard unit. Unreduced u/u remains different from that empty promise until an explicit cancellation read is selected. Standard differences remain visible in full unit compatibility; bare algebraic comparison is a separate read.

Repeated factors and nested unit shapes survive as authored. A composite quotient stays whole in an outer product or sum; the reader does not normalize nested quotients. Coarse resolution and opposed flow can wrap quotient units without turning a denominator into a negative coordinate or opposed face. Unit snapshots preserve captured coordinates when an original standard tick changes. A subsequent read can therefore detect a stale output promise.

Syntax adds exactly one form: quotient_unit(expression). It returns the unit read for use by carrier(...), resolution(...), or another unit constructor. Open/unsupported factors and an unbound standard refuse capture. The partial expression is still valid and can be retained, bound and read later; a premature constructor call reports a located syntax error.

Numeric division in an exact native basis

QuantityQuotient connects:

group(numerator sample, denominator sample, output carrier, selected standard)

Each readable sample is a signed location. Its native carrier unit participates once in the corresponding quotient role, including when that native unit is itself composite. The output must have exactly that ordered quotient promise under the same standard. This initial numeric reader does not use a canceled output unit or infer a conversion to another calibration.

gojo 1;
let $mile = carrier();
let $hour = carrier();
let $standard = point($mile, 1) -> point($hour, 1);
let $units = group(ports(point($mile, 1)), ports(point($hour, 1)), $standard);
let $speed = carrier(quotient_unit($units));
root group(at($mile, 7), at($hour, 2), $speed, $standard);

TryObserve derives the projection S(numerator,0,d) -> S(output,0,1) and retains the denominator sample connected to that projection. Existing exact projection arithmetic supplies q = trunc(n/d) and source remainder r:

n = q*d + r
7 = 3*2 + 1
7 = (-3)*(-2) + 1

The remainder is in numerator ticks, not quotient ticks. This integer read plus its remainder preserves exact participation; it does not assert that 7/2 is exactly 3. Samples are observations, not reads of Carrier.Value by convention.

The witness connects the request, denominator-to-projection evidence, output location, numerator residue and derived unit expression. No carrier receives value or tension. Source tick references remain live evidence; this witness is not a durable snapshot of a past sample.

For a structurally valid request, zero denominator, open samples, incompatible output units, or an unreadable standard return false with a witness whose result is a placeholder and whose residue retains both operands. The request and its expected output remain selected evidence. This states a failed local read, not fault in one participant. A malformed request returns false without a witness. Stored tick overflow throws consistently with projection; it never wraps or partially settles carriers.

Resolution remains a separate projection. The fixture divides 237 miles by 2 hours into 118 speed ticks plus 1 mile, then reads 11 ten-speed ticks plus 8 speed ticks. Both stages reconstruct the original numerator:

237 = 11*10*2 + 8*2 + 1

The two residues have different carriers and must not be added without their correspondences. Litres/100 km likewise requires the supplied denominator's native hundred-kilometre unit. A kilometre sample is refused for that output; the standard's retained 100 does not secretly convert it.

Corrected unit factor inventory

Before this work, nested product inspection could expose its product frame as an extra factor. It could also split an unreduced additive unit or an opposed unit into its internal parts and marker. These were reader bugs.

TryReadOrthogonalProductOperands now unwraps nested product framing while preserving additive, opposed, resolution, descriptor and orthogonal factors. The read returns (u*v)*w as the three ordered factors u,v,w, without changing the stored nesting. (u+v)*w keeps the additive factor intact; this is not an implicit distributive expansion.

Explicit nested composition, September 6

UnitQuotient.TryExpand exposes ordered product and quotient factors only when explicitly selected. For (a/b)/(c/d) it yields numerator [a,d], denominator [b,c]. Each source factor remains connected to its decomposition, including its local standard. Additive factors, opposed faces and calibration wrappers remain atomic. Open factors remain present.

TryNormalize connects both expansion and cancellation witnesses. It does not rewrite the native input promise. TryProjectEquivalent then admits a 1:1 observational projection into an exactly matching reduced unit; no calibration conversion is inferred. For example, a speed tick multiplied by an hour tick can be explicitly read in miles while its original product remains inspectable.

QuantityQuotient.TryObserveReduced first performs native-basis division, so zero/open denominators remain unresolved before any dimensional simplification. It then selects the explicit equivalent output read. Dividing 7 speed ticks by 2 inverse-hour ticks produces 3 mile ticks and 1 speed tick of numerator residue. Both witnesses and the original request remain connected.

Composition tests cover denominator inversion, products, partial factors, nested standards and refusal of incompatible calibration or faces. The authored composition fixture exercises the same laws before and after numeric graph restoration.
