A fork with two owners

Give a junction one pulse. Its sibling advertises two exits. On beat 1 both are free and the junction places the pulse in its left offer site. On beat 2 a separately supplied obstacle occupies the left gate. The old report still explains the choice. The actual handoff now encounters an occupied gate. Follow the pulse and its reply in layer 22.

What makes the decisions

exit gate + inbox ── vacancy read ──→ junction guard
waiting ── controlled passage ──→ left or right offer
offer ── port seam, gated by current receiver state ──→ inbox
inbox ── local passage ──→ end ── public presence read ──→ reply latch
seam before/after ── event read ──→ optional refusal meter

The two material owners have private packed state. The junction also has a signal circuit that remembers an observed arrival. The author supplies the wiring and the two Boolean read laws. Each law compiles to an inspectable 64-byte table of admitted input/input/output words.

  • Each exit advertises availability when its gate and inbox are both empty.
  • The left offer guard reads left availability. The right offer guard reads right availability together with left unavailability. This connection supplies the initial left preference when both exits are free.
  • A single occupied waiting site feeds the two guarded passages. Moving into an offer drains waiting. This physically latches the choice. There is no return connection from the offer to waiting in this fixture.
  • The connector pulses its packed seam rows in stored order. Each row names one exported material outlet and one exported intake. The intake binds the receiver's gate and inbox. While holding both owners, the passage reads source, gate and inbox, consults its 8-byte transition bitmap, then commits the two material bits together. Each row settles independently.
  • The receiver moves an accepted packet from its inbox to its end on the next delivery opportunity. Presence at either end can advance a separate reply circuit from waiting to seen. Removing the return wire leaves a delivered pulse and an unanswered sender.

The material export differs from an ordinary read connection. Only an explicitly constructed outlet/intake pair can do the joint handoff. Both sites must still have compatible constant O/D and one V bit. A conflicting receiver lock, incompatible migration or nonunique transition defers the attempt with both sites unchanged. The experiment uses a single-threaded clock. With competing rows at one inbox, row order gives the first row the first opportunity and the occupied target holds the subsequent source.

Clock and environmental input

Every beat runs the same delivery, seam, offer and reply phases, in that order. Both lanes get an opportunity on every beat. The environment may move a separate blocker from reserve to the left gate or back using an ordinary local passage before those phases. The input tape fixes those beat numbers before the run. The recorder supplies no clock decisions or retries.

The report read and the later intake check deliberately occur at different times. The waiting pulse can move into an offer at the end of beat 1, after the seam opportunity has already passed. The receiver checks its live state on beat 2. If left stays blocked, the offer remains there even while right is free. This exposes a limitation of the authored policy. Adaptive recovery or new structure would need an actual return signal, local state and a connected change law inside the system.

The calculated vacancy endpoints are 1 and 3. Endpoint 5 reads the fallback condition. The junction imports these observations as guards. Each material outlet maps to junction port 1 or 2. Intake 0 binds receiver ports [0,2], and intake 1 binds [1,3]. Those public interface bindings are host handles in this prototype. The one-byte seam table selects among them.

A stale report, a refusal, an arrival

The default tape closes left on beat 2 and releases it on beat 4.

Beat Junction Exit owner Reply Refusal meter Physical result
0 01 C0 01 0 Waiting holds the pulse
1 0A C0 01 0 Left offer holds it
2 0A 81 01 1 Seam reads state 3 and holds state 3
3 0A 81 01 2+ A second refused attempt
4 08 C4 01 2+ Seam moves state 1 to 4. Left inbox now holds the pulse
5 08 D0 06 2+ Left end holds the pulse. The reply latch changes

Here the seam state number is S + 2C + 4T. Only one of waiting, the two offers, the two inboxes and the two ends is occupied at any snapshot. The blocker moves separately. A read of its position consumes none of it.

Separating an instrument from a controller

The small refusal meter has its own three state sites. Once per measured beat it reads the before/after pair from each connected seam. An eight-byte mask 00 02 00 08 00 20 00 80 admits the four diagonal transitions that held a present source. A presence read combines the results. Two guarded passages advance the meter from zero to one and then to two or more. There is no outgoing wire from the meter into the material owners. With a single input pulse, at most one connected seam carries an offer per beat.

The exact counts beneath the diagram are calculated afterward from detached frame copies. Offers count resolved seam attempts with a present source. Refusals count those that left it held. Arrivals count a newly occupied end. Multiple attempts on one pulse can therefore produce several refusals and a single arrival. A disconnected offer can stay held without producing any seam event. A deferred, unreadable transaction supplies no event sample.

Removing the left report wire leaves its local guard at its initialized zero. Removing the left material seam leaves a stranded left offer and an instrument reading of zero. Removing the arrival reply leaves material at the end while the reply stays waiting. Independent replays with the meter unplugged and no intermediate frame capture produce identical junction, exit and reply bytes at every beat.

These results exercise a fixed, authored coordination rule. A later experiment could wire refusal evidence into a local change mechanism and measure its utility afterward against the same tapes. Here neither the meter nor an external interpretation of failures or fragments alters a route.
