Primitive Explorer

An interactive visual overview of the primitive experiments, from the original ODV word to connected flow, geometry constraints and repeated patterns.

dotnet run --project Visualizations/Gojo.PrimitiveExplorer

Or run Open-Explorer.ps1 in this directory. This is a local Avalonia desktop application, with native drawing and no XAML, web service or external assets. It requires the repository's .NET 10 development environment.

How to explore

Choose a layer on the left. The dark panel contains actual packed bytes, with hexadecimal byte labels and individual bits in low-bit-first storage order. The diagram is on the right. Hover either side to highlight its counterpart; click to pin a selection and scroll to its encoding. Escape clears it.

Use the controls above the panels to change observations, wiring premises or construction. Passage chapters step actual owner-local transitions. Repetition and composition chapters advance one external beat at a time, or play slowly. Reset returns to the selected scenario's initial state. Changing a scenario also resets its timeline. Each displayed timeline is reconstructed by replaying the bounded experiment from its selected initial state; it is not a general live simulator or persistence format.

The result underneath explains what changed and what can be concluded. The smaller text states the interpretation's limits. Encoding notes opens the corresponding experiment document in the repository.

What the pictures mean

The viewer references experiments/PrimitiveMeasure directly. It calls the same packing, prediction, settlement, shape readers and controller fixtures as the console experiments. There is no second arithmetic or flow implementation in the UI. Numeric row charts are repacked with EndpointRows and checked against the originating fixture's bytes where that fixture exposes them.

Screen positions, colors, labels and highlight mappings are presentation data. They are not new fields in the primitive representation. Shape dimensions and placement offsets on screen are illustrative; they cannot supply absent lengths, calibration, closure or equality. A phase cycle does not prove a turn angle without its displayed contour correspondence. The shape readers select explicit geometric contracts, rather than deriving physical geometry from ODV.

Keep three different bit interpretations separate:

  • Stored values: ODV words, or owned V/D/O fields in their positional context.
  • Read supports: bit w admits word w; several bits represent alternatives.
  • Joint states / transition laws: bits describe complete multi-site patterns or before/after pairs. They are not extra ODV quantities.

Grey final bits are padding. Rows have no internal padding. The owning table's arity, reference width and law supply the interpretation. Current port/binding maps use host references and integers; the viewer does not imply that these maps already have a compact serialized format.

Unknown control is a limited read of actual state; hiding it does not mutate the stored bit. Predictions are never committed as actual flow. The material passage executor requires a complete resolved observation; the later pulse controller can avoid reading an irrelevant guard. The two behaviors are shown without claiming they are the same executor.

Automatic material splitting, discovered radial fields, automatic hardening, calibrated ladders, garbage collection, shrinking and contact detection remain open or separately scoped work. They are explained at the relevant layers, not animated as if already implemented.

Layer 39 now implements one deliberately narrow shrinking construction: regular three-to-five-direction strips with a shared unit gap and a protected positive core. This does not supply arbitrary offset or contact discovery.

Checks and visual review

dotnet test Tests/PrimitiveExplorer.Tests/PrimitiveExplorer.Tests.csproj
dotnet test Gojo.slnx
dotnet run --project Visualizations/Gojo.PrimitiveExplorer -- --capture=C:/Temp/gojo-primitive-explorer

The focused checks exercise every exposed choice combination at several step counts, enforce bit/padding bounds, compare uncertain conditional storage with its joint predictions, test missing shape evidence, verify shared-reference highlight spans, and check repetition/composition correspondence.

Capture mode renders all 15 chapters from the actual Avalonia control tree, plus a linked selection, then exits. It is a visual review aid; it does not replace testing pointer and keyboard interaction in the live window. Capture mode also exercises the native direction button, uncertainty selector, step button and diagram hit detection. Representative screenshots are retained in previews/; the complete generated capture set is not required at runtime.
