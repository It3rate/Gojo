Syntax exercised against graphic structure

Status: representation review and executable fixtures, September 2026.

Follow-up: the selected affine construction fixture now implements exact interpolation, derivative-displacement checks and exposed motif correspondence. The review below records the original pass and the gaps it identified. The linked follow-up is authoritative for the current status of those three contracts; the Bézier and frieze fixtures have been extended.

The syntax can express the selected graphic structures with one small authoring addition: group_on(holder, ...). The more significant gaps concern how much a selected reader may conclude from a compact shape. Transporting a graph does not establish geometric intent, continuity, construction validity or an infinite pattern's symmetry.

This pass authors structures, applies explicit placeholder bindings and checks existing local reads. It adds no curve sampler, frieze expander, rendering backend, compiler or machine-learning operations.

One authoring addition, no graphic opcodes

group, ports and frame allocate a fresh holder. To retain an explicitly authored shared holder, the syntax now also provides:

let $space = carrier();
let $x = carrier();
let $y = carrier(unit_of($x));
let $p = group_on($space, at($x, 2), at($y, 4));
let $q = group_on($space, at($x, 8), at($y, 10));
root $p -> $q;

This expands directly to Relation.OrderedGroup(space, ...), with no geometry dependency in the parser. For two participants its primitive expansion is:

root ((point($space,0) -> at($x,2)) -> (point($space,0) -> at($y,4))) -> $space;

The raw form was already expressible. The addition improves readability and reduces accidental loss of holder identity. Empty and singleton groups remain ordinary groups. A missing or unbound holder is not silently replaced.

The current CAD reader compares ordered coordinate carrier identities. It can read points with different holders in the same coordinate basis. Sharing a holder does not make swapped x/y axes compatible. Compatible unit shapes do not make those axes identical. The fixture tests these distinctions in three coordinates as well as the two-coordinate curve examples.

Parameters are calibrated perspectives

The parameter fixture maps a 0..100 parameter onto a 10..210 metric span. Its coarse parameter 40 gives 90; its refined parameter 47 gives 104. A separate coarse read retains the known seven-tick remainder. The record connects the basis, domain, both parameters, both results, refinement evidence and calibration. The known remainder is not a statistical error bar.

TryReadNormalizedValue previously accepted any relation in its domain role and any two grounded points in its parameter roles. Seven regression cases reproduced false reads: an unrelated relation, zero/reversed/non-origin scale, mixed-axis endpoints and parameter points on unrelated carriers.

The reader now requires a concrete same-carrier span from zero to a positive scale, with both parameter points on that carrier face. Rejected records remain available as structure. This still does not prove that supplied results were correctly calculated. Values outside 0..scale are not newly forbidden: extrapolation is a separate question from the existence of a calibration.

A template instance also preserves its repeated center role:

let $template = ?center -> (?center -> ?rim);
let $bindings = frame(bind(?center, $p), bind(?rim, $q));
root group($template, $bindings, $p -> ($p -> $q), ?provenance);

This is the shared-frame circle recipe. Its center occurs twice by identity. A partial instance retains an open rim and unconstructed result. This uses the same template/binding approach as SketchViewer leaf and opening instances, without reproducing the house's processing code.

Direction, sites and native carriers

An ordinary ordered span and an opposed-start directed segment are distinct:

let $axis = carrier();
let $joint = tick(3);
root span($axis, -4, $joint);
root point(dual($axis), -4) -> point($axis, $joint);

Reversing endpoints, negating a location and opposing a carrier face remain different structures. A following segment reusing $joint follows its motion. A separately authored equal coordinate does not.

A circle carrier crystallizes from an orthogonal point; a line carrier from an orthogonal zero span. These structures retain origins without requiring global x/y coordinates. Crystallization captures the tick promise: moving the original anchor does not silently recenter the stabilized carrier. Live moving origins require an explicit connection.

Carrier-native arcs and shared-frame curves can coexist at different perspectives. This is a useful benefit, but not yet a general law for angles, curvature or arbitrary embeddings.

Bézier intent needs more than a convenient silhouette

The current concrete recipes are compact:

root ($start -> $end) -> $control;
root ($start -> $end) -> ($first_control -> $second_control);

The second shape also pairs two curve sections. The current reader chooses a transition when those spans meet and a cubic curve otherwise. It also declines a shared-frame segment with equal-position endpoints. Consequently closed cubics, repeated control spans and endpoint-coincident controls can lose the intended cubic read, although such Bézier controls are legitimate.

Reversing the outer relation exchanges chord and control span. It does not reverse traversal. A reversed cubic needs reversed control order, with the parameter correspondence t -> 1-t when comparing it to the original.

These are recorded limits of the existing recognition heuristic. This pass does not change the global reader's choice or introduce a Bezier tag to decide between otherwise identical graphs.

The new fixture instead connects three first interpolations, two second interpolations and a final interpolation, all sharing one parameter surface. This is the de Casteljau construction: each intermediate interpolates adjacent points of the preceding level. See its geometric derivation.

flowchart LR
    A[Start] --> AB[q0]
    B[Control A] --> AB
    B --> BC[q1]
    C[Control B] --> BC
    C --> CD[q2]
    D[End] --> CD
    AB --> ABC[r0]
    BC --> ABC
    BC --> BCD[r1]
    CD --> BCD
    ABC --> P[Curve point]
    BCD --> P

An edge is ordinary relation structure:

let $ab = ports(?start, ?control_a, $grade) -> ?q0;
let $bc = ports(?control_a, ?control_b, $grade) -> ?q1;
let $abc = ports(?q0, ?q1, $grade) -> ?r0;

These are existing TransformSurface.Interpolate requests in a selected interpolation context. That generic three-input shape alone is not a universal proof of affine interpolation. The fixture supplies the construction dependency pattern and a shared bounded parameter domain. A general numerical interpolation reader, spatial calibration validation, parameter-domain enforcement and residual propagation remain to be developed. Intermediate values and the final point remain open; parsing does not evaluate the curve.

Construction intent survives coincident controls, partial binding and reversed control order. Uncertainty can attach to a control or parameter without inventing missing coordinates. This is inspectable construction evidence, not yet a canonical replacement for every CAD curve.

Actual Blocks bridge correction

The Bézier glossary builder used fixed example coordinates for all three source/target combinations. The line–line bridge ended at (26,-10) while the outgoing line started at (8,-10). Arc combinations needed different entry/exit sites too. Even equal coordinates were separately constructed.

The bridge now reuses the actual incoming end and outgoing start relations. Nine checks cover the three rows before and after one/two restores. Controls remain illustrative: endpoint connection does not establish tangency, curvature continuity or a computed best bridge. The export note now states that boundary.

Frieze patterns as motif plus generators

No active explicitly named frieze implementation was found in the searched sources. This exploratory fixture uses current repeat/transform and projection mechanisms, connecting an asymmetric triangular motif to candidate generators:

T(x,y) = (x+12, y)
G(x,y) = (x+6, -y)

The operations are actual affine frames:

let $translate_x = span($x,0,12) -> span($x,12,24);
let $glide_x = span($x,0,6) -> span($x,6,12);
let $reflect_y = span($y,0,1) -> span($y,0,-1);

Reflection reverses the target frame on the same carrier, rather than selecting an opposed flow face. Existing projection checks establish G(G(p)) = T(p) on 35 signed integer samples per axis at each transport depth. The general affine identity follows directly from the frames; this is not an exhaustive classification of frieze groups.

Each repeat retains motif, controller, transform, output-to-next-input feedback, residue and history. The controller connects a cell-index carrier to open support. A -2..2 observation window uses that same index carrier but is separate from the unresolved repetition domain. A bounded observation does not claim to define the whole infinite strip.

The pointwise extension of coordinate maps to every motif participant remains an exposed correspondence slot. Another example leaves x calibration unbound until an explicit binding arrives. A missing step is not zero translation.

A generator has a potential advantage over a list of repeated shapes: motif, period and operation have a common structural source. Learning that generator from observed copies is a separate inference. Changing a period must update or invalidate its dependent calibrations; sharing one period tick alone does not maintain an entire affine relation automatically.

Remaining representation work at this checkpoint

  1. Selected curve construction reads. Establish affine interpolation over exposed spatial bases, including degeneracies and partial controls. The six-edge fixture is a concrete starting point.
  2. Continuity constraints. Attach desired endpoint, tangent and curvature relationships to a bridge, separately from the fact that a curve exists.
  3. Identity versus geometric contact. The coordinate reader can find candidate joins from equal coordinates. That is not shared tick identity or a material/topological connection without further evidence.
  4. Repeating constructions. Define correspondence over transformed motifs, integer-domain repetition, inverse traversal and generator composition. A complete frieze classifier or expander is unnecessary for the next fixture.
  5. Authoring reuse. Version 1 lacks reusable text definitions, imports and a canonical text writer. Aliases and structural templates suffice here; repeating six interpolation clauses will become cumbersome at larger scales.

This pass does not establish coverage of every possible structure. It shows that the small vocabulary reaches useful geometric examples and reveals ambiguity and missing connections that scalar tests alone would not expose.
