Core2, Core4 and current Gojo: retained capabilities and gaps

Review date: September 10, 2026. Current Gojo baseline: c29fbf6, including the joined-offset path construction. Historical source checkouts inspected: Core4 at c91c7d6, and ResoEngine/Core2 at d14f058. These are source and test comparisons, not a claim that the archived binaries were rebuilt or that all historical tests were rerun. Local checkout additions around deployment are not treated as changes to the historical mathematical implementations. Historical source links assume Core4 and ResoEngine remain beside the Gojo checkout; they are reference material, not current project dependencies.

September 11 follow-up: the complex interpretation work now restores selected complex coefficient arithmetic through opposed-face and contour constructions, exact square-root alternatives and partial fractional- power equations. The gap descriptions below record the September 10 baseline; general complex root families, analytic branch tracking and split-complex arithmetic remain outside the new implementation.

Main assessment

Gojo retains much of the early primitive intent, but it is not a feature-complete replacement for every old demo. Three kinds of change are easy to confuse:

  1. Re-expressed: the capability exists, but a different connected structure now carries it. Points, directed segments, units, projection and partial constructions mostly belong here.
  2. Implemented under a narrower or older contract: useful behavior exists, but does not yet meet every law of the new construction work. Several GojoCAD area and rounding helpers belong here.
  3. Historical capability without a complete current equivalent: the older implementation still exists, but its behavior has not been rebuilt in Gojo. Trolley navigation, the letter-formation system, complex fractional-power branch selection and resistive-network relaxation are significant examples.

The narrower syntax demonstrations should not be mistaken for the entire Gojo library. Conversely, a name or shape in Blocks does not establish that its construction or dynamics have been implemented.

Which older demos are we comparing?

The archived website contains three engines: Core4 Interactive Probe, Core2 Pattern Visualizer, and the separate Foom Numbers tour. Foom is earlier related work, not another name for Core2.

The Core2 desktop page registration (external repository reference) lists 20 pages: pinning and shared carriers, resolution, symbolic operations, boundary continuation, friezes, booleans, fractional powers, units and letter formation. Its browser registration (external repository reference) includes only six: Letter A Goal Pins, Letter Formation Dynamics, Resolution Modes, Boolean Operations, Orthogonal Boolean and Parallel Boolean. Absence from that browser menu is therefore not evidence that a desktop experiment was deleted.

Core4 has a separate primitive example collection (external repository reference), site examples (external repository reference), and runtime experiments in topology, calibration and circuits. The current Gojo solution does not reference those projects. The archive preserves access to them; it does not make them current Gojo behavior.

Changes that affect interpretation, not just naming

A directed pair does not imply the old complex algebra

Core2's Axis (external repository reference) defaults to a complex two-slot algebra and can select a split-complex table. Its algebra table (external repository reference) specifies where each cross-product goes. Its pin interpreter (external repository reference) also uses the sign of a proportion's support to select carrier rank and orthogonal breakout.

Gojo has explicitly opposed carrier-face products and selected folds, tested as such. These should not be silently interpreted as the same complex or split-complex algebra. A negative scalar location, the dual of a carrier, a reversed relation and an inverse unit have distinct meanings. Reintroducing complex arithmetic would require its own connected basis and product law, with ordinary complex conformance tests. The shapes alone do not supply it.

The early intuition that a direction has an opposite survives. The historical rule coupling denominator signs, dimensional lift and imaginary participation does not survive as a universal kernel law.

The former unresolved ratio ladder has been replaced

Core2 Proportion.Fold (external repository reference) maps 0/0 to its scalar zero fallback and nonzero-over-zero to a positive or negative overflow sentinel. The original proportion still exists, but that folded read can hide its unresolved character.

Current Gojo retains missing operands as slots, unavailable quotient results, residue, blocked lanes or unsettled tension according to the actual cause. Our measured offset can keep 65*s*s = 4 with a nonnegative scale domain without inventing a zero or a floating-point exact answer. This is a substantive improvement in how intermediate constructions remain usable.

Approximation is also no longer assumed to carry an intrinsic hard tolerance. Nominal landmarks, allowed variation, conditional bounds, selected resolution and learned expectations remain different structures. The uncertainty reference describes that separation.

A visible Boolean result may have had a narrower law even then

Core2 Area.Union (external repository reference) calls Envelope, whose own comment says it is an axis-wise convex envelope, not a union that preserves separate regions. Two disjoint rectangles can consequently acquire filled space between them under that particular helper. This does not say all Core2 Boolean implementations behaved that way; its separate axis projection and gallery mechanisms need their own reads.

Gojo's scoped span operations explicitly retain covered, overlapping, exclusive and uncovered participation. Its CAD area implementation can preserve separate loops and openings, but has a different numerical boundary discussed below. Similar pictures should be compared by their selected laws, not by matching the word “union.”

Important behavior that exists in Gojo outside the new syntax fixtures

The recent statement that “caps, rounded joins and regions remain future work” is true for the new exact measured-offset/path contract. It is too broad as a statement about the whole repository.

Current GraphicBoundaryConceptSurface already finds cusp candidates, rounds selected line/line and line/arc corners, and builds circular caps at selected line tips. The corresponding tests exercise them.

Current GraphicAreaSettlementSurface splits segment/arc boundaries, resolves positive and anti-area loops, preserves holes, and assembles outlines. Its tests include separated composites, rings, crescents, overlapping boundaries and rounded H-like forms. These are executable behaviors, not only labeled pictures.

However, this code also uses double, trigonometry and fixed constants such as Epsilon = 0.01, SampleDistance = 0.55 and EndpointSettlementDistance = 1.5. The line/line rounding helper limits trim distance to 45% of the shorter incident length, computes an effective radius and rounds output coordinates. These are useful implementation choices, but they do not amount to an exposed, scale-aware uncertainty or admissibility law. The presence of structural result and participation witnesses does not by itself make every internal numerical choice an exact construction.

This is an integration and conformance gap within current Gojo, not a lost Core2 feature. The useful next move is to reconnect those existing cases to explicit metric, support, resolution, correspondence and rejected-candidate structure. Reimplementing them as if no prior code existed would waste work.

Similarly, GraphicStructure can retain transition/clothoid and arc/unwound-length involute recipes. That alone is not evidence of a general clothoid or involute solver. The construction audit distinguishes supplied geometry, recipes and calculated results.

Earlier optimized backends Separate Core4-era kernel/SIMD and demo projects They are not current solution targets or evidence that the new syntax already compiles into those backends.

The letter stepper (external repository reference) is real application behavior. It also contains explicit proposal types, numeric response constants and random jitter. It is evidence of a useful experiment, not evidence that general letter recognition or adaptive local intelligence had already emerged from the old primitive kernel.

The trolley tests (external repository reference) and topology tests (external repository reference) are particularly valuable recovery material: they specify concrete situations, not just aspirations. We should preserve the problems and useful expected behavior while rebuilding the representation.

The resistive-circuit tests (external repository reference) include a 12-to-0 potential network with unequal branches whose middle potentials settle to 9 and 6. That is a concrete old capability. Current resistor value structures and ECAD documentary tests describe nominal values, tolerances, terminals and nets; they are not a replacement for the relaxation procedure. Two-space calibration is also a different law, despite the similar visual motif of neighbours balancing.

Core2's fractional-power tests (external repository reference) are another clear loss of application coverage: they test sqrt(-1) candidates i and -i, plus selection near a reference branch. They also explicitly defer negative fractional exponents, so the old implementation was not complete either. Current integer power tests and constrained geometric square roots do not cover those branch semantics.

For signals, the existing plan already records the Core4 code and its caveats. For example, its LastSlope is a sample difference rather than a rate divided by elapsed time. Current forecast construction explicitly separates source time, arrival, issuance and later assessment. That is better experimental discipline, while still covering fewer signal-model families.

What I would recover next

The strongest direction is a capability ledger attached to concrete fixtures, not a promise that every old class will return. For each family retain its old example, current structural equivalent, known false reads and remaining law. This review is the first version of that ledger.

For the current construction work, I would prioritize:

  1. Bring the existing CAD corner and region helpers into the audit. Start with a rounded corner whose requested radius cannot fit, and two boundaries that are close but not the same site. Expose the adjustment or refusal, rather than leaving it inside fixed thresholds.
  2. Recover a small topology sequence from the old tests: shared T junction, terminal reflection, then a loop/merge. Distinguish geometric contact, permissible traversal and conserved transfer in each fixture.
  3. Keep the larger deferred capabilities explicit: inverse/root alternatives, letter-like goal formation, a passive resistor network and signal-family discovery. Each needs its own selected law and comparison experiment before claiming migration parity.

There is no need to abandon the recent primitive work. Its stronger treatment of identity, construction evidence, mathematical conformance and partial results gives us a better basis for recovering those capabilities. But it is accurate to say that the older demos explored a broader application surface than current Gojo has fully re-established.
