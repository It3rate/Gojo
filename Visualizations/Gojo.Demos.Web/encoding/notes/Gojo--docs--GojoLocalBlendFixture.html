Local blend syntax fixture

Status: executable experiment, September 5, 2026. It uses the existing version-1 syntax and kernel; it does not establish a general learning or blame-allocation law. The companion review explains the design questions that led here.

This document records the initial experiment and its scoped capture. The later syntax and dynamics checkpoint summarizes completed numeric transport, uncertainty and replay work, and the adaptive mechanisms still to be tested.

Source: local-blend.gojo. Execution: GojoSyntaxLocalBlendTests.

What this experiment establishes

A partially constructed input and an open filter can be authored as ordinary relations, then refined through binding. Two exposed inputs contribute through different calibrated frames. Another mixture remains available even when one mixture is being changed. A requested output gives the experiment a focus; local calculation exposes mismatch without identifying a guilty participant.

The test driver explicitly introduces a response into a gain carrier through Add or Remove, which use the existing signed-tension settlement. A new endpoint observation then binds the partial filter into a new concrete model. The previous model, captured input, prediction, claim, and response remain inspectable through refinement reads and a timeline.

The feedback recipe keeps three stages connected: the introduced delta enters the gain carrier; its settled value is observed through the endpoint calibration; the resulting endpoint supplies the next filter binding. A delta is not itself the next endpoint. The repeat feedback bundle records these stages; the test driver executes them explicitly rather than treating this bundle as a simple slot-to-slot feedback frame.

Parsing constructs the recipe. The test driver supplies the observation and perturbation schedule. No parser action executes feedback, chooses a mixture, or learns a response rule implicitly.

The authored structure

The fixture has six public roots. This positional layout belongs to this experiment; it is not a universal component schema.

Root Contents
0 Access surface: ambient light, heat, and sound; only light and heat exposed
1 Two-component input pattern, partial and complete binding frames, second-input expectation
2 Partial mixture, shared open endpoint, initial endpoint binding, independent alternative mixture
3 Goal, expected signal, error basis, gain carrier and initial point, endpoint calibration, two response routes
4 Focus inside a repeat recipe, open controller, adjustment action, feedback, initial timeline
5 Four-fine-ticks-to-one-coarse-tick read

Names such as light describe the experiment to its author. Source carriers, references, exposed ports, and calibrated relations carry the local meaning. The mixture need not represent a campfire, kettle, or any other interpretation that another space might build from its output.

The input pattern is simply:

let $input_pattern = ?first -> ?second;
let $partial = frame(bind(?first, $light_read));
let $expectation = ?second -> span($heat, 0, 20);

The expectation is visible structure. The fixture does not claim that this relation automatically enforces an interval constraint. Applying the partial frame preserves the second placeholder. It cannot be read as a complete two-input equation. Binding that role to a concrete zero, however, produces a different, readable structure: zero participation is not missing participation.

A partial filter does not need an invalid point(C, ?integer) constructor:

let $heat_filter =
    span($heat, 0, 10) -> (point($signal, 0) -> ?heat_endpoint);

The missing endpoint is a whole point participant. Binding it to a grounded point completes the target frame. Its slot identity connects the model, experiment focus, and eventual refinement binding.

The additive topology is:

let $mixture = ($light_filter -> $heat_filter) -> point($signal, 1);

This is the existing operation structure used by the equation helpers. No stored blend or sum opcode is added. Exposing it in expanded notation makes the exact relation order available for inspection before adding shorthand.

Numeric trace and uncertainty

The selected sample is light 6 and heat 10. Mixture A reads half the light plus g/10 of the heat. Initially g = 5, so its two contributions are 3 and 5. Mixture B reads all the light plus four tenths of the heat, producing 10. The external requested signal is also 10.

Observation or action Gain g A's signal Requested minus A Response evidence
Initial observation 5 8 2 Both input contributions remain connected
Half-response perturbation, then new observation 6 9 1 One positive gain tick introduced
Another half-response attempt 6 9 1 Zero whole gain ticks; one error tick remains as route residue
Explicit change to the full-response route 7 10 0 One positive gain tick introduced

The stalled step is a valid outcome at this resolution. Its remainder is retained; the test driver explicitly tries the separately authored full-response route. There is no hidden minimum step, automatic resolution increase, or claim that this is always the right response.

Mixture B remains intact throughout. Both models eventually produce 10 for this sample. For light 8 and heat 10, A at gain 7 instead produces 11 and B produces 12. Equal results on one sample do not imply model equality or a reason to collapse identity.

The scalar formula describes the exact cases in this trace. At fractional participation the kernel's selected integer read retains residue: with light 7, half-light reads as 3 signal ticks with one light tick unrepresented. The observation retains that projection residue separately from its claim mismatch of 2 signal ticks. Claim mismatch is conditional on that coarse prediction; it is not an exact discrepancy computed from imaginary precision.

The four-tick coarse output read similarly sees both 8 and 10 as 2 coarse ticks, retaining 0 and 2 fine ticks respectively. The coarse view cannot tell those finer results apart without its remainder. It remains a valid coarse read when finer information is retained elsewhere.

Why the response is an experiment

The goal records why this model is being explored. The focus exposes the heat endpoint as the participant to perturb, carries the light filter and alternative model as complement, and connects the proposed next endpoint by correspondence. This is a chosen local experiment, not evidence that the heat relation caused the mismatch.

In the selected sample, changing the gain by one tick changes the predicted signal by one tick. The response calibration is therefore easy to inspect. Changing the heat input changes that sensitivity. A general system must read and refine such response assumptions; it cannot reuse this ratio as a universal gradient or settlement law.

The error carrier is the basis of the claim read. Computing a mismatch does not put that quantity into its live value or tension. The response route reads the mismatch and justifies a new actuator perturbation. This is not material transfer of error or drainage of sensory participation. The sources and signal carrier are not mutated by observation, and observing the same input through two alternatives does not duplicate conserved material.

A negative-feedback case lowers the requested signal to 6. The same selected route introduces a negative gain delta through Remove, leaving the request and source reads unchanged. This tests signed response mechanics without asserting that the request deserves to be held fixed in all situations.

The existing OperationFollow also supplies useful response mechanics, but its follower is the current claim participant. Here the feedback receiver is a separate gain carrier, so the fixture uses an explicit error-to-gain port and ordinary tension input. This keeps that extra relationship in the graph.

Evidence and identity boundary

The execution constructs a RefinementRead for each observation, containing the endpoint binding and a ReadWitness. That witness retains the calculated prediction, raw operation read, projection residue, claim residual, requested goal, gain observation, and source-to-captured-input correspondence. The feedback witness retains before, introduced delta, after, remaining carrier tension, route residue, and the observation that motivated it.

Source input aliases retain their carrier and TickRef identity. A CarrierLocation is a value type; boxed location reference equality is not the identity law. The captured observations deliberately copy current tick values into separate TickRefs. Moving the live light reference later leaves the old input and prediction unchanged, and a local claim through the retained correspondence exposes that the old input no longer matches the current one.

This is a scoped capture for two distinct inputs, the expected value, and the gain-driven refinement. It is not an arbitrary graph snapshot or a general invalidation service. Shared topology in a more general capture needs a memoized correspondence policy. Retargeting other shared calibration sites is outside this fixture's historical stability guarantee.

Subsequent numeric graph transport now preserves active attachment ownership, shared unknowns and their connected expectations. The inventory tests round-trip this fixture's authored graph. That coverage is distinct from automatically capturing every later execution state; live references that already drifted cannot recreate an earlier observation. External reference revival, event deduplication and identity across independent packets still need explicit connection contracts. Legacy diagnostic snapshots remain unsuitable for restoration. Deliberate lossy memory also needs its own retained-information contract.

What should follow

The mathematical conformance checkpoint now corrects general-ratio accounting and range bugs and exercises more kernel and authored arithmetic. Later fixtures add explicit unit quotient composition, material refinement/borrow and conditional bounded uncertainty. See the current checkpoint for their limits and the recommended sequence into local adaptation. The blend alone is not evidence of a complete structural arithmetic surface.

Beyond the initial calibration experiment, connection work should expose participation and utility, along with the local observations and response routes that alter it. A useful experiment would let an outward signal receive feedback from two neighboring spaces, with different support histories and incompatible demands. Retain competing response routes and measure what each locally changes before adding any automatic strengthening or pruning.

This still leaves substantive questions: how support changes responsiveness; which local constraints admit a new connection; how correlated observations avoid being counted as independent support; what keeps a mutually reinforcing but unhelpful loop open to correction; and what a compressed memory promises to retain. A scalar confidence field, a fixed decay timer, or a global winner selector would choose answers before those laws exist.

Blocks remains an experimental inventory for additional cases. Its current motifs can reveal gaps and overspecification as readily as they reveal common primitives. The fixture therefore uses ordinary component structure rather than promoting a particular Blocks shape into a canonical value taxonomy.
