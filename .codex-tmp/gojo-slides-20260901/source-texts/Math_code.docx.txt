[normal] Operators can solve equations (x>3 = -3i+5, the part greater than 3), or evaluate (x>3=true). But they can also ‘cause’ it to be true. [normal] An equation has two fundamental parts, a start state, and a target condition (a point where it becomes true). This condition can be a result value, but also something like “result > 5”, which is why the result is a condition and not a specific value. The transition between them can be characterized by a transform value required to satisfy the condition (a stretch, as in, a multiplication). Though it is not required, we are usually interested in the minimum energy transform to make a condition true. The fact that we are making a condition true implies a test happens, and this test can be a part of the equation, and even the point of it.
[normal] A multiplication supplies the initial state and the transform, the result is calculated meaning the condition is always true (assuming the answer is correct). A division supplies the result and the transform, and you figure out the initial state. When a transform isn’t required (the initial state is already true), this is the identity transform.
[normal] This assumes the transform is one directional. Normally 4*6 makes the 4 six times larger, and the 6 is ‘used up’. 6*4 is the opposite. But if they are evenly correlated (0.5 : 0.5) then half of each is used up transforming the other, so you end up with two squares, 4*3 and 2*6. The result is still 24, but the geometry is different. 4*6^2 just continues the transform rate, like 6*4^2 does. (6 :0.5*0.5: 4)^2 continues creating the two squares at that rate. 6*4^0 is not scaling 6 at the rate of 4x, but it’s still comparable to 6*4^1. This detail matters more with comparisons and bool ops, as they are usually used as comparisons to ^0 resulting in true or false.
