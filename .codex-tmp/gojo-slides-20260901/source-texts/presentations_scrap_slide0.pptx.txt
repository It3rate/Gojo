What is a Quantity
All quantities derive from a measure of change. This may not seem intuitive because it is hard to isolate a single measure from its context. It’s like a deep sea fish has no idea of air, and therefore has no idea of water. Imagine living in a void - it isn’t black, it is the absence of the concept of light. You can’t be ‘here’ because there is no concept of ‘there’. There can’t be quantity, because there is no change.
It's only once there is a second thing to compare to — something different from the first — that the concept of quantity begins to emerge. It was cold, now it's hot. This is bigger, that is smaller. Two samples may not be able to fully calibrate and manipulate quantities, but it is a giant phase change in perception. The change allows us to notice a difference, and therefore notice the concept at all. This change can be over perceptible things like time, place, or scale, but it can also be fully abstract or imagined things. The most abstract ideas in math may never touch the real world, but they are only able to exist as ideas by exploring unequal things.
If existence is the transition from 0 to 1, comprehension begins at 2.
