[normal] Positive/negative vs unit/unot: In terms of an amount of money +/- would be saving more and saving less (the amount of money is positively correlated to the amount of ‘verb’). Unit vs unot would be spending more and spending less (money amount is negatively correlated to amount of verb). Debt isn’t the negative of savings, it is the negative unit version of savings. Other negatively correlated examples: hole in a pocket, missing chair, telling a lie, running in the wrong direction, dry. The states of things range across missing, lacking, default, optimal, excessive. Unot words point left, thus the more they increase, the further from optimal and below default they go. Words that point to default like average, or away from default like ‘unusual’ express degrees of surprise. The unot words tend to point toward surprise, but the context of what is optimal relative to default determines this. Eg. An unremarkable artist is a unot adjective, because more unremarkable isn’t more optimal. Compare to ‘a normally shaped head’, ‘dizzy’, or even ‘typical upbringing’. Usually there isn’t a positive or negative connotation, but there tends to be a natural increase or decrease. So a bigger dog, a fatter dog, a longer dog, an older dog - all refer to properties that have a natural positive and negative direction (and are units) vs smaller, thinner, shorter, younger, which are unot words. Things like ‘blue’ don’t really have a unot without context, so are just units. Pure adjectives are just scalars on root properties, like more/less/extra(amount, addition), very/slightly(scale, multiplication), not/non (negation), un/counter(inverse, unot), re/multi(repeating) -- and then category specific scalars like bigger(size), worse(quality), pure(concentration), faster(speed)
