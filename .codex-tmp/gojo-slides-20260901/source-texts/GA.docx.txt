[normal] Is Math Universal?
[normal] It is often claimed that math is a ‘universal language’ — that it was discovered rather than invented. Given our limited interactions with alien species, that is a difficult claim to test, but it certainly feels like at least parts of it would be similar in any form of intelligence. Being able to categorize things requires grouping, grouping leads directly to counting and measuring, which leads to adding and multiplying — and then all mathematics tumbles out. Or is this summary already so rife with human bias that it wouldn’t even be describable to another intelligence?
[normal] The best we can probably do is to imagine communicating it to another very very different life form. Maybe a smart octopus, a blank computer AI, or a giant smarty pants gas cloud that had no concept of time, space or pants. In this context base ten numbers and even Cartesian coordinates don’t seem so fundamental, but what about counting, adding, comparing and multiplying?
[normal] Ok, let’s imagine then that there’s an alien AI supercomputer gas cloud floating somewhere in deep space, and by some distant friend of a friend we can communicate with it. You say, “Hi, I’m a human and I come in peace”. And it says, “What’s a human, and what’s an I, and what’s a peace?”.  A few exchanges like this get nowhere, and then you remember the universal language that is mathematics. The impulse is to start with geometry, because other than having ‘geo’ in the name, it seems pretty universal (which has ‘universe’ in the name). But before triangles and all that you’ll need to explain positions, which implies relationships between things, which implies things. So we are going to need a thing.

[normal] An interesting paradox with counting and measuring is that continuous things can not be partitioned, but can be adjusted to a specific magnitude, while discrete things can be partitioned, but are not bound to specific magnitudes. As an example, you can not draw half a line, but you can adjust a line to be as close to three inches long as your precision allows. On the flip side, you can have 68.2% of an apple, but apples can not be defined by a specific weight, diameter or color. The implication of this is the only way we can get an exact number (say exactly three) is with discrete things, but with these we lose the ability to have properties with consistent values. We can still use a single discrete thing as an exact unit of measure and get a continuous value, but then all comparisons to the unit will be measured, and limited by our precision (this apple is 1.153 times heavier than our unit apple, to three decimal places, not four). The only numbers that can be ‘infinitely’ precise are the ones that can contain no information about the values of their attributes. The declared membership in a category (including the unit category)  is the ‘true’ axiom, and is the only value with no error in its magnitude.
[normal] This means only discrete things can be added to get counting (because one line plus one line is still one line, albeit with a new length), and only continuous things can be multiplied (you can stretch or scale a continuous thing and get a new measure, but you can’t partition or append to a discrete thing without getting fragments or additional things).
[normal] An adding number line starts at zero, and pulls in one direction or the other. It doesn’t stretch anything, but everything it passes gets removed from existing field and added to the one being created. Eg white paper is removed and a black line is added. This is similar to the reciprocal in multiplication where you connect each end of the lines to each other, but in this case you start at zero, one side is positive value, the other negative. When adding to an existing line you can start at zero and ‘push’ to the positive, moving everything along (or start at max, and push to negative first). Now if that line is circular like in mod arithmetic for example, that becomes a rotation. If it isn’t circular, it is still sort of a rotation, just it doesn’t run out of ‘chairs’ so never circles back to the start. Contrast that with the stretching in multiplication, first one way then the other - if you would do that to a line it would fold up to a single point, and then unfold in reverse order. That is flipping. So multiplication is like flipping,and adding is like rotation. We can rotate by 90 degrees with multiplication using two perpendicular lines, but that is a flip that unwraps in the same direction (which is why it only works for the angles of the two lines, and you need cos/sin to actually rotate).
[normal] While adding in a mod system is like rotating (pushing the numbers around a clock a bit each time you add one), multiplying is going to potentially create gaps and overlaps (imagine all the numbers on the clock scale by 4, one becomes 4, 2 becomes 8, 3 becomes 12, 4 becomes 16=>4, 5 becomes 20=>8 etc). Some numbers will perfectly fill (1 always does because there is no change), and some will land all digits on the same number (*size always will). This should show certain patterns with primes, factors, symmetries and groups, and maybe even information, probability etc. Also if you keep track of the number of times something goes around as a place value system, you may get place value, exponents, multiple dimensions etc.
[normal] You use addition to create lengths (carve something out, negative, and push it out, positive), and then multiply using two of those objects, one as unit. With a single object, you can multiply (stretch), but it will be a flip end to end. You need to start at an end for the stretch because there is no unit (or the object is the unit if you prefer), and the end is zero rather than one. When stretching a single unit, the only landmark is the other point, so the points can either exchange (flip) or revert to how they were (identity).
[normal] Using category whole numbers, accuracy is as detailed as you want because fractions are valid. So PI is whatever large number over whatever other large number to get you 3.14159865… as precise as you want — but limited by how large your max number is. In this sense whole numbers are the ‘reciprocal’ of measured fractions, as they are also limited in precision based on how good your resolution (measurement) is. One is more samples, the other is a better ruler, but it comes to the same. Neither are groups unless you account for these limitations in closure (technically the answer is always in the group, just the correctness is limited, maybe similar to ‘mod’ in that sense).
[normal] Anything plural is continuous, anything singular is discrete? [normal] If two lines have no attach points they are unrelated and can only be compared. With one attach point, when one is extended to the other it stretches without affecting the other, so the unit doesn’t change. This is addition. With two attach points, any stretching of one will stretch the other, and relative to the original units this is multiplication. With three attach points, stretching from both ends makes the center attach point the origin with the positive and negative units potentially different sizes. With three lines connected, A->B and B-C this becomes a power of two scaling, which is also a curve when viewed with two axes. With two separated parallel lines attached with different unit sizes (a vertical connection and a second angled connection) this also will be a scaled power of two connections, but will result in a curve (one side longer but connect points and distance the same will pull one or both into a curve).
[normal] ** Stretching the unit causes all things measured by the unit to have more or less units per former unit, meaning multiply or divide. If non unit elements are scaled, they won’t affect the unit at all, so they just have room for more or less units depending on growth direction. This is just addition or subtraction.
[normal] Two options in a unit relation are 1) measuring from ‘unit’ or 2) sizing from ‘unit’. This leads to either addition or multiplication as the unit changes size.
