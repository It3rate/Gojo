[normal] Multiplying AI Segments
[normal] Here is an intuition for how multiplication works. Imagine I have a dirty window in my garage, and my squeegee is the same width as the window. As I drag it down, I‘m gradually combining the width along the entire height. I’m merging the width into the height.
[normal] A segment then can be thought of as the path that’s taken on a partially cleaned pane. If I’m 40% of the way down, then that’s the start point (let’s call it 4), and the bottom of the window is the end point (let’s say 10). There is the area that is already clean, and it defines the start area. The remaining area is the goal area. So we start at 4 and end at 10, and the length of this segment is 6.
[normal] This suggests the start point has the opposite polarity of the end point, positive up instead of positive down. So the start point is -4, the end point is 10, and the length is 6. That makes more sense.
[normal] Note that, if we set the start point 2 units above the window, it now starts at (positive) 2 and ends at 10, with a length of 12. The segment length is still computable by just adding the start to the end.
[normal] There are other ways to interpret these positions. In fact the previous two images both show a segment with three key points, a start point, a zero, and an endpoint. If the zero is in the middle, it calibrates the segment to the squeegee frame of reference, and if the zero is on the window edge, it's an absolute measurement in relation to a fixed point.
[normal] Units
[normal] Our regular unit goes from 0 to 1, meaning it starts at zero and ends at one. No matter how you multiply that, the start point will always remain at zero because zero times anything is zero.
[normal] The unit for the start point of a segment is the opposite -- the start point moves around and the end point is fixed. The polarity of the start point is in the opposite direction, so it reasons that the start unit is a segment that starts at the negative one position (1i), and ends at zero. No matter how you multiply it, its endpoint will remain at zero. These two units define a directed segment that can be offset from zero, and exist anywhere on the number line.
[normal] We can derive mathematical operations for these segments. In fact this pair of values maps exactly to complex numbers. Here are (3i+2) and (-2i+4). We've describe two units, but they both have an inverted version. Combinations of the four units allow for segments, as well as inverted numbers and mirroring. Our regular numbers are a subset of these, where the unit is '1', and the start point is always at zero.
[normal] Multiplication Part II
[normal] Ok, halfway there! We have merged the width into the height, now we need to merge the height into the width. Back to the window metaphor. While I’m cleaning my side of the window, my dear wife is outside cleaning the other side. Her squeegee matches the height of the window, so she wipes it left to right instead of top to bottom. She is merging the height into the width. Like me, she has a start point and end point, and everything works the same (other than it being horizontal instead of vertical). Together we are cleaning both sides of the pane, resulting in a clean window.
[normal] Multiplication is the full merging of two values, A into B, and B into A. If we always start from one edge and swipe all the way to the other, then everything can be encoded with our normal numbers. However if we want to deal with partial states or interpolation, we need to use segment math.
[normal] Multiplied segments create four main quadrants, vertical only, horizontal only, both and neither. These can travel any direction, and be interpreted in different ways. In the window metaphor, 'both' could mean both sides clean or both sides dirty. Note that with either interpretation, the direction of motion towards the goal (end points) is the same.
[normal] Important point: if we label the start point with ‘i’, then the multiplication intuitively maps to complex numbers. On a segment, a start point denotes the partial position of the swipe. The end point indicates the goal position of the swipe. The equation used in multiplying complex numbers is:
[normal] (XiY+YiX)i+(−XiYi+XY). In this analogy, the imaginary variable combines the two areas that have had half the work completed, and the real variable is the full goal area minus the fully completed area. The complex multiplication calculation makes more sense in 1D, but you can visualize what it is doing in 2D. Notice it behaves quite differently depending where the 0,0 point is placed.
[normal] This multiplication formula gets harder to interpret when dealing with negative values (where complex multiplication is used it often doesn't allow negative values, or uses circular encodings to sidestep the problems). Imagine one dimension has extra work (a positive i value) and the other skippable work (a negative i value). What does the i*i area mean? Some extra and some skippable, blended? A formula that accounts for these types of blends could interpret the areas as a proportional contribution from each dimension. When you think of it, that is essentially what the rectangle of 3X4 does; it shows an area where one axis contributed 3/7th and the other 4/7th of the total area 12. The ratios are explicit in the shape of the rectangle, where as the area of 12 is something that must be calculated.
[normal] We won't get too deep into the formulas, but you can see that getting these areas and ratios is a fairly simple calculation. The key is understanding that each quadrant uses different unit combinations. The two start points are (i*i), and this unit box is at the bottom left of the axis intersection. This unit indicates the positive directions for each axis. The endpoints would be the unit box we are used to, real*real, in the top right quadrant. And then the start-end and end-start combinations (i*r and r*i) are in the top left and bottom right quadrants.
[normal] Of course positive can point up or down (as in math vs computer graphics) and the horizontal positive can point east or west. The point is, no matter how we orient this, there are up to four quadrants, and they have specific meaningful interpretations.
[normal] You can merge these four areas back into a single segment like we do with complex multiplication, or deal with them in other ways that have real world meaning. But if you convert two segments into one, there will always be a loss. This is the same with any 1D multiplication. Take 4x3=12, if you convert the two numbers to 12, you will lose information about the original numbers. One dimensional multiplication will always have loss, because the units are the same, so the dimension modifies itself. It's like mixing two volumes of water together -- you can't get the original ratio back.
[normal] In two dimensions (which has two different units), we preserve all information when multiplying geometrically. Segment multiplication works the same, just there is more information to start with, and even more when two values are combined. With 3 segments in 3 dimensions it grows even more. Eventually you must choose what you care about, and preserve that. This can be partial preservation (discarding information), or at higher levels, statistical approximation (discarding resolution). Ideally with machine learning, a model can discover which parts of a multiplication it needs to pay attention to given different contexts.
[normal] Final Notes
[normal] When two different units are combined they form a 2D grid. Calculations will change in segment multiplication depending how you align the grid. You can have the 'zero' point fixed, or have it follow a point of interest. The math will give very different results, and afford different types of information. This is a feature not a bug. This calibration is akin to grid cells vs place cells, observing vs moving, or measuring vs comparing.
[normal] Imagine a mouse moving around a square, there are two possible calibrations, a fixed zero point, or a zero point that follows the mouse. In the squeegee analogy, this is the zero point being fixed somewhere on the window pane, vs the zero point being on the intersection point of the two swiping squeegees. There is a ton of useful computation available with this simple context switch.
[normal] Lastly, you may have noticed an issue with the squeegee metaphor. When multiplying 3*4, we connect 3 to 4, and 4 to 3. But 4*3=12, and 3*4=12. That suggests the result should be 24 (we cleaned an area of 12 on one side for the window, and an area of 12 on the other.
[normal] This is because rectangles are not the correct shape to represent the combination of two dimensions! We find rectangles extremely intuitive because we evolved in a gravity field for 3 billion years, but in reality a triangle is the most natural shape. If you see three stars, the impulse is to create a triangle, not a parallelogram.
[normal] I still use rectangles because they work well for discovering the concepts, but the real shape of multiplication is as below. The tips of the arrows are connected to each other, as are the start points. The 'red only' and 'blue only' connections only happen once (or more exactly, 100% of one area, and 0% of the other).
[normal] So 4 x 3 now looks like (½ * 3 * 4) + (½ * 4 * 3). As you use this triangular system, it starts to feel very natural, and it can help with reasoning about the meaning of each connection. The main thing is to remember if both sides are 'cleaned' or both are 'dirty', that is two areas, which is the full rectangular area. Likewise, when you have x-only or y-only, that's half the rectangular area.
[normal] Multiplying is combining numbers with each other, not just one into the other.
