**SCREEN 1: THE CARRIER AND TENSION**

**ANIMATION:** 
A pitch-black void. A single, glowing node appears. A sleek, monospace label hovers beside it: `Carrier`. The node begins to fill with a cool blue light (Accounted Value). Suddenly, a jagged, red/orange energy pulses around its perimeter. The label updates to show `Tension`.

**NARRATOR:**
Welcome to Gojo. In traditional programming, math errors or unaccounted changes are hidden exceptions or failure states. In Gojo, they are physical. Meet the Carrier: a physical value site with a unique identity. It stores positive accounted value, but alongside it, it holds signed, unresolved Tension. Tension isn't an error—it’s visible, unresolved physical pressure that actively drives the system to settle or route information elsewhere.

***

**SCREEN 2: LOCATIONS, POINTS, AND THE TICKREF**

**ANIMATION:** 
Zooming in on the edge of the `Carrier`, small hash marks appear. A second `Carrier` fades into view next to it. A bright line snaps between a specific hash mark on Carrier A and a hash mark on Carrier B. They lock together, highlighting as `TickRef`. 

**NARRATOR:**
To locate information on a carrier, we use a `CarrierLocation`—a signed tick site. But values aren't isolated. When multiple locations across different faces need to lock together, they share a `TickRef`. They now share the exact same physical coordinate and move as one. Grounding these sites gives us Points, which act as origins, landmarks, or boundaries in the system.

***

**SCREEN 3: SEGMENTS AND NATIVE PROJECTION**

**ANIMATION:** 
Two Points on a single Carrier are connected by a solid, glowing arrow, labeled `Segment`. A second Carrier appears below it. The Segment projects downward, casting a scaling grid onto the second Carrier. Values flow across the grid, automatically adapting to the new scale.

**NARRATOR:**
Connecting two points creates a Segment—a directed, bounded interval. Segments are crucial because they serve as projection frames. They allow values to map, scale, and stretch across different bases seamlessly, completely bypassing the need for symbolic math operators.

***

**SCREEN 4: INDEPENDENT AXES OF BOUNDARY AND FLOW**

**ANIMATION:** 
The Segment expands into a 3D interface. It splits visually into two distinct layers: "Boundary Axis" and "Flow Axis." The Boundary layer highlights the endpoints as rigid stops. The Flow layer shows animated arrows moving forward, then reversing into a mirrored "Inverse" face.

**NARRATOR:**
Orientation in Gojo isn't a simple plus or minus sign. It’s split into two independent axes. The Boundary side dictates the physical domain, such as a Positive-only span, a Negative-only span, or a Bounded Whole. The Flow face dictates calibrated difference and traversal direction, which can be Forward, or a physically opposed Inverse flow. Together, they define legal Lanes that dictate exactly how tension and values are allowed to move and settle.

***

**SCREEN 5: ORTHOGONALS AND POTENTIAL SHAPE**

**ANIMATION:** 
Red Tension flows rapidly down a 1D line but hits a hard clamp boundary. Unable to move forward or backward, the red energy bursts outward at exactly 90 degrees. It forms a faint, pulsing circular ring around the point point, labeled `Orthogonal Potential`.

**NARRATOR:**
What happens when tension cannot settle along an existing linear gradient? It pushes outward into Orthogonal Potential. A simple point pushes out to form an uncalibrated circular or radial seed. A segment pushes out to form a 2D sheet or panel seed. The existing structure determines the new dimensional possibility, turning unrouted pressure into new geometry.

***

**SCREEN 6: TOPOLOGY BY CONNECTION**

**ANIMATION:** 
Several independent lines (extents) drift into view. Their endpoints snap together at a single, glowing shared point. A pulse of blue value pushes through one line, hits the shared point, and splits seamlessly down the others. The label reads `Merge / Branch`.

**NARRATOR:**
As these structures expand, they physically connect. Topologies like branches, merges, and T-junctions aren't symbolic tags or enums—they emerge organically from shared sites and local flow. A split is simply a relation where one incoming directed endpoint shares a site with multiple outgoing endpoints. Topology is actual connection.

***

**SCREEN 7: RESOLUTION LADDERS**

**ANIMATION:** 
A massive numeric block reading "396" shatters. The pieces route into three separate, connected carriers: a "Hundreds" carrier holds 3, a "Tens" carrier holds 9, and a "Ones" carrier holds 6. A "Read Lens" sweeps over them. The lens focuses only on the Tens and Hundreds, blurring out the Ones. The read output cleanly displays "39".

**NARRATOR:**
As systems grow complex, values aren't stored as massive, flat floating-point numbers. They are distributed across Resolution Ladders—interconnected carriers representing different resolutions. This allows the system to confidently perform low-resolution, coarse reads. Missing fine detail is not treated as a fake precision or zero; it remains inspectable as unresolved partial resolution or residue.

***

**SCREEN 8: UNIT SHAPE AND CRYSTALLIZATION**

**ANIMATION:** 
A complex, messy mesh of points, orthogonals, and projection relations spins rapidly. A flash of light fuses them together. The messy wireframe is hidden inside a sleek, solid geometric patch. A label stamps onto it: `UnitShape`.

**NARRATOR:**
When a complex assembly of relations and ports stabilizes, it can undergo Crystallization. This process hardens the assembly into a brand-new carrier with a stable Unit Shape. It’s a crystallized promise of what one tick means—like an area-like tick or a specific gear tooth. It encapsulates the internal computational complexity into a local interface, while preserving the mathematical provenance beneath it.

***

**SCREEN 9: STRUCTURAL AND CONCEPTUAL LADDERS**

**ANIMATION:** 
A sharp, rigid rectangle sits in the void. A new translucent layer appears directly above it—a shape with perfectly rounded corners. Thin projection lines drop down, linking the straight edges of the new shape back to the old rectangle. The original sharp corners glow red, labeled `Residue`.

**NARRATOR:**
At the highest multidimensional level, design evolves through refinement without replacement. Complex structures are built in "rungs" on Structural and Conceptual Ladders. If you refine a sharp rectangle into one with rounded corners, the new operation exists on a higher rung. The lower-resolution sharp rectangle isn't destroyed. It remains fully traversable, preserving the full transform history, patch witnesses, and unresolved tension across all conceptual layers. 

***

**SCREEN 10: OUTRO**

**ANIMATION:** 
The camera pulls out rapidly. The rounded rectangle reveals itself to be a component in a massive, sprawling mechanical CAD assembly made entirely of pulsing Carriers, Flow Lanes, and Resolution Ladders. 

**NARRATOR:**
This is Gojo. It is not a symbolic algebra engine trying to solve text equations. It is a system where math is physical, error is tension, and structure builds itself through settlement.
